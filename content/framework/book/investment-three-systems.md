## Introduction: investment as a testing ground for method

The preceding articles address [constraints and future options](./constraints-and-future-options.md), [what belongs in a model](./what-belongs-in-a-model.md), and [acting under uncertainty](./acting-under-uncertainty.md). Investment brings these questions together: information arrives continually, participants change the environment, judgements must meet actual capital allocation, and funding horizons and execution conditions affect outcomes.

Correctly anticipating operating change does not ensure an attractive purchase price. Correctly anticipating price direction does not ensure the holder can survive the intervening path. This article separates research into three interacting systems, recording judgements, evidence and failure conditions for each.

| System | Primary question | Common omission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company | Can future intrinsic value per share keep increasing? | Treating revenue or total profit growth directly as shareholder-value growth |
| Market | What changes expectations, desired holdings and transaction prices? | Treating one's valuation as a conclusion the market must accept |
| Investor | Can funding, exposure and execution conditions support the strategy along its path? | Ignoring forced exit during the path |

This is a working division, not independence. Financing prices affect corporate investment capacity, investors' orders affect markets, and corporate disclosures change expectations. Research the main mechanisms separately, then reconnect them.

## Chapter 1. Investment's three systems

Company research studies operating-state transitions: whether samples become mass-produced products, certifications become recurring orders, invested capital turns into cash, and how these changes ultimately accrue to each share.

Market research tracks what participants know, believe and already hold, and which information would prompt adjustment. Prices do not reveal a single unified “market belief”: different preferences, funding constraints and future assumptions can generate the same price.

Investor research concerns one's own balance sheet and execution arrangements. A judgement that materialises years later has a maturity mismatch if supported by short-term funding callable at any moment. Correct analysis and sustainable capital deployment both need evidence.

State the system to which the question belongs, its objective and horizon, and conditions in the other two systems that must hold simultaneously.

## Chapter 2. Financial statements: trace outputs back to mechanisms

Statements are important historical records that also contain accounting estimates, measurement judgements and future obligations. “Output logs” is a metaphor prompting inquiry into causes, not a claim that statements record only mechanical facts. The [SEC's financial-statement guide](https://www.sec.gov/about/reports-publications/investorpubsbegfinstmtguide) explains the distinct questions answered by balance sheets, income statements, cash-flow statements and statements of shareholders' equity.

For example, inventory increases fit at least two explanations: deliberate stocking against strong demand or passive accumulation as demand weakens. An inventory number alone cannot choose between them.

| Competing explanation | What to observe next | What would weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparing deliveries for reliable orders | Order quality, shipments, inventory age and collections | Cancellations, continued ageing and delayed collections |
| Products are not selling | Discounts, provisions for impairment and customer loss | Planned deliveries and improved cash recovery |

Falling operating cash flow also needs decomposition into receivables, inventory, payables, payment arrangements and seasonality. Fixed-asset purchases are normally investing cash flows, and should not be mixed with working-capital changes as the same operating-cash-flow cause.

The procedure is to observe outputs, construct competing mechanism explanations and seek evidence where they diverge.

## Chapter 3. Corporate state transitions: capabilities, mechanisms, environment and constraints

A useful unit of long-term research is a state transition rather than a static quality score.

| Object | What to specify |
| --- | --- |
| Capability | What the company can already achieve reliably and repeatedly, with supporting evidence |
| Mechanism hypothesis | How capabilities become customer value, cost advantage and cash flow |
| Environment | Demand, competition, supply, institutions and technology |
| Constraints | Funding, certification, yields, talent, capacity and time |

“The technology is good, so growth will follow” omits most intermediate steps. A more testable statement specifies yield, cost, certification and demand conditions for a move from trial production to scale delivery, and how delayed certification or unimproved unit economics would change the judgement.

Identifying a possible next state is only a start. Estimate transition probability, timing, capital requirements, residual value after failure and the extent to which the price already incorporates that expectation.

## Chapter 4. From company growth to per-share value

Revenue, total profit and assets can grow alongside low-return investment, continuing dilution and declining per-share value. Decompose totals into cash and claims ultimately available to shareholders.

A common diagnostic is the productivity of additional invested capital:

\[
\text{Incremental ROIC}\approx\frac{\Delta\text{NOPAT}}{\Delta\text{Invested Capital}}.
\]

NOPAT means net operating profit after tax. The difference ratio is not mechanical: investment-to-maturity lags, acquisitions, impairments, capitalised expenses and cycle position change its basis. A very small or negative denominator makes a single ratio especially difficult to interpret.

[Damodaran's account of the fundamental sources of growth](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/valquestions/growth.htm) links reinvestment and returns on capital and distinguishes total from per-share profit growth. The resulting questions are where the next unit of capital goes, at what marginal return, and for how long it can be deployed.

**Fictional example:** company A's profit rises from 100 to 120, while shares increase from 100 to 150. Company B keeps profit at 100 and shares at 100. Ignoring other differences, A's earnings per share fall from 1 to 0.8 while B remains at 1. This illustrates dilution only. Earnings per share are not a complete valuation; cash flow, risk, growth quality and purchase price still matter.

## Chapter 5. Capital allocation as a decision policy

Retaining cash means management continues allocating capital on shareholders' behalf. Internal expansion, acquisitions, repurchases, dividends, debt repayment and liquidity retention need comparison under the same opportunity costs.

For major allocations, record whether the project threatens continued operation, what can be recovered after failure, which repeatable capabilities it creates, what information waiting might obtain, and scenarios making commitment unaffordable.

Repurchases do not inherently create value: price, financing, forgone alternatives and post-execution financial flexibility all affect the result. Dividends likewise need tax, opportunity and debt conditions.

Different firms can follow different reasonable patterns: high-return internal reinvestment, returning cash from mature businesses, or allocating across businesses. What matters is the use and conditions of the next unit retained, not an advance label of corporate quality.

## Chapter 6. Corporate viability: external channels and internal capabilities

Low-cost financing, major customers, imported equipment, supply networks, subsidies and licences can enlarge the current feasible space. Record these separately from capabilities the company can reliably repeat internally.

Network dependence is normal. Diagnose critical-channel failure possibilities, time to substitute, time resources can support the transition, and conditions behind those estimates.

“Borrowed viability” is a working diagnostic for continuation supported partly by external arrangements. It is not a precise cross-industry quantity that can simply be subtracted, and hypothetical total disconnection cannot replace realistic stress testing.

Tests should specify shocks, duration, cash consumption, replacement costs and adaptation paths. If required adaptation exceeds resource-supported time, investigate the feasibility of finance, contraction or changing paths.

## Chapter 7. Expectation gaps and hypotheses about excess returns

Forecasting business improvement does not mean discovering improvement absent from the price. Specify exactly where the judgement differs from assumptions compatible with the current price.

Reverse valuation can construct several implied scenarios: which margins, capital requirements, growth durations and discount conditions support the price? These are candidate explanations for comparison, not a uniquely inferred market consensus.

Differences may concern certification success, marginal margins, reinvestment space, failure risk or capital requirements. Testable future expectations are more useful than a generic claim that “the market undervalues it”.

The source draft paired state-transition judgements and expectation gaps as a heuristic for return sources. This public edition preserves that question structure without presenting an additive Alpha formula or sufficient condition for excess returns. Risk exposure, transaction costs, timing, competition and chance can change realised returns.

## Chapter 8. Intrinsic-value estimates and market prices

Intrinsic value is estimated from cash flow, distributions, risk and discount assumptions; unlike a traded price, it is not directly observable. Operating evidence, market interest rates and required risk compensation can all change it.

Prices form through specific trading mechanisms. Even with an unchanged business path, liquidity, discount rates, risk preferences and position constraints can move prices. Corporate valuation and price formation are modelled separately here, but are not independent, unrelated systems.

A decline may demand revisiting operating assumptions or may arise mainly from market conditions. Distinguish these with events, disclosures and evidence. Do not prescribe that “the price is wrong” or let a long-term thesis justify every short-term trading mistake.

## Chapter 9. Beliefs, holdings, order flow and marginal participants

One question-organising mechanism chain is: reality and news affect understanding; understanding and funding constraints affect desired holdings; orders and available liquidity jointly form transaction prices. Feedback is present; this is not a one-way empirical law.

Ask separately what changed in reality, how participants may interpret it, whether holdings already reflect that interpretation, and which capital still has willingness and capacity to adjust. Holdings and willingness are usually only partly observable; distinguish data, inference and unknowns.

“Who would keep buying or selling at the next price?” is a research prompt, not a formula that predicts prices once funding is identified. Every trade has two sides; price change depends on urgency, quotes and liquidity on both.

A failure to rise on good news does not prove that “everyone is already fully invested”. Compare anticipation of the information, changed valuation, execution quality and other compatible explanations.

## Chapter 10. Narratives, phases and discriminating market events

Narratives compress complex changes and coordinate attention, but may omit capital needs, certification cycles or competitive limits. Examine what is retained, what is omitted and whether omissions change action.

The same price recovery can reflect a widening buyer base, temporarily improved liquidity or reduced supply. A “life cycle” is therefore only a phase hypothesis to test. Do not define phases retrospectively from price paths and then explain the paths by the phases.

A discriminating event should generate different predictions from competing explanations in advance. “The buyer base is still expanding” and “new participation has diminished”, for example, can predict different subsequent transactions, breadth and funding persistence after comparable adverse news.

Freeze the window and expectations before recording results, allowing neither explanation to gain support. The event's value is its ability to distinguish explanations.

## Chapter 11. Market institutions and executability

Settlement, trading hours, price limits, securities lending, margin, order types and disclosure rules change available actions. Verify rules by exchange, instrument, account and effective date rather than using a market label to characterise all securities.

This chapter states an adaptation requirement, not a permanently valid rule table. Research and backtests should retain contemporary rule sources and specify whether fills at observed prices were possible and how suspensions or insufficient liquidity are handled.

Spreads, fees, impact, queues, capacity and exit restrictions separate forecast returns from realisable returns. A backtest assuming purchases at untradeable prices cannot support an executable-strategy judgement.

Institutional information belongs in the current model when it changes actions, funding needs, holding risks or exit capability.

## Chapter 12. Investor viability and investment policy

Investor viability means retaining the capacity to fulfil obligations and execute or revise a strategy under explicit loss, financing and liquidity scenarios. It is not merely low volatility, nor does high conviction establish it.

Position and loss-capacity research must distinguish severity, correlation, funding horizon, liquidity and other obligations. These are research dimensions, not position percentages or execution standards for readers. Models must allow high-confidence errors and apparently different positions dependent on common finance or demand.

Capital horizon, capacity and exit paths are part of the strategy. Greater size can reduce opportunities and increase impact, preventing replication of earlier small-scale returns. Waiting capacity requires preserved funding and sustainable obligations.

“Several routes to profit, few fatal failure modes” is a prompt. Actual analysis needs path dependencies, probabilities and severity. Three stories about the same business improvement do not create three independent routes.

Research can represent a candidate strategy as conditional entry, exposure, maintenance and exit hypotheses, recording assumptions, evidence and failure conditions. These are objects for evaluation, not live parameters, order rules or execution policies for readers.

## A one-page investment research record

| Item | Record to retain |
| --- | --- |
| Objective and horizon | What is judged, when it is reviewed and against which benchmark |
| Company | Present state, expected transition, capabilities, constraints, capital needs and per-share value |
| Market | Price-compatible scenarios, participant assumptions, expectation gaps and discriminating events |
| Investor | Funding sources, obligations, size, liquidity and worst feasible paths |
| Competing explanation | At least one alternative model fitting current evidence |
| Hypothesis updates and failure | Facts changing mechanisms and conditions invalidating a candidate strategy; no live instructions |
| Review | Separate outcome noise, parameter, representation, market-timing, execution and portfolio errors |

Profit does not automatically validate a causal explanation, and loss does not automatically reject all research. Return to information and action grounds available at the time, examining which expectations gained support and which need revision.
