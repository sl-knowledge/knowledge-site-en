## Where to begin?

New readers can start with the [research architecture](../methods/research-architecture.md) to see what the programme seeks to answer, then read the five parts in order. Consult the [canonical vocabulary and symbols](../methods/canonical-vocabulary.md) for definitions. For testing the research, go directly to the [originality and independent validation plan](../methods/originality-and-validation.md).

The shared thread is: real evidence → representation → reasoning → prediction and action → feedback → revision. Each part has a distinct responsibility; they can be read sequentially or entered from the current question.

## Versions and numbering

The source book numbers chapters continuously from 1 to 52, while each public part restarts at Chapter 1. The list below uses continuous book numbering and links to the actual chapter in its part. Thus Chapter 9 below is Chapter 1 of part two, and Chapter 43 is Chapter 1 of part five.

Public-edition versions and creation/update dates are recorded separately from research-draft versions. All five parts are continually revised public drafts. Complete contents do not imply completed academic peer review or formal book publication.

## How to use these articles

For an analysis, use part one to specify systems and feasibility and part two to build a testable representation. Move to part three when action is needed. Investment questions continue into part four; part five supports long-term evidence and research maintenance. When results conflict with expectations, revisit the representation and action grounds rather than merely rewriting the conclusion.

## Editorial scope and retained source drafts

The five structural consolidations are the principal public versions; companion documents retain their own provenance and status. Earlier reconstructions and conversational drafts remain in the research library for traceability, rather than appearing as several near-duplicate current texts.

Readers can follow later changes through page versions and the site's Git history. Source drafts remain intact; public editions are maintained separately by the site.
