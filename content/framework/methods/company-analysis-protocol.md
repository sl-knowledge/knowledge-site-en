## 0. Default starting page: three questions and a research gate

This is a starting point for organising company research. Inadequate information can remain unknown; tables need not be filled merely to produce a conclusion. “Protocol” means the research workflow, not securities or asset operations.

| Three required questions | Minimum answer |
|---|---|
| **1. What economic value can the company genuinely create repeatedly?** | One sentence identifying the customer's high-value task, distinctive capabilities/operating rules, cash cycle and dominant current constraint, with at least one direct fact. |
| **2. What future does today's market price already require?** | Market capitalisation and reference date, plus at least one concrete range for normal earnings or cash/growth assumptions justifying the price. If data are inadequate, write UNKNOWN rather than “the market has not noticed”. |
| **3. What independent evidence supports an explanation different from those price assumptions?** | State the difference hypothesis, independent sources and alternatives. Reverse valuation is not a unique reading of market psychology; inadequate information remains unknown. |

**Research-resource labels:** these allocate attention only, not buying, selling, holding, position sizes or return ratings.

- **DROP:** the core mechanism is disconfirmed, or there is neither a clear distinctive capability/testable expectation gap nor remaining research value.
- **WATCH_DISCOVERY:** strong historical capabilities, important unresolved constraints and plausible change paths exist, but critical evidence or a price gap is missing. Accumulate knowledge capital without requiring stock capital.
- **DEEPEN:** a specific testable expectation gap, independent leading evidence and a potentially executable path warrant deeper cash-flow, competitive, valuation and trading research. **This does not mean the security can be bought.**

**Stopping rule:** if questions two and three cannot be answered this round, report UNKNOWN / WATCH and specify only the next most valuable evidence. Do not invent valuations or transformation probabilities for apparent completeness.

## 1. The company model: one mechanism, not dozens of labels

### 1.1 Concepts and boundaries

| Concept | Meaning | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| **Environment (E)** | Technology, demand, customer budgets, prices, competitive institutions, geography and regulation | Treating profits from industry tailwinds as a changed operating model |
| **Input (I)** | Actual orders, funding, energy, raw materials and customer inputs | Treating cheap resources or a price cycle as enhanced competitiveness |
| **Resources (R)** | Assets, knowledge, employees, brand, patents, contracts, equity and funding | Inferring efficient use merely from possession |
| **Capacity (C)** | Tasks actually achievable in R&D, manufacturing, delivery, acquisition, service and learning | Inferring scale capability from a plan or sample |
| **Generator (G)** | How resources and capabilities are organised, allocated, transformed and monetised into repeatable economic output | Treating more products/revenue as reconstruction of G |
| **Economic Output (Y)** | Revenue, profit, cash, capital requirements, risk and shareholder value per share | Treating one result such as margin as the final objective |

Generator is a working name for operating mechanisms here, not a new independent mathematical object. Naming a mechanism does not establish it; evidence is required.

**Minimal economic chain:** customer task/payment constraint → resources and capabilities → organisation/product/channel → delivery and competition → charging/collections → reinvestment/debt → cash value per share for original shareholders.

When needed, use G=(A,Π,Φ,Λ) to ask: **A**, has organisation or connectivity changed? **Π**, how are people, money and capacity allocated? **Φ**, how are resources converted into products and delivery? **Λ**, have terms, pricing, credit and collection rules changed? These symbols support complex-reconstruction checks, not mandatory everyday mathematics.

### 1.2 Distinguish changed fuel from a changed engine

**Five common sources of improvement:** external demand/prices, changed inputs, greater utilisation of existing capability, operational parameters/execution efficiency, and genuine reconstruction of organisational and capital-cycle rules. They can coexist.

**Primary criterion:** if demand and price changes could produce similar results without changing customer, credit, production, organisational or allocation rules, prefer an environmental/operating-state explanation over a new Generator.

Evidence progresses from **strategic slogan → changed rules → costly execution → actual customer transactions → better unit economics and cash → replication across cycles**. An action's existence is not yet successful reconstruction.

### 1.3 Present and subsequent constraints

> Under an explicit model and other conditions, changing which condition could affect subsequent per-share economics? If several conditions jointly constrain results, what combined changes are necessary? What may bind next after improvement?

Demand, price, capacity, yield, channels, delivery, credit, funding, regulation, competitors and coordination can bind jointly. **Relaxing the present bottleneck does not create unlimited profit space.**

## 2. Three research depths: not every company needs a thesis

| Depth | When to use it | Formal output |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial screening** | First encounter, unclear information or unknown price gap | Three questions + DROP/WATCH_DISCOVERY/DEEPEN + the most important unknown |
| **Thesis research** | Grounds for continued work and assessment of reasonable value | Economic chain, cash and competition, critical evidence, three-scenario valuation, disconfirmation and review date |
| **Structural deep research** | Genuine reconstruction, complex ownership/capital cycles, major external change or financial contradictions | Causal-mechanism audit, crisis timeline, G changes, peer counterfactuals, alternatives and stress tests |

The public edition names depths to avoid confusion with evidence-consequence levels L1/L2/L3 below. Their source-draft depth labels correspond respectively to L1, L2 and L3.

**Reasons to deepen:** statements tell conflicting business stories; profit and cash diverge sharply; new cross-market operations depend on unproved capital efficiency; acquisitions/subsidiary claims are complex; critical constraints may change; new prices or shocks materially change viability.

**Stop adding concepts:** if another theory changes neither the present decision, critical next evidence, falsifiable prediction nor risk control, retain the simplest model. Research time is scarce.

## 3. Analytical sequence: twelve ordered questions

### 01 | Freeze objective, horizon and information

What question and horizon are studied—for example, multi-year operations, cash verification over several quarters or a historical price scenario? Record cut-off, industry, listed entity, share count and data dates. **Do not fill historical judgements with information from after the outcome.**

### 02 | Industry and customers: the actual economic task

Why do customers pay? What failure is most expensive? Which quantity, frequency, price, share or budget drives demand? Separate policy announcements, orders and actual settlement. Map customers, suppliers, substitutes and complements before listing products.

### 03 | Historical baseline: conventional finance and competition

Using same-date material, first produce a strong conventional analysis: earnings sources, cyclical versus structural growth, industry and competitive position, cash conversion, normalised valuation, and strongest bullish/bearish arguments. This is the **control baseline** for testing incremental value from more complex methods.

### 04 | Company-specific economic Generator

What can this company repeat that competitors cannot readily reproduce? Does pricing, low cost, brand, certification, distribution, service or workflow capture actual payment? Distinguish **company-controlled links** from links controlled by the overall solution or integrator.

### 05 | Resources, capabilities and replication

Is this one success or scalable replication? For the 10th and 100th similar customer/project, how do R&D, staffing, field delivery, inventory and capital consumption change? Large scale is not low replication cost; specialists can standardise too. Synergy needs evidence from repeat/cross-purchasing, interface/R&D reuse and capital returns.

### 06 | Constraints, causes of change and autonomous choice

What does external pressure force the company to change, and which changes are foresight versus survival? **Being forced under pressure while actively choosing a good path** is coherent, not a simple active/passive binary. Distinguish unchanged G, efficiency improvement, capability upgrade, partial/whole reconstruction and environmental activation.

### 07 | Competitor dynamics and disconfirmation

Which customer task is most vulnerable? Could lower costs, general technology, interfaces, open standards, acquisitions or new workflows bypass the moat? Can the company defend cheaply? Compare adaptation speed, losses and incremental ROIC. Allow specialist victory, platform victory, layered coexistence or low returns for all.

### 08 | Profitable growth space, not abstract TAM

Discount sequentially from **physical/technical possibility → willingness to pay → company access → margins after competition → incremental receivables/inventory/capital → economic profit per share**. After an old constraint improves, identify the next bottleneck, remaining headroom and breakthrough cost. “Applicable to a field” is not an established meaningful market.

### 09 | Bridge financials, cash and capital

Check key links across all three statements: material prices, order stocking, inventory mix, receivables/notes/factoring, contract assets/liabilities, supplier terms, impairments/write-offs, nonrecurring items, debt maturities, capex, acquisitions, minority interests and shares. **One quarter's operating-cash-flow sign is not a quality verdict; “strategic stocking” cannot explain everything without proof.**

### 10 | Organisational learning, governance, crisis and allocation

Examine management's historical forecasts and choices, not personality. In crises, did it abandon bad business, protect cash, rebuild customer-selection and authority rules, admit failure and stop spending? Compare alternatives genuinely available then, without judging earlier choices by today's outcomes. Past-market success does not prove risk controls replicated in another country or business.

### 11 | Critical evidence, disconfirmation and long-term assessment

Compress mechanisms into **one to three propositions most likely to determine future success or failure**. For each specify Criticality, Confidence, observable strong evidence, alternatives, deadline and response to failure. Do not upgrade conclusions by counting short-term news. See section 7.

### 12 | Valuation, market assumptions and conclusion boundaries

At the same reference date, compare price-compatible assumptions with independent defensive, base and successful operating paths. Record capital needs, costs and model sensitivity. Market variables and candidate-strategy executability require separate tests. **Report corporate conclusions, research priority and verification status of price assumptions separately; this article outputs no securities action.**

## 4. Competition, growth and capital: seven indispensable checks

1. **Customer constraints before technology lists:** customers pay for reliable task completion, not engineering skill lists.
2. **Dynamic comparison before static labels:** specialists, platforms, large and small firms learn. External technology such as AI may increase old capability's value or commoditise it.
3. **Value capture before social value:** saving a customer 100 does not mean the supplier receives 100. Identify pricing rights, ownership of switching costs and scarce interfaces.
4. **Business reuse before project count:** does revenue growth accompany lower relative development/delivery cost, faster deployment, better cash and incremental returns?
5. **Growth space beyond one relaxed constraint:** watering a plant in a box does not allow unlimited growth. Demand, competition, assets and the next constraint jointly limit returns.
6. **Net per-share outcomes before scale:** access to group resources, who bears R&D/funding costs, subsidiary minorities and continuing issuance belong in valuation.
7. **Allow durable compounding and regenerative revaluation:** a good company need not have spectacular new space; a formerly strong company may fail to regenerate. The proof burdens and valuation margins of safety differ.

**Avoid two substitutions:** “the overall system is hard to replace” does not imply “this supplier is hard to replace”; “the group has cash/technology” does not imply original listed-company shareholders capture the full return.

## 5. Management and organisational DNA: test decision records, not personality labels

Organisational DNA means repeatable **priority ordering, authority allocation, risk tolerance, correction and resource-reallocation policies** across environments. It can change through learning; it is not management personality or immutable nature.

### 5.1 A six-stage audit around crisis

| Stage | Facts to retain |
|---|---|
| Before crisis | Contemporary customers, competition, finance, opportunity costs and cycle; later losses alone do not make every earlier choice wrong |
| Warning | Signals then visible and when management spoke and acted |
| Preservation and abandonment | Protection of capabilities and buffers, rejection of bad orders, disposal of poor assets |
| Rule reconstruction | Changes to admission, credit, delivery, responsibility and allocation rather than slogans |
| Capital trade-offs | Actual allocation across debt repayment, dividends, repurchases, acquisitions and new projects; recorded stopping conditions |
| Repeated stress tests | Whether disciplines survive contrary temptations during recovery or overseas expansion |

### 5.2 Strength of organisational-learning evidence

Declaration → institutional design → costly execution → repeated adherence in adverse and favourable conditions → improved long-run risk-adjusted cash returns. Later stages approach evidence of MetaCapacity, but macro tailwinds and accounting effects still need separation.

**Risks are not interchangeable:** customer credit, payment fraud/internal controls, overseas acquisition allocation, listed-company governance and shareholder rights require different controls. Success of one regime does not remove every risk; one operational incident does not automatically destroy group-wide capability.

**Counterfactuals must use contemporary information:** without that expansion, acquisition, contraction or correction, what opportunity costs and risks were foreseeable then? Avoid survivorship and hindsight bias.

## 6. Financial due diligence: reconnect reported earnings to the full capital cycle

### 6.1 The accounting chain that cannot be skipped

Customer payment and revenue recognition → operating profit → working capital and operating cash → maintenance/expansion spending → debt and finance → minority interests and share count → estimated cash value per share.

This is a checking path, not an accounting identity or directly subtractable formula. Keep entity, period, cash-flow category and ownership basis consistent.

A single margin or EBITDA figure cannot replace these layers. A capital-intensive company earning incremental returns above capital costs can still be a good asset; a high-margin, asset-light company unable to collect receivables can still be poor business.

### 6.2 Decompose major operating-cash-flow changes

| Change | Possible good news | Possible risk | More discriminating next evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Higher receivables | New customers/more normal sales | Easier credit, note substitution, bad debt | Customer/channel breakdown, ageing, losses, write-offs and actual collections |
| Higher inventory | Ordered stocking or locked material prices | Misjudged demand, impairment and obsolescence | Delivery of new orders, composition, turnover and provisions |
| Changed payables/contract liabilities | Better terms or customer advances | Delayed supplier payments or weak presales | Terms, cash settlement and fulfilment |
| Operating cash turns positive | Better sales quality or lower credit risk | Seasonal working-capital release, terms or nonrecurring receipts; factoring requires substance/standards checks | Rolling four-quarter cash bridge and normalised capital needs |
| Rapid revenue growth | Organic demand/share gains | Consolidation, price increases or one-off orders | Comparable organic volumes, prices, contribution margins and post-investment cash |
| Higher capex | Future efficiency and capacity | Low-return expansion or sustaining losses | Project returns, actual orders and subsequent depreciation/cash |

Fixed-asset purchases and disposals, acquisitions/disposals of subsidiaries and financing need separate classification under applicable standards, not all “operating” merely because cash changes. See [IAS 7 cash-flow classification](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-7-statement-of-cash-flows/).

**Industry adjustments:** banks, insurers, brokers, developers, construction contracts and platforms need appropriate balance-sheet/cash logic. Manufacturing working-capital formulas are not universal.

### 6.3 Company and shareholder are different levels

Examine ownership, joint-venture co-investment, funding costs, options/share compensation, related parties, acquisition consideration and minority distributions. Transfer pricing and minority claims can allow group revenue growth without adequate returns to original shareholders.

**Incremental economics:** under consistent definitions and reasonable attribution, compare additional after-tax operating return with additional capital costs. Test through long-term free cash returns per share rather than producing falsely precise ROIC from one year's volatility.

## 7. Critical Evidence: what determines the research conclusion

L1/L2/L3 from the companion charter label consequences of evidence failure for a proposition, not research depth, corporate quality or position size. Confidence records evidential support, not calibrated success probability.

### 7.1 Every important proposition needs an evidence card

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Core proposition | Object, horizon and environment, e.g. a new channel sustaining cash profit with lower credit-capital use |
| Criticality | **L1:** core thesis collapses; **L2:** reduced future growth/revaluation; **L3:** local efficiency change. Specify whether this affects the company thesis or present-price thesis |
| Confidence | Long-term validation / early support / unproved / material disconfirmation / conflicting information |
| Most discriminating evidence | Company/customer/contract/segment and attributable cash basis, persistence and benchmark |
| Strongest alternative | Cycle, consolidation, price rises, delayed payment, subsidy, accounting or selection |
| Strongest disconfirmation / Kill Test | Conditions requiring material downgrade or withdrawal, not narrative escape |
| Deadline and absence rule | Next report/two-year horizon; specify in advance whether continued absence means unknown, lower confidence or research termination |
| Effect on conclusion | Which mechanism or price scenario changes, what remains missing and when to reassess; no trading instructions |

### 7.2 Both supporting and opposing evidence need discrimination

**Poor example:** “New-business revenue rose 20%, proving reconstruction succeeded.”

**More discriminating but still unvalidated:** “After removing acquisitions and price changes, the new channel shows sustained improvement in receivables/revenue, long-run credit loss, unit gross profit and attributable free cash returns at similar customer risk. Revenue growth reliant on longer terms and debt would not support success.”

**Caution:** comparable revenue, margin and cash changes can share one macro driver. Highly correlated evidence is not three independent validations. If rivals all predict the observation, seek more discriminating evidence rather than another page of theory.

### 7.3 Gradual accumulation and irreversible disconfirmation

Daily/weekly prices or one quarterly report usually change short-term expectations or local parameters, not automatically years of Generator understanding. Fraud, material default, lost core licences or irreversible major-customer exits can trigger core constraints and require immediate L1 review. Long-term investing does not mean ignoring major new information.

**Missing evidence is not success evidence.** Repeated absence of expected customers, profit, cash or learning by the deadline should revise confidence under frozen forecasts, not perpetually become “wait another quarter”.

### 7.4 Three separate conclusions, not one mysterious score

1. **Corporate economic judgement:** supported operating foundation / insufficient transformation evidence / structural erosion / insufficient information. State dominant mechanisms, constraints and unresolved L1/L2/L3 issues.
2. **Research gate:** DROP / WATCH_DISCOVERY / DEEPEN, allocating attention by potential information value.
3. **Price and candidate-strategy research status:** assumptions recorded / insufficient data / awaiting independent tests / disconfirmed. This is not securities advice and does not derive trades or exposure from the first two.

## 8. Anonymous teaching comparison: failure consequences and evidential support are different axes

The source compared research on two actual companies as of 2026-10-11. Their facts and numbers were not reverified for this publication. To avoid apparent current ratings, the public edition uses anonymous teaching scenarios. They are not substitute disclosures about actual companies and retain no stock codes.

| Item | Scenario A: cash-cycle reconstruction unproved | Scenario B: established business supported, new opportunity unproved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Premise | Changed channels/credit arrangements observed, effects unknown | Existing capability provisionally retained; new-business effects unknown |
| Core unresolved proposition | Can new rules sustain cash returns with lower credit consumption? | Can a new task generate post-competition profit and cash attributable to original shareholders? |
| Failure consequence | L1 if the original thesis requires the new cash cycle | Mainly L2 if established-business assumptions remain valid |
| Missing discriminating evidence | Comparable receivables, ageing, credit loss, organic revenue and capital use | Payment/repeat purchase, independent cash, minority claims and replication costs |
| Alternatives | Demand tailwinds, consolidation, longer terms, exported risk | Cycle improvement, a few customers, subsidies or one-off gains |
| Research output | Unsupported claims and next observation; no security rating | Supported elements separated from new assumptions, without actions from a “good company” label |

Consequences still depend on specific theses and price scenarios. If a price assumption requires extraordinary new-business success, its evidence gap can be L1 for that assumption. Reliable cash evidence in A can reduce the consequence of unresolved questions.

This compares **what is unproved and which conclusion depends on it**, not stock returns, relative allocations or buying/selling.

## 9. Traceable case-research template

> **Company / code:** ____　 **Fact cut-off:** ____　 **Objective / horizon:** ____
> **Information completeness:** sufficient / insufficient / conflicting　 **Conclusion:** provisional / review required

| Order | Content: decision-relevant facts and hypotheses only |
|---|---|
| **1. Core economic mechanism** | Customer task, actual Generator, distinctive value-control points and two strongest direct facts |
| **2. Environment and old/new constraints** | Demand, competition, budgets and policy; dominant constraint, relaxation mechanism and next bottleneck |
| **3. Business/capital economics** | Organic versus consolidated growth, margins, normal profit, OCF/FCF, capital/credit, minorities and per-share value |
| **4. Profitable growth space** | Accessible incremental demand, responses, cash/capital costs, old-business decline and possible five-year ceiling |
| **5. Organisational DNA / correction** | Decisions, contemporary conditions, outcomes, corrections and falsifiable future behaviour |
| **6. Most dangerous competitive/financial disconfirmation** | How substitution breaks the thesis; cash/credit/capital arrangements creating irreversible risk |
| **7. Critical evidence 1, preferably L1** | Proposition, criticality, confidence, best evidence, alternatives and deadline/Kill Test |
| **8. Critical evidence 2–3, optional** | Only evidence changing long-term conclusions, not ordinary quarterly KPI filler |
| **9. Scenarios and valuation** | Defensive/base/success cash value per share, assumptions and price-implied future; UNKNOWN if inadequate |
| **10. Long-term fundamental assessment** | Supported foundation, unproved components, emerging damage and facts changing the prior assessment |
| **11. Research-resource gate** | DROP/WATCH_DISCOVERY/DEEPEN and one to three worthwhile next observations |
| **12. Price/strategy validation status** | Evidence for assumptions, data timing, costs and executability; gaps separately listed, no reader-directed trades or exposure |
| **13. Next review** | Report/policy/customer evidence dates and a non-indeferrable observation deadline |

### Minimal event-update record

Record time and information-availability time | material and original definitions | fact/statement/inference/hypothesis/unknown | affected proposition | evidence independence | support/disconfirmation/irrelevance | changed long-term assessment and grounds | required price/strategy hypothesis revision | next evidence and review deadline.

Preserve previous records as read-only snapshots. Append evidence without rewriting original forecasts. Remodelling is allowed but must be identified as revision, not prior knowledge.

## 10. Evidence standards, boundaries and real-world tests

**Source priority:** statutory announcements/statements, exchange/regulatory disclosure, independent customers/partners, product acceptance/actual cash, industry/official statistics and credible third-party research. Separate management discussion from actual economics. Community views are leads only.

**Fact labels:** FACT / REPORTED / INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS / UNKNOWN / CONTESTED. Verify entity, period, ownership attribution, accounting definitions, units, year-on-year/sequential bases, consolidation and comparability.

**Strict order for cross-domain reference:** build a mechanism Signature from company facts—objective, activities, constraints, repeatable rules and failures → seek structurally comparable candidates elsewhere → verify preservation of mechanisms and boundaries → transfer only predictions testable with the target company's facts. Similar stories are not proof.

**External checks against bias:** compare strong conventional baselines, include non-transformers, failures and fully priced cases, avoid hindsight and survivorship in historical work, and do not let one complex case continually expand the vocabulary.

**Performance remains unproved:** a credible company model does not guarantee market outperformance. Evaluate through frozen prospective forecasts, out-of-sample judgement and actual returns/risk net of costs. Simplify or remove language that does not improve these outcomes.

## 11. Editorial basis, validation status and reading route

Based on the unified *Company Analysis Protocol v2.0* dated 2026-10-11, independently numbered public v0.1. It retains three questions, resource routing, three depths, twelve ordered questions, competitive/capital checks, organisational learning, financial verification, critical evidence and event monitoring.

The public edition replaces security-action codes with research status, unverified dated company comparisons with anonymous teaching scenarios, distinguishes depth from evidence consequences, and tightens operating-cash-flow classification and calculation-chain wording. Source drafts and archives remain; this reports no new stock data or current ratings.
