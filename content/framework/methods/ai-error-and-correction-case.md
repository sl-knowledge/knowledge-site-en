## Overview

In a discussion about cross-domain research methods, the researcher first built a general framework for deformation and fracture in metals, intending to introduce hydrogen later. The AI criticised the work for being insufficiently specific to hydrogen embrittlement because it contained no hydrogen. The researcher pointed out that the task at this stage was to assess the generative framework; filling in domain-specific parameters belonged to the next stage. The AI withdrew that criticism while retaining a question about a channel the general framework might have omitted.

This distinction runs through the record. Some errors concerned facts. Others came from misreading the research goal, the level of evaluation or the division of roles. Even accurate domain knowledge can send the discussion in the wrong direction when it is applied at the wrong level. The following 17 observations record what went wrong, who identified it and what happened afterwards.

## Four checks before an assessment

| Check | What to ask first | What went wrong here |
|---|---|---|
| Material read | Did the AI read the original, a summary or only a title? How much can its judgement cover? | Declared the site short of testable results before reading it |
| Task and level | Are we choosing a direction, building a generative framework, adding domain parameters or validating results? What is the deliverable? | Used a recall-oriented test for knowledge integration, and domain coverage to assess a general framework |
| Evaluation criteria and roles | What does the researcher want to compare? Who can give hints, and who assesses the result? | Substituted originality for the agreed integration criteria; the examiner also gave clues |
| Evidence and status | What has been done, and what is only planned? What comes from the record, and what is conjecture? | Reported unfinished preparation as complete and treated an unknown future change as a known trend |

These questions adapt the approach in [Where Does Ethical Disagreement Come From?](./ethical-disagreement-map.md): reconstruct the question before separating roles and evaluation structures. In this case they helped locate deviations retrospectively. Whether using them beforehand reduces such deviations requires a separate comparison.

## 1. Seventeen observations and corrections

### Establishing what to assess

1. **Judging before reading.** Without opening the site, the AI said it lacked sharply testable results and suggested adding an English edition. The researcher pointed out that the English site already existed and asked what justified the judgement about the articles. The AI subsequently found the English edition, propositions with conditions that could weaken them, and an existing validation plan. It withdrew its earlier assertions. It had mainly examined a small number of tool summaries, still insufficient for assessing the entire site.
2. **Conflating an article with a sustained research programme.** The AI assessed the work by saying that anyone could produce an apparently complete framework. The researcher distinguished producing one article from maintaining dozens of mutually constraining studies across domains. The AI accepted the distinction while retaining a question: papers using the same framework may also share blind spots.
3. **Testing the wrong ability.** The researcher wanted to see whether, within an hour, he could enter an unfamiliar field, organise the material and produce a synthesis with a usable checklist. The AI proposed a closed-book prediction exercise that leaned towards recall and factual questions. After the researcher objected, the task was changed to writing an integrative article, with an AI comparison condition proposed.
4. **Replacing the first test domain.** The first domain selected by the AI involved high-risk medication. The explanation was interrupted and the domain withdrawn. This episode concerns the choice of test material; it does not establish success or failure of the research method.
5. **Choosing an overly favourable task.** The AI then selected a topic closely suited to the existing framework. The researcher pointed out that this made it difficult to learn whether the method worked in an unfamiliar field that did not already fit it. Hydrogen embrittlement became the exploratory topic instead.

### Exploring the domain and checking the record

6. **Reporting preparation as complete too early.** The AI said it had already sealed a set of items for later checking. In its retrospective review, it found that the file was created and hashed in the next turn. That was still before the domain explanation, but the earlier statement of completion was inaccurate at the time. The AI identified this in its own review.
7. **Giving hints while acting as examiner.** The AI was to assess the work, but in its interim comments it named domain mechanisms and suggested a missing channel. It subsequently identified those contributions as its own hints. Later findings related to those hints should be recorded separately and cannot be credited as independently derived by the researcher.
8. **Assessing a generative framework by domain coverage.** The researcher showed a general representation of deformation and fracture in metals. The AI objected that hydrogen was absent. The researcher corrected it: “I'm looking at the larger framework first. I'm not in a hurry to put hydrogen into it.” The AI withdrew the criticism: omitting the parameter was a deliberate stage of the work. It retained the question of whether the general framework lacked a channel for external species to enter and accumulate. That question could still be considered at the appropriate level.
9. **Calling a paper a book.** The AI described Herbert A. Simon's *The Architecture of Complexity* as a book. At the researcher's request it checked the reference, confirming that the work at issue was a [1962 journal article](https://www.jstor.org/stable/985254).

### Discussing direction and the value of the method

10. **Losing the main question.** Asked about the overall programme, the AI opened several lines of discussion about experimental measures, controls and future effects. The researcher returned to the main question: how much of the ability to ask and organise across fields is expressed in the method, and how much still depends on personal experience? The discussion returned to inventory, comparison with prior work, a standalone description and transfer tests.
11. **Answering a question about direction with an implementation plan.** The researcher asked to discuss the significance of the work first. The AI still included experimental design. After the researcher clarified the scope, the AI restricted its answer to direction.
12. **Treating an unknown change as a trend.** The AI repeatedly predicted that the method would become less valuable to AI as models improved. The researcher noted that the method might also help organise AI work and check its answers, and that greater capability did not automatically eliminate the mismatch seen here. The AI revised the future change to unknown. This dialogue supplies an instance that can be examined now, not a prediction of future value.
13. **Rebutting a stronger claim.** The researcher proposed that the method could be a foundational course. The AI answered as though it had been proposed as the only course needed. The researcher clarified the claim, and the AI acknowledged that its response went beyond what was said.
14. **Omitting “known elsewhere, unknown here”.** The AI initially distinguished only what was not yet known from what was known but poorly applied. The researcher added another possibility: a method may already exist in another discipline without having been transferred to the present problem. This changed the assessment of the method's potential value; whether a structure transfers still needs case-by-case checks.
15. **Using the wrong evaluation criterion.** The researcher had asked for assessment of integration, exposition and usable checklists. The AI repeatedly treated novelty as the main measure. After correction it assessed the quality of the integration first. This remains compatible with accurate attribution of prior work and independent checks of usefulness.
16. **Not using an available checking procedure first.** A reference card for analysis was available in the conversation, but the AI did not use it before reviewing the work. At the researcher's request, the AI used it to revisit the discussion and separate facts, tasks, roles and levels of evaluation, locating the deviations.
17. **Misattributing errors across stages in retrospect.** The AI previously assigned deviations and corrections from different stages to the last part of the conversation. It corrected that attribution during the review. The 17 entries are observations, not a count of one model's errors or of the times the researcher intervened.

## 2. Which points remain after the corrections?

The researcher's corrections brought the evaluation back to the task and left some questions open:

- One person's ability to use the method does not establish that another person or an AI can use it from the document. Transfer depends on the results of independent use.
- Similarity to existing conclusions, when reached with an AI that has absorbed prior work, can supply a comparison. Agreement alone does not establish independent validation.
- Whether it is easy to add domain content after building a framework depends on the field and the evidence required. A framework applied broadly also needs checks for recurring blind spots.

Corrections came from four sources: the researcher intervening, checking materials, reviewing the conversation with an existing procedure, and the AI's retrospective self-checks. Entry 8 shows why correcting the evaluation level does not amount to accepting the whole result. Putting criticism at the appropriate level makes it possible to decide what to withdraw and what to investigate further.

## 3. What remains to be tested?

The record shows how deviations were identified and corrected retrospectively. To test whether advance checks improve collaboration, compare similar tasks with and without the four questions. Define a task mismatch beforehand and record hints, factual corrections, researcher interventions and their costs separately. One retrospective case cannot estimate error frequency or the size of any improvement.

Reusing familiar categories, supplying default evaluation criteria and losing the overall goal in a long conversation are possible explanations inferred from behaviour. The researcher also suggested a role for training methods. The conversation does not independently identify these causes.
