> **Public edition v0.1 · source v2.0 (2026-10-11).** An investment-research methodology retaining the principal source sections, edited for public research boundaries. Material, models and examples are not investment or trading advice. No excess-return ability validated through large samples, out-of-sample tests and actual portfolio performance is reported.

## 0. One-page charter: what are we doing?

**Research objective:** use reliable evidence to understand a company's ability to sustain cash value attributable to each share in changing environments, compare scenario-conditioned value estimates with assumptions required by prices, and test whether this research adds explanatory or predictive value. This article provides no reader-directed return targets, trading plans or suitability judgements.

**One core chain:**

> High-value customer task → environment and critical constraints → resources and capabilities → economic Generator → actual competition, value capture and capital cycle → sustainable cash value per share → price-compatible assumptions → research conclusions, alternatives and later tests.

**The emphasis beyond an indicator checklist:** identify **causal mechanisms actually determining outcomes**, rather than maximising financial-data lists. In particular, identify one to three unvalidated pieces of decisive evidence—Critical Evidence—on which future success depends.

**Keep three questions separate:**

1. **Company quality:** can the company sustain value creation in present and future environments?
2. **Investment thesis and evidence:** how much of the decisive mechanism is established, and what is the strongest disconfirmation?
3. **Price and strategy research:** which future assumptions fit the current price, and which risks, costs or data gaps obstruct testable strategy hypotheses? These questions do not directly imply securities actions.

**Six boundaries:**

- Revenue growth ≠ cash growth; corporate growth ≠ shareholder value growth per share.
- Capability ≠ sufficient **profitable** growth space; space ≠ eventual profit capture.
- Historical success ≠ persistent advantage in a new environment; managerial action ≠ successful reconstruction.
- One abnormal quarter usually cannot overturn a long-term thesis, but one irreversible event breaking a core constraint can.
- A good company ≠ a good purchase price; owning excellence ≠ never selling.
- An elegant explanation ≠ a prospectively effective forecast. Complexity must justify itself through testable incremental value.

## 1. Research objects: corporate economics and secondary-market trading

Investment confronts two coupled systems.

| System | Research object | Main evidence | Invalid inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Operating economic system** | How demand, competition, organisation, technology, capital and collections generate long-run per-share value | Customer payment, products, segments, statements, contracts and capital returns | Growth does not imply high ROIC or high valuation |
| **Secondary-market system** | How investors, risk budgets, liquidity, expectations and rules form actual prices | Valuation, policy, prices, volume, relative strength and transaction structure | A market rise does not establish better operating mechanisms |

**Two-way feedback:** prices may affect finance, acquisitions, incentives and credit. The systems are not fully independent, but identify the feedback actually reaching operations. Technical information can lead, lag or misread fundamentals. It should neither be excluded in advance nor replace evidence of long-run economics.

**Time layers:** operating mechanisms, competitive positions and allocation are mainly multi-year; statements, orders, receivables and inventories often need several quarters; price/volume and funding changes operate over days, weeks and months. They correspond to different assessments, valuations and trading decisions.

## 2. Minimal concepts: enough to explain, not extra layers for new names

**E (Environment)** covers demand, prices, technology, regulation and industry conditions; **I (Input)** specific inputs and orders; **R (Resources)** deployable resources; **C (Capacity)** tasks resources can actually accomplish; **G (Generator)** operating rules organising resources/capabilities for repeated value creation and capture; **Y (Output)** economic results and risks.

> Y = F(E, I; R, C, G) is a conceptual expression preserving causal boundaries, **not an estimated forecasting function**.

When needed, separate G into **A (organisation/relationships), Π (allocation), Φ (production/delivery transformation), Λ (charging, credit and capital recovery)**. Ordinary analysis need not expand these symbols. Generator and MetaCapacity are working language for organising questions, not new universal mathematical atoms or replacements for domain mechanism identification.

**Constraint:** a condition limiting admissible states, actions or transitions under specified objectives, environments and horizons. Corporate diagnosis must explain its limitation on economically relevant improvement, not call every adverse factor binding. Demand, cash, acquisition, cost, yields, competition or coordination may constrain jointly. After relaxing an old constraint, identify the **next one**.

**Viability:** the ability to maintain operations, finance and necessary choices through transformation and adversity. Book net assets alone do not fully express viability.

**MetaCapacity:** the ability to discover new tasks, abandon old arrangements, recombine resources and correct rules as conditions change. Test it through costly actions and sustained outcomes against alternatives, not praise of visionary management or past success.

**Growth box:** even with capability/funding constraints relaxed, demand, customer access, competition and capital returns limit **profitable space**. Describe accessible tasks → post-competition share → sustainable profit → added capital → per-share value, not industry TAM alone.

## 3. Three opportunity paths: discovery leads, not permanent identities

| Path | Critical causal judgement | Primary disconfirmation |
|---|---|---|
| **I: Generator Reconstruction** | Operating/credit/delivery/allocation rules genuinely change and produce better long-term economics | Merely changed channels or consolidation, without better unit capital returns or cash cycles |
| **II: Durable High-Quality Generator** | Existing mechanisms sustain shareholder cash value and withstand future competition/reinvestment | A changed environment devalues the moat, or returns require price rises/continual finance |
| **III: Environment-Driven Revaluation** | External constraints relax, letting existing capability solve more valuable tasks, without necessarily changing G | Industry demand grows but competitors capture value and sustainable profit is absent |

A company can fit several paths. **II needs no technological revolution; I cannot equate strategic commitment with completed reconstruction; III cannot book industry tailwinds directly as shareholder cash.**

**Priority research profile, not exclusive criteria:** established economic capability difficult to replicate cheaply, a potentially changing constraint, sufficient profitable space and capital efficiency afterward, and a market not fully paying for net value. Mature low-growth firms at suitable prices may nevertheless be better investments.

**Two value-formation hypotheses to test:**

- **Continuing-operation value:** under explicit competition, capital and cash assumptions, examine whether current business sustains shareholder value. This does not guarantee stable returns.
- **Reconstruction/environmental-change value:** examine incremental value from existing capability after constraints change, retaining competing failure, timing, financing and capture scenarios.

“Regeneration option” is a scenario-analysis metaphor, not an identified tradeable option value. Not every company needs a reconstruction story.

## 4. Research sequence: understand the company independently, then compare price

1. **Build a strong conventional baseline.** Clarify industry, statements, competition, normal earnings, conservative valuation and common risks. New concepts must not obscure fundamentals.
2. **Rebuild the distinctive economic chain target-first.** Why pay, what does the company control, and which cost, capital and credit links determine profit? Separate industry commonalities from company-specific capability.
3. **Identify binding constraints and transitions.** Did environment, capability, rules or merely cyclical/accounting parameters change? Which counterfactual distinguishes them?
4. **Compare competition and growth space.** Technology may improve capability or commoditise old advantages; rivals learn. Assess joint demand, share, pricing, cost, funding and bottleneck changes.
5. **Translate growth into economics per share.** Check pricing, credit loss, inventory, maintenance/expansion capex, funding, tax, minorities, dilution and capital costs.
6. **Identify one to three decisive pieces of evidence.** They must distinguish success mechanisms from alternatives, with current support, strongest disconfirmation and deadlines.
7. **Evaluate value and market state independently.** What outcomes are possible, how much is priced in, and is there tolerance for error? Examine technical conditions separately.
8. **Separate research progress from capital action.** Discovery, validation and actual investment differ. Conclusions here allocate research resources, not readers' capital.

**Depth rule:** usually start with one page, deepening only when decisive mechanisms, cash, competition, governance or transformation truly warrant it. Explanations that change neither evidence, forecasts, risk judgement nor action do not belong in the core.

## 5. Evidence: decisive support, not news counts

### 5.1 Specify what must be established before judging evidence

The same fact matters differently to propositions about **survival**, **new-model success**, **rapid growth** or **undervaluation at the present price**. Do not merge them.

Record each core proposition as:

> Proposition (target, environment, time) → decisive mechanism → most direct observable → alternatives/strongest disconfirmation → independence and completeness → current support → how the thesis changes if requirements are not met.

### 5.2 Two axes that must not be merged

**Criticality: consequences of evidence failure**

| Level | Question | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **L1 Thesis-Critical** | If false, does the core long-run thesis or a necessary price-scenario assumption collapse? | Revalue/withdraw the thesis, not merely wait longer |
| **L2 Revaluation-Critical** | Does the operating foundation remain while growth space, speed or premium fails? | Revise growth/revaluation scenarios, not automatically reject all existing value |
| **L3 Optimization Evidence** | Is the main logic intact, with only local efficiency/growth affected? | Adjust parameters, not the long-term assessment on one ordinary quarter |

**Confidence: degree of support:** multi-period historical validation / preliminary support / unproved or insufficient data / material disconfirmation / conflicting facts. “Unproved” is not “failed”; an announced plan is not a successful plan.

**Relativity:** levels depend on the proposition and price assumptions. For a hypothetical mature firm, new-business failure might chiefly affect L2 if existing operations remain viable, but be L1 for a price scenario dependent on that business succeeding. Deteriorating mature operations may elevate formerly lower-priority questions. This is a logical example, not a current company assessment.

### 5.3 Discrimination matters more than quantity

- Strategies and partnerships establish actions/claims, not outcomes. Distinguish stronger contract, usage, repeatable unit-economics and attributable-cash evidence.
- Reprints or common-source data are not independent; group averages cannot be imposed on undisclosed sub-businesses.
- Revenue may grow from acquisitions, prices, loose credit or organic demand. **Data distinguishing alternatives** genuinely improve confidence.
- Revenue, margin and OCF can share one industry-cycle cause; do not add them mechanically as three validations.
- When unavailable, mark UNKNOWN and reduce confidence in dependent propositions. Never invent success probabilities.

### 5.4 Evidence grows over time—and can die when conditions change

Corporate capability resembles a plant shaped by internal generation, available inputs, external space, competition and irreversible risks. **Long-run judgements should follow consequential causal slow variables**; quarterly revenue and short-term price are mainly fast variables. Yet credit collapse, fraud or unrecoverable customer loss can justify changing long-run conclusions after one major event.

Each update records: new evidence → affected proposition and level → genuine incremental information → changed operating assumptions → changed price/trading plan. **Set expiry reviews and Kill Tests:** repeated absence of critical evidence cannot perpetually mean waiting one more quarter. Revise hypotheses under advance conditions.

### 5.5 Levels 1/2/3 are not mechanical numerical scores

These classify **consequences**, not three mandatory company types. Do not confuse them with research depths or old maturity labels, and do not map them to fixed exposure. Use qualitative assessments and traceable records, not precise uncalibrated scores.

## 6. Earnings quality and risk boundaries

**Economic evaluation basis:** estimates of sustainable cash value attributable to each original share across scenarios, accounting for risk and capital needs. This is an analytical choice, not uniquely observable “true value”. ROE, PE, PB and gross margin are not substitutes.

### 6.1 Decompose falling operating cash flow

Examine receivables/ageing, notes and receivables finance, bad debts/write-offs, contract assets/liabilities, prepayments, inventory/provisions, payables/supplier finance, tax, seasonality and scope changes. Capex, subsidiary acquisitions/disposals and financing need separate bridges in applicable investing/financing categories, not inclusion as operating-cash-flow causes. Inventory can reflect deliberate stocking or weak sales; later sales, margins, losses and collections discriminate.

**When cash and profit conflict**, do not choose the favourable explanation selectively. Construct an operating-cash-flow bridge and a cash–debt–capex bridge.

### 6.2 Incremental returns before scale

Higher profit may not increase per-share value if invested capital grows faster or credit losses/minorities absorb it. Test:

> Incremental sustainable economic profit = incremental after-tax operating profit − capital cost of incremental invested capital, under consistent definitions.

This is an auxiliary check; financing and distributions also need calculation. Acquired consolidated scale is not proven internal replication. Compare allocation with debt repayment, buybacks, dividends, reinvestment and acquisition opportunity costs.

### 6.3 Three distinct buffers

| Buffer | Research question |
|---|---|
| **Business Buffer** | Can the company service debt, operate and retain choices under stress? |
| **Valuation Buffer** | If success fails to materialise, how sensitive are price assumptions to failure and valuation error? The model supplies no actual loss floor. |
| **Portfolio Buffer** | Can the investor bear concentration, liquidity, drawdown and waiting? |

Avoiding bankruptcy does not prevent stock losses. A promising company does not make a high-valuation drawdown acceptable at the current price.

## 7. Valuation: compare estimated value with the future required by price

**Minimum:** defensive/failure, base and successful operating paths, each specifying cash, capital, shares, duration, competition and terminal/discount assumptions. Maximum risk along the path differs from endpoint return.

**Reverse valuation versus independent scenarios:** use price, market capitalisation, debt and a suitable model to infer steady earnings, per-share cash, growth and returns needed to justify the price. This depends on parameters and **does not directly read actual market psychology**.

Compare independent scenarios with the implied requirements. Without sufficient independent evidence, or when all good news is priced, allow NO VERIFIED EDGE.

**An expectation gap does not imply an immediate rise:** corporate value realisation, market recognition and capital occupation have different timing. Examine opportunity cost, catalytic evidence, interest rates and possible multiple compression. Scenario probabilities without independent frequency grounds are not calibrated objective probabilities.

**Two distinguishable price-change sources:** changed estimated corporate intrinsic value and changed price deviation relative to valuation. Rates, funding, psychology and liquidity can change the latter and feed back into the former. Not every rise means the market finally recognised our correctness.

## 8. Market behaviour and strategy hypotheses: separate research and offline tests

Fundamentals concern mechanisms and long-run economics; market research concerns price, transactions, volatility, liquidity and participant constraints. They can inform each other or share one news source; agreement is not two independent validations.

The source considered relative strength, weekly/daily structures, transactions, volatility and liquidity. The public edition retains research variables but no moving-average parameters, breakout thresholds, trigger prices, exposure percentages or staged trades. Without fixed advance definitions and out-of-sample comparisons, these are not reliable trading laws.

| Research situation | Explanations to distinguish | Evidence for further research |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating scenario supported, market improving | New information, valuation, risk preference or liquidity | Availability timing, incremental operating evidence, costs and counterfactual baseline |
| Operating scenario supported, market weakening | Omission in the thesis or changed discount/market conditions | Disconfirmation, sensitivities, horizons and financing dependence |
| Weak operating evidence, strong market | Price change is not improved mechanism | Independent material distinguishing narrative from cash realisation |
| Neither operating nor market explanation supported | Inadequate model or unidentified question | Retain unknowns and assess further evidence value |

Offline candidate-strategy evaluation must predefine information timing, baseline, costs, fill assumptions, failure criteria and window. Parameters are objects to evaluate; this article does not select them for readers or convert research into securities actions.

Store operating hypotheses, price scenarios and strategy hypotheses separately. Supported business inference does not establish suitability of an operation; profitable simulation alone does not validate business explanations.

## 9. Prevent the method from generating its own biases

**Six failures need special attention:**

1. **Explanatory bias:** Generator/constraint language can retrospectively fit almost anything. Record observable forecasts and counterexamples first.
2. **Capability bias:** strong historical capacity need not stay scarce. Examine substitution, commoditisation and rival learning.
3. **Growth-space bias:** technically possible uses ≠ profitable demand ≠ attributable economic profit per share.
4. **Selection/survivorship bias:** include failures, ordinary firms and uneventful cases, not only attractive transformations.
5. **Frozen long-term views:** accumulation is not indefinite waiting. Record deadlines/failure thresholds and allow rejection of the main thesis.
6. **Human–AI confirmation bias:** AI helping build a framework may seek only fitting cases. Use an independent conventional baseline, allow important Unmapped Residue and test incremental prediction.

**Ethics and audit discipline:** separate facts, company statements, inference, hypotheses, unknowns and conflict. Do not force reality into CF terms. Cite dates and listed-shareholder attribution. Cross-domain analogy starts from target mechanisms, then verifies preservation; analogy is not empirical proof.

**Freeze in advance:** preserve date, information cut-off, horizon, critical propositions, price, baseline forecast, own forecast, Kill Test and conditional action when first proposing research. Append versions; old records are read-only. Distinguish improved frameworks from more accurate real judgements.

## 10. Test the method itself: from deduction to validation

**A relatively complete research logic does not establish excess-return ability.** Compare under identical securities, information availability and consistent risk assumptions:

- **Baseline A:** strong conventional company research—operations, competition, cash, valuation and ordinary risks.
- **Method B:** add constraint, Generator, transfer and Critical Evidence diagnostics to A.
- **Offline strategy comparison:** with the same candidates and information window, compare simulations without and with advance-fixed market variables. Results are not automatically live rules.

Use advance records to evaluate **operating-state prediction, speed of recognising severe disconfirmation, valuation error/capital occupation, and realised risk-adjusted returns net of costs**. Include failures and delistings; correctly handle disclosure timing, reporting lags, corporate-action adjustments, suspensions, slippage and taxes/fees. Use rolling out-of-sample tests. Small samples warrant preliminary observations, not statistical proof.

If complexity cannot reliably improve any dimension, remove concepts without incremental value rather than adding research layers.

## 11. Record company conclusions, resources and strategy assessments separately

| Conclusion layer | Question | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long-term company research | Which mechanisms are supported and which critical conditions unproved? | Multi-period support / reconstruction under validation / impaired mechanism / insufficient information, with criticality and support |
| Research resources | Is further verification worthwhile and which evidence has most value? | DROP / WATCH_DISCOVERY / DEEPEN, allocating effort only |
| Price/candidate strategy | Which price assumptions, costs and executability need tests? | Recorded / insufficient data / awaiting out-of-sample test / disconfirmed; no trades or position instructions |

Missing important L1 evidence may mean greater research uncertainty, not a ranking of security returns, company quality or purchase suitability. Research, information priorities and capital action should not collapse into a mysterious score.

## 12. A traceable human–AI research interface

For a company or industry question, the companion [Company Analysis Protocol](./company-analysis-protocol.md) organises material:

1. State objective, horizon, cut-off and initial three questions; retain unknowns.
2. Deepen only when mechanisms, cash, competition or governance require it.
3. Record one to three critical items, consequence levels L1/L2/L3, support and deadlines.
4. Keep company conclusions, priorities and untested price/strategy hypotheses separate.
5. Use versions and later facts to test improvement over conventional baselines.

This research interface does not authorise AI orders or provide reader suitability judgements or execution policies.

In brief: **study how operations generate cash value per share, identify missing decisive evidence, and revise through disconfirmation and advance comparisons.**
