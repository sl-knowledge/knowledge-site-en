> This account was assembled retrospectively with AI assistance. The original conversations are not public, and the narrative may be more coherent than the actual process. Article versions and the [site's revision history](https://github.com/sl-knowledge/knowledge-site-en) help trace changes to the public texts, but cannot fully reproduce the early discussions. The stages below are also a retrospective organisation. For the site's purpose and how to use it, see [What This Site Is For](./site-purpose.md).

The work on this site did not begin with a predesigned system or a survey of earlier literature. It began with questions about the universe, life and order, continued to absorb concrete problems from life and research, and gradually developed ways of studying them.

## Background: a philosophical starting point in 2025

In 2025, the author wrote philosophical reflections on the universe. The central conjecture was: **the universe moves towards the highest complexity at the fastest possible rate.** The original text formulated its central principle as selecting, among all possible paths, the path that maximises the growth rate of internal structural complexity (\(dC/dt\)). The interest was in how quickly complex structures form, as well as whether complexity increases.

The text focused on the process of increasing complexity and did not specify a definite upper bound on its final level. This was a philosophical conjecture, unvalidated by physics. Its questions about how order, life, cognition and civilisation arise became a starting point for later work.

## July 2026: starting with the universe, life and information

Discussions began with philosophical questions, such as whether the world's underlying nature always involves two opposing aspects. They then moved into physics, complex systems and information theory: Why do differences produce change? How does change produce structure? Why do some structures persist?

In discussing the emergence of life on Earth, the author pursued questions beyond the description “life is a dissipative structure”: Why do different environments form different structures even when energy dissipation occurs in each? What conditions on Earth support complex chemistry and the evolution of life?

## Late July–August: seeking common mechanisms of generation

The question broadened: Why do unorganised degrees of freedom form order, life, cognition, language and civilisation? Might these apparently different processes of emergence share mechanisms?

Attention gradually turned to the processes that form complex structures. A provisional sequence helped organise possible common mechanisms: “degrees of freedom → constraints → stable organisation → new control capabilities → new degrees of freedom.”

By mid-August, the question became more specific: **Can a system retain information from its past and use it to change the states it can reach in the future?** This gave the earlier questions about cosmic evolution more concrete objects of study and made the evidence required easier to specify. It was a refinement of the research questions. Whether analysis of local systems can support the original conjecture about the universe as a whole requires a separate argument.

## September: from explaining the world to building ways of studying it

One point became increasingly clear: the same phenomenon can have opposite implications under different environments, goals and time scales. Rising inventory may create a cash-flow risk for a company, or provide a strategic reserve against shortages. The focus shifted from “what a system possesses” towards “what it can actually do under current conditions.”

In late September, this line of thought produced an early version of [CF Grammar](./framework/methods/cf-grammar.md). It offers a scaffold for asking questions and organising candidate explanations across fields: specify the object, goal, environment and horizon, then examine supports, constraints, buffers and substitutes. Particular mechanisms still require domain models and evidence; the scaffold is not a validated universal causal standard.

An everyday example helped distinguish several points. Consider someone with back pain:

- Without a gardening goal, back pain cannot simply be called a gardening constraint: **a constraint is relative to a goal**.
- If digging is needed, the pain limits doing it personally, but help or powered tools may be available: **a blocked path does not make the goal unreachable**. Alternatives and buffers change the feasible space.
- Possessing a capability does not mean it can be called upon now.
- A solution that arrives too late is not feasible: **the time scale is a necessary condition**.

Some claims were narrowed or removed at this stage. An attempt to rank “dominant constraints” across fields lacked reliable criteria. The public framework still permits conditional diagnostic uses of the concept, but does not require a universal ranking of all constraints. “Pressure transfer” was moved out of the core grammar and treated as a dynamic pattern requiring a specific mechanism. The framework underwent selection, downgrading and removal; this does not mean all such concepts became unusable.

## Late September–October: studying abstraction itself

Two analytical tools were compared: one sought invariant structures across systems; the other sought differences that actually change future outcomes. Both compressed complex information, while preserving different things. This raised another question: On what basis should people and AI decide which differences must be retained and which can be ignored?

In early October, material previously scattered across topics was reorganised by logical dependency into five book drafts: how reality works, how to represent it, how to decide under constraints, how to apply the ideas to investment, and how people and AI can sustain joint research.

The same month brought an item-by-item originality self-check against existing sources for the main concepts, summarised in [What This Site Is For: earlier work](./site-purpose.md#prior-work). This was a process of finding sources and identifying differences, rather than a completed independent assessment of originality. These articles are under continuing review and revision. Errors and points deserving further discussion are welcome.

## Late October–November: examining the framework itself, and studying safety and control

Research began using the framework to examine itself: Could it reliably guide discovery, as well as plausibly explain known outcomes? In one small experiment, for example, the author explicitly required that a cause could not be supplied directly from common knowledge or filled in retrospectively after seeing the result.

Work also moved further into safety and control: who is responsible for protecting which constraint, what information judgement requires, how authority and time should be allocated, and whether simplified rules change subsequent recoverability. This work produced [Control Authority, Information and Time v0.2](./applications/viability-first-control.md) and [Simplification and Recovery Paths: Recoverability in Constrained Systems v0.2](./framework/foundations/simplicity-and-recovery.md). They organise abstract relationships, conditional arguments and mechanisms to be tested; they are not operating plans for specific systems.

## Looking back: how the questions became more specific

> **A conjecture about cosmic evolution maximising complexity growth → structure generation, hierarchical causation and control → constraints and reachability → a testable diagnostic language**

The broad questions about the universe were gradually divided into more specific research questions, with clearer conditions and evidence requirements. Some early claims were narrowed or revised. Meanwhile, analysis of constraints, representation and recovery paths began to be used in different fields.
