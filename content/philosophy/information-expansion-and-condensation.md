## Abstract

This essay uses a cycle of information expansion and condensation to consider how structures, life and cognition emerge in the universe. New possibilities produce differences; some condense into relatively stable structures. Those structures preserve earlier outcomes and may develop new generative capabilities, opening another round of possibilities. This philosophical perspective also runs through the present research direction: condensing dispersed knowledge into theories, applying them across domains, and generating new questions and knowledge.

## 1. The nature of the universe as information

What is the universe fundamentally made of?

We usually think of it as composed of matter. I lean towards a different philosophical understanding: **information may be a more fundamental reality, with matter as one of its manifestations under particular regularities.**

In this conception, information means more than recorded or communicated human knowledge, and more than a quantity in communication theory. It refers more broadly to existence, relationships, structures and possibilities for change.

Viewed this way, a deeper question arises:

**How does information evolve? Why do matter, life, cognition and such a diverse world emerge?**

This leads to a central idea in my thinking about cosmic evolution: information expansion and condensation.

## 2. Information expansion and condensation: two basic processes of cosmic evolution

I use two connected processes to consider the changes continually occurring in the universe.

**Information Expansion** means the exploration and realisation of new possibilities: the emergence of new differences, relationships, states and structures.

**Information Condensation** means that some of these changes form relatively stable organisations, preserving outcomes of prior evolution.

Expansion brings diversity; condensation forms structures.

More importantly, the two can support each other.

Expansion generates possibilities. Through interaction, selection and organisation, some condense into relatively stable structures.

Once formed, those structures change the conditions for subsequent evolution, making previously unavailable processes possible.

Cosmic evolution can thus be considered through a dynamic cycle:

**Information expansion → information condensation → stable structures → new generative capabilities → another round of information expansion.**

This is not simple repetition.

Each new round begins from structures formed by earlier condensation.

Past outcomes become conditions for future evolution.

This is the significance I attach to condensation: **it not only preserves the past but may also create the future.**

## 3. Recursive evolution: new levels generated from existing structures

Condensation does more than increase order within an existing system.

Its new structures may become components of systems at higher levels.

Nature offers examples of such organisation.

Elementary particles participate in forming atoms, atoms form molecules, and molecules participate in complex chemical organisations. Under suitable conditions, chemical systems may give rise to life.

Through heredity and evolution, life accumulates organisational capabilities, eventually giving rise to nervous systems, cognition, language and society.

Human societies further develop science, technology, institutions and civilisation.

The mechanisms differ across these levels. Yet they display a common feature worth studying:

**Stable structures formed at one level can become a basis for evolution at another.**

New levels often possess capabilities absent from individual components.

Living systems metabolise and reproduce; brains learn and predict; societies use language and knowledge to preserve information far beyond one person's experience.

Those capabilities make further evolution possible.

I therefore understand cosmic evolution as a potentially recursive process of forming new levels.

It may generate new structures and new generative rules.

New rules, in turn, allow further structures to emerge.

This is an open process in which the space of possibilities itself can change.

Not every structure persists, however, and not every condensation produces a higher level of organisation. Structures can disintegrate, evolution can stall and formerly effective rules can fail in changed environments.

Formation, maintenance, change and dissolution together constitute the richness of cosmic evolution.

## 4. From preserving states to preserving generative rules

One distinction has become increasingly important in this reflection:

**Preserving a complete state and preserving rules capable of generating it are different things.**

Reproduction, for example, does not begin by making a complete copy of a mature organism's entire structure.

Genetic information interacts with cellular mechanisms and external conditions to generate an organism through development.

Likewise, scientific theories do not preserve every observed phenomenon unchanged.

Scientists seek common regularities across observations, then use them to explain the past and anticipate the future.

Newton's laws of motion exemplify this kind of knowledge condensation.

A compact theory can organise dispersed experience and generate previously unavailable understanding.

A particularly valuable condensation therefore does more than compress information: it forms **rules capable of generating further information**.

Such rules make it unnecessary to begin from nothing each time.

Knowledge can be inherited, recombined and used to create new knowledge.

This also connects with the later focus of my research on generative mechanisms, or Generator.

The interest is not only in the world's present states, but in how those states arise and which mechanisms shape their possible changes.

## 5. Life and cognition: parts of the universe come to understand it

Within this philosophical perspective, the emergence of life and consciousness has a particular significance.

Over its history, the universe has produced living systems capable of preserving information, sensing environments, learning from experience and changing their behaviour.

Human beings subsequently developed language, abstraction, mathematics, science and technology.

These capabilities enable inquiry into surrounding environments and into the origin, structure and operation of the universe itself.

This is a striking relationship:

**Cosmic evolution produces living systems capable of understanding the universe; those systems then investigate cosmic evolution.**

We are not observers standing outside the universe.

We are part of it.

Thoughts, scientific theories and acts of understanding are also information structures and processes appearing within its history.

Research, theory-building and technology participate in the world's subsequent development as well as observing it.

The point is that cosmic evolution has produced systems capable of knowing, understanding and reflecting on their existence. It does not require imagining the universe as a whole to possess human-like consciousness.

**A part of the universe begins to understand the universe itself.**

That understanding may, in turn, influence the next stage of development.

## 6. Human civilisation: a new stage of knowledge condensation and expansion

Civilisation can also be viewed through information expansion and condensation.

Life, exploration, practice and scientific inquiry continually produce knowledge.

Different people, societies and disciplines accumulate experience from different perspectives.

Language, writing, education, mathematics and scientific theories condense those dispersed experiences into knowledge that can be preserved and transmitted.

That knowledge need not disappear when an individual's life ends.

Later generations can continue inquiry from earlier achievements.

Knowledge development thus has a recursive structure:

**Exploration produces knowledge; knowledge condenses into theory; theory makes further exploration possible.**

AI introduces new conditions for this process.

It can help retrieve, connect and reorganise knowledge previously dispersed across domains.

The important question is not simply whether more information becomes accessible.

It is whether shared structures can be found that improve explanation and understanding and support transfer across fields.

If complex knowledge can be condensed into effective generative rules and those rules help understanding in new domains, knowledge may become a powerful cognitive lever.

Limited human cognitive capabilities may thereby be extended.

This is the research direction currently being explored: using AI to develop methods for more effective questioning, understanding, learning and inquiry.

From this philosophical perspective, it is another expression of expansion and condensation in contemporary cognition.

## 7. An evolving philosophical conception of the universe

Return to the initial questions: what is the nature of the universe, and why do structures and changes continually emerge?

I continue to approach these questions through information and its evolution.

In this conception, the universe is a dynamic process of change, relationship formation and structure generation, rather than a static collection of fixed things.

Information expansion brings new possibilities.

Condensation forms relatively stable structures.

Stable structures may preserve past evolutionary outcomes and generate new capabilities.

New capabilities may open wider spaces for exploration, producing further information and levels of organisation.

The process may recur at different scales as hierarchical, recursive evolution.

It includes structural dissolution, information loss and failed development. Generation and disintegration, stability and change, exploration and selection all deserve attention in understanding a complex world.

These ideas first took shape in philosophical reflections on the universe in 2025. Those reflections also proposed more ambitious possibilities concerning cosmic cycles and the fastest information condensation.

The present aim is to retain the central philosophical perspective while drawing on complex systems, information theory, life sciences, cognitive science and AI to determine which ideas can develop into more precise mechanisms and testable theories.

For me, this is a continuing philosophical inquiry as well as a research endeavour.

Within the conception itself, thinking and research also involve exploration, condensation and renewed generation of information.

What is being studied is also a process in which the research participates.

**The world generates new possibilities; some condense into structures; those structures become a basis for creating further possibilities.**

This is a central thread running through the philosophical conception.

It is also a direction for continued inquiry.

## 8. Related ideas and reading

Different approaches already examine the universe through information and computation. John Archibald Wheeler's *It from Bit*, Seth Lloyd's account of cosmic computation and Stephen Wolfram's work on simple programs and complex behaviour offer comparisons. They are not equivalent to the expansion–condensation conception here, nor do they establish information as the universe's sole reality.

1. Wheeler, J. A. [*Information, Physics, Quantum: The Search for Links*](https://cqi.inf.usi.ch/qic/wheeler.pdf): a discussion of information, quantum theory and physical reality.
2. Lloyd, S. [*Programming the Universe*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/102977/programming-the-universe-by-seth-lloyd/): a perspective on the universe through computation and information processing.
3. Wolfram, S. (2002). [*A New Kind of Science*](https://www.wolframscience.com/nks/): simple programs, complex behaviour and natural phenomena.

For connections with subsequent research, see [From Reflections on the Universe to Later Research](../cosmic-reflections-and-later-research.md).
