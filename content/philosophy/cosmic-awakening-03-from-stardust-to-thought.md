## 6. Within one material universe: from stars to life

Sections 4 and 5 concerned relations between cycles. We now return to the material universe in which we live. How did structures grow level by level from the simplest states?

The same principle is used throughout: **within one material universe, generative rules apply universally and create statistical tendencies towards some outcomes; what actually appears depends on local parameters and the history that has already occurred.** “Universal” means that the same regularities operate throughout this material universe. “Tendency” means that under given parameters some structures are more likely than others to appear or persist, not that the rules “want” them to appear.

The principle is applied below to three cases: generations of stars, the origin of life, and the path from learning to thought. The appearance of humans is left to Section 7.

### Generations of stars: a spiral within the material universe

The clearest condensation and expansion in our material universe occurs between generations of stars.

Soon after the Big Bang, matter was distributed almost uniformly and consisted mainly of hydrogen and helium, with a very small amount of lithium. Under gravity, slightly denser regions attracted more matter. Gas gathered and formed the earliest stars and galaxies. This was **condensation**.

Nuclear fusion inside stars combined light elements step by step into heavier ones such as carbon and oxygen, up to iron. This, too, was condensation: dispersed matter compressed into more complex nuclei.

At the end of their lives, massive stars exploded and scattered newly synthesised elements into space. Elements heavier than iron, such as gold and uranium, are produced mainly in processes including neutron-star mergers. This was **expansion**: condensed results spread across a wider region and mixed with interstellar gas.

Later generations of stars and planets formed from matter that already contained heavy elements. This was **new condensation**: new structures began from conditions left by earlier generations. Astronomers classify stars into “populations” by their heavy-element content. Stars formed later usually contain more heavy elements. The theoretically earliest stars contained almost only hydrogen and helium and have not yet been directly confirmed by observation.

Our Sun and Earth formed after many such generations. Most of the carbon, oxygen and iron in rocks, oceans and our bodies came from earlier stars. [NASA on the stellar life cycle](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/star-lifecycle/) · [Burbidge, Burbidge, Fowler and Hoyle (1957)](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.29.547)

Two points matter. First, this is an observable spiral within a material universe: each generation starts from conditions left by the previous one. Second, gravity and nuclear reactions drive condensation here, not purpose. Condensed structures remain only because they are sufficiently stable under the conditions of the time. In the terms used above, gravitational and nuclear laws apply universally and tend to produce stars and heavy elements; exactly where and in what order they form depends on local density, composition and history.

### The origin of life

Before life appeared on such planets, rocks and minerals and changing chemical environments already existed. They formed conditions under which life could later emerge.

How did the earliest life arise? Energy sources, chemical composition, temperature, liquid water and available time are all important local parameters. Under suitable parameters, chemical systems capable of self-replication and heritable variation may be more likely to form, but that does not make life's origin inevitable. The sequence experienced by the early Earth remains under investigation. From Earth as our single known example, it is difficult to estimate how common such parameters are in the universe and therefore whether life is common or rare. [Research on inference from life's early emergence on Earth](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1111694108)

### From learning to thought

Once life appears, experience can condense at several levels, forming a ladder:

1. **Heredity.** Some traits pass to descendants through reproduction. Experience condenses in genes and changes across generations.
2. **Learning.** Along some of Earth's evolutionary lines, organisms developed perception, memory and learning. Past experience changes what they do the next time they meet a similar situation. Condensation can now occur within one lifetime rather than waiting for genetic change across generations.
3. **Thought.** Some organisms form internal models of the external world, simulate without acting, and use the result to guide action.
4. **Reflection.** At another level, they form models of their own models: they think about their thinking.
5. **Knowledge.** Thought is fixed in carriers outside the body—language, writing and tools—and can persist beyond the individual.

**Thought**, as used here, begins at the third level: **a kind of structure in the material universe that forms models of other structures, uses them to simulate and predict without first acting, and lets the result guide action.** Two features matter. The first is representation: thought carries information about something else and can be wrong. A thermometer merely covaries with temperature; a judgement about tomorrow's weather can be false. Its ability to be false shows that it represents rather than merely reacts. The second is offline simulation: the system can try something internally before trying it in reality.

Two easily confused words must be separated. **Knowledge** is the external form of thought and may outlast the individual. **Consciousness** means subjective experience—what it is like to be the system. The definition above concerns only the function of thought, not consciousness. This essay does not explain why subjective experience exists. By the functional definition, some current artificial-intelligence systems contain part of the structure of thought. Whether they have experience is a different question.

This ladder resembles earlier accounts. Kenneth Craik proposed in 1943 that the mind builds “small-scale models” of reality and uses them to predict. Daniel Dennett described a “tower of generate-and-test”: from genetic trial and error, to learning during life, to trying possibilities first in an internal model, and then using language and tools to draw on other people's experience. From the perspective of condensation, each level makes condensation faster and less dependent on paying the cost of real-world trials.

## 7. The appearance of humans and the meaning of existence

Among the many forms of life on Earth capable of learning, one evolutionary line reached thought, reflection and knowledge: humans.

### Was the appearance of humans inevitable or contingent?

Stephen Jay Gould imagined rewinding the tape of life and playing it again: humans would probably not reappear. Simon Conway Morris places greater weight on convergent evolution, noting that different lineages sometimes independently develop similar structures. This disagreement separates two questions. Under similar conditions, will a class of structures recur? And specifically for humans, how much of Earth's path could be repeated? [Conway Morris, *Life's Solution*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/lifes-solution/82BFF629ABD29A44F1B428D03C094102)

Both positions fit the earlier principle. Tendencies in the rules explain why similar conditions can produce convergence; parameters and prior history explain why a particular path remains contingent. Under some conditions, disturbance-resistant structures are more likely to remain. When the environment changes, a formerly stable structure can lose the conditions that sustained it. **Humans are one particular path—a phenotype—produced by Earth's parameters and history.**

With humans, the carriers of condensation expanded from genes to language, writing and tools. Experience could pass between individuals and generations and the pace of condensation accelerated. Genetic change takes millions of years, cultural change generations, and some knowledge now changes within one lifetime. At the current stage, the most complex and resilient structures in the known universe continue to extend. Humans are the present leading edge of that extension on Earth, **but neither its endpoint nor its purpose.** Along the path of human knowledge we can observe how what remained from the past changes the range that later people can encounter and explore.

### Several senses of meaning

Once humans appeared, asking about meaning became possible. People ask about the origin and structure of the world and turn the question back towards themselves, considering how to understand a finite life. From rocks to sentient life to people who can ask these questions, “meaning” has several senses.

1. **An assigned purpose.** The universe has no predetermined purpose. People can set purposes in their own lives.
2. **A place in evolution.** Rocks and minerals form part of the environment of living things; plants turn sunlight into energy usable by organisms. In this sense everything has a place. Many structures formed before us became conditions for later life.
3. **Good and bad as experienced.** What contact with the world means for a sentient being must be understood from its experience. Even if animals cannot say the word meaning, pain and pleasure are real for them.
4. **Asking about and creating meaning.** People organise experience through language, look back over their lives, imagine the future and know that they will die. They therefore ask what they value and how they want to live. We ordinarily ask what things are for, extend that question to natural objects, and finally ask it of ourselves. [Kelemen on children's teleological explanations](https://doi.org/10.1037/0012-1649.35.6.1440)

In summary:

> **Everything has a place in evolution; sentient life also experiences good and bad; and humanity is the part of the universe that has begun to ask about and create meaning.**

We are not observers outside the universe. Our thoughts, feelings, science, art and creations also belong to its unfolding history.

### Life and death, and cosmic awakening

The cycle of life and death can be expressed in one sentence:

> **Life moves towards death; death makes life whole.**

Life is the process of writing a unique chapter that will eventually be complete. Death completes that particular process and opens space and conditions for later life. Although a life ends, its effects may remain in other lives, relationships, knowledge, works and civilisations and become parts of later structures.

The meaning of life is therefore not only to resist its end, but to make its finite process as rich, deep and creative as possible. Our small cycles of life and death participate in the world's longer generation and transformation.

“Cosmic awakening” need not mean that the universe as a whole has a consciousness resembling ours. It can mean this:

> **The universe evolved life capable of knowing, understanding and reflecting on the universe. A part of the universe began to know the universe itself.**

Carl Sagan expressed a related thought in *Cosmos*. [The Carl Sagan archive at the Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/static/collections/finding-our-place-in-the-cosmos-with-carl-sagan/about-this-collection/overview.html)

From the position of Section 1, this sentence has another depth. “Universe” in the title means the information field as a whole. Matter is a stable pattern in information, and thought is a pattern capable of representing other patterns (Section 6). When humans discover mathematics, logic and physical laws, a structure at the shallowest level—the material universe—re-condenses the two deepest levels within itself. Information recognises information; manifestation recognises the source of manifestation. What awakens is therefore not merely one species in a material universe. Through one of its manifestations, the information field begins to know itself:

> **The information field manifests as the world, and the world in turn recognises the information field.**

No purpose is required. Manifestation did not occur in order to be known; one manifestation simply left structures capable of knowing it. Because mathematics and logic remain the same in every manifestation, knowledge in every cycle encounters the same Gödel boundary (Section 4). The information field continues, manifestation follows manifestation, and knowing likewise continues without an endpoint.

### Knowing and changing: the coupling of material and thought structures

“A part of the universe began to know the universe itself” can be made more concrete. A structure appeared within the material universe that contains models of the material universe, including its generative rules. Human brains and human knowledge contain physical laws, cosmic history and accounts of star formation. Part of the universe has condensed the universe's generative rules. This is the deepest level of condensation: what is condensed is no longer only phenomena, but rules that generate phenomena.

At the level of reflection described in Section 6, a system represents itself and self-reference appears—the site of Gödelian incompleteness. In Section 4 the Gödel spiral was still an analogy about cosmic laws. In beings able to know themselves, it appears for the first time inside a concrete system. A knower can never know itself completely, so knowing can never be complete. This claim has theorem-level support; see “From distinction to incompleteness” at the end of Section 4. Awakening is therefore not a completed state but a process without an end.

Knowledge is not detached from matter. Within a material universe every thought is a state of a brain, and every item of knowledge is stored in a material carrier—a neuron, a page or a hard drive. Under the definition in Section 6, thought is not another substance outside matter but a special material structure: deeply organised and capable of representing other structures. This is consistent with Section 1: matter consists of stable patterns in information, and thought is a pattern that can represent other patterns.

Matter and thought are coupled in both directions. Matter constrains thought. The human brain is about two per cent of body mass but consumes about twenty per cent of the body's energy. Landauer's principle in physics says that erasing one bit of information must release at least a determinate amount of heat: information processing is never free. Destroy the carrier and the thought disappears unless it has already been transferred elsewhere. This is one reason the “living seed” matters.

Thought in turn changes matter, with enormous leverage. It can act only through bodies and tools, but a small pattern can direct a great flow of matter: a blueprint determines how much steel a bridge uses; a genome determines how a tree organises tens of tonnes of matter. Information acts as a constraint that guides flows of matter and energy. One estimate found that around 2020 the mass of human-made materials—concrete, metals, plastics and buildings—surpassed the mass of all living biomass on Earth. This is consistent with the Hierarchical Causal–Control hypothesis proposed by the author: higher-level information structures control lower-level physical processes through constraints.

At the scale of the material universe, this influence remains tiny in absolute terms, but it has leverage and is growing. At the same time a transition is visible. The cosmic star-formation rate peaked roughly ten billion years ago and has since declined to about one tenth of its maximum. Cosmic expansion also changed: it began accelerating about six billion years ago, and from roughly four billion years ago dark energy exceeded matter in the cosmic energy budget; it now accounts for about seventy per cent. At the largest scale, gravitational aggregation is yielding to accelerated expansion: the material universe is moving from condensation towards expansion at this scale. Physical condensation is slowing while the condensation of knowledge accelerates. The 2025 version distinguished two kinds of complexity: manifest physical complexity can rise and fall, while the depth left by history can continue to grow. The leading edge of evolution is moving from physical structures to structures of knowledge. No one arranged this; the new carrier simply condenses faster.

Accelerated expansion does not prove that the universe will expand forever. That depends on whether dark energy is truly constant. In 2025, baryon acoustic oscillation measurements in DESI's second data release remained compatible on their own with the standard cosmological model; when combined with cosmic-microwave-background and supernova data, several dataset combinations preferred time-varying dark energy. This is a hint, not direct proof that dark energy is weakening. In one axion dark-energy model, using best-fit parameters, the authors estimate a total cosmic lifetime of about thirty-three billion years before expansion stops and collapse begins. This is model-dependent, not a direct conclusion of DESI. The framework here does not depend on how this cycle ends (Section 5), but it does imply one point: contraction would not be condensation. Stars can drive planets, life and thought because energy flows from hot stars into a colder environment. Expansion cools the background radiation and sustains a large temperature gradient. During prolonged contraction the background would warm, and the usable gradient would eventually shrink and disappear. Collapse would erase distinctions rather than condense them. In the language of this essay, expansion is a condition of condensation. Condensation without expansion does not produce deeper condensation; it stops condensation. The universe would not contract “in order to condense.” If contraction occurs, condensation would first slow and then stop.

Why do new carriers appear? Condensation reaches the capacity limits of its carrier. Individual memory cannot hold enough, so writing appears. Hand copying is slow and error-prone, so printing appears. No person can read everything, so disciplines and scientific communities appear. Calculation exceeds human capacity, so computers appear. No person can read and connect knowledge across all fields, so artificial intelligence appears. A bottleneck is often broken not by exerting more force inside the old carrier, but by changing carriers. This is the epistemic form of Section 4's claim that when adjustment within existing rules stops producing novelty, change is more likely to come from changing the rules. Each new carrier does not eliminate the bottleneck; it moves it to the next tightest constraint.

Even before artificial intelligence, condensation using only humans as carriers showed signs of a bottleneck. Economist Benjamin Jones found that as knowledge accumulates, researchers take longer to reach the frontier, the age of first major invention rises, and teams grow larger. Bloom and colleagues found that maintaining the same growth in research output requires increasing numbers of researchers. Under the mechanism proposed here, once AI relaxes the “too much to read and connect” bottleneck, the tightest constraints will move to contact with reality (experiments and field tests), verification of truth and falsity, asking good questions and judging what matters, and eventually to the physical limits of energy and computation.

This also answers a question left by Section 5. The “richness” of a material universe is neither physical structure alone nor thought alone but **the depth of its whole history**. Bennett's “logical depth” is a useful concept: the complexity of a structure depends on the length of the incompressible process required to generate it from the simplest starting point. A hot gas contains vast numbers of molecular collisions but has little logical depth. A living being, a book or a theory is logically deep. Thought structures are among the deepest material structures we know, and they continually generate other deep material structures. If the conjecture in Section 5 is correct and the starting point left at the end of a cosmic cycle depends on the depth of its history, then the appearance of knowers affects the starting point of the next cycle. This does not mean that the universe produced us for a purpose. Knowers appeared contingently; once present, they became the deepest part of this cycle's history.

The leverage itself is neither good nor evil. It can build and destroy. The discussion of protection in Section 8 therefore acquires a deeper reason.

The material universe is the form of manifestation we know. We cannot know whether it is the only form available to the information field.

“A part of the universe begins to know the universe itself” thus has two meanings. It knows the universe, and on the basis of that knowledge it can reorganise part of the universe. **Knowing and changing are two sides of the same coupling.**
