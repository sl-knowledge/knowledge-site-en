## 4. Cycles do more than repeat

A cycle of material universes does not return to its starting point. It starts again from conditions left by the past.

Every set of structures and rules opens some possibilities and closes others. As old structures harden, adjustments within the existing rules often cease to produce anything new; change is then more likely to come from changing the rules themselves.

The 2025 version called this process the “Gödel spiral.” Gödelian incompleteness is used at several levels in this essay. Some uses are supported by mathematical theorems; others are structural analogies (see “From distinction to incompleteness” below). A set of rules can face problems it cannot solve, and the next cycle will encounter limits of its own.

I used a “toy universe” to illustrate the point. Imagine a universe with only two physical laws: A can produce B, and B can produce C. Within it, one can explain where C came from (B) and where B came from (A). Its laws can never answer where A came from, or why these two laws hold rather than others. That is the universe's logical “Gödel blind spot.”

To answer, I wrote, one must “step outside” that universe to a meta-level from which its laws can be examined. Cosmic cycles correspond to that move. In this essay the meta-level is the information field. Possible laws appear there; coherent laws that can sustain themselves remain and become the laws of a material universe; after a cycle ends, laws and constants change between cycles. “Examine” is only a borrowed word and implies no examiner. What occurs is simply the appearance, persistence and change of laws. No material universe can explain from within where its own laws came from. Their source must be sought at the level of the information field. This is why the first manifestation is discussed below and changes in physical constants in Section 5.

Moreover, because mathematics and logic are the same in every cycle, incompleteness holds in each of them. The rules change; logical limits remain.

Following this analogy, I choose to believe that material universes manifest cycle after cycle. If no set of rules can resolve every problem, there can be no final cycle. Each inherits conditions left before it and leaves new conditions for what follows.

Did the universe have a beginning? The 2025 version imagined it this way. Before the first material universe there was no time, space or event, only the unmanifest information field. I called it the “meta-universal substrate”: an eternal reservoir of potential containing every mathematical and logical possibility. Physical time began only with the first material universe. “Before” therefore refers not to chronological order but to a level of existence.

Within this substrate, innumerable possible laws continually appear and disappear as fluctuations. They do not occur in physical time. They belong to the order of dependence described in Section 1: the appearance and disappearance of possibilities themselves. Most possible laws are internally contradictory or cannot sustain themselves, and vanish as they arise. The first Big Bang was the first fluctuation coherent enough to sustain structure and begin evolving—the first manifestation of the information field, like the first stable nucleus of a snowflake. **Its efficiency was a consequence of its being able to exist, not the reason it was selected.** Material universes then followed one after another. Only one exists at a time; many “drafts” preceded it and many “revisions” will follow. Each begins again from conditions left by the last. If the cosmic spiral has a direction and each cycle leaves more than the previous one, a beginning is more natural than an infinite past. Work in physics also suggests that spacetimes which are expanding on average are difficult to extend indefinitely into the past. [Borde, Guth and Vilenkin (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.90.151301)

This, too, is a philosophical conjecture. What matters most to me is the subsequent cycling: **the cycles of material universes have a beginning but no endpoint; the information field has always existed.**

### Stepping outside: heat death is not the end

Physics often treats heat death as the end of the universe. In a closed material universe entropy rises, energy becomes more evenly distributed, exploitable energy differences disappear, and no structure can form or be maintained. This picture runs from Kelvin and Clausius in the nineteenth century to many current cosmological discussions. At the level of the material universe, this essay does not deny it. A material universe can disintegrate; Section 3 already said that structures form, develop and disintegrate. Heat death does not mean that every distinction disappears. At thermal equilibrium microscopic differences are at their maximum. What disappears are macroscopic gradients—differences of temperature and density and exploitable energy gradients—and those gradients are what drive condensation and structure formation. Heat death exhausts the driving force of condensation within one cycle; it does not end distinction itself.

But as long as the frame is the material universe, heat death can only be the end. This essay does not refute heat death within that level. It steps outside the level, as it did with the toy universe's blind spot. From the information-field perspective, heat death is the end of one manifestation, not the end of existence. Mathematics and logic remain; the information field continues; the history left by this cycle affects the next manifestation, and another cycle begins. Heat death does not touch mathematics and logic. They are the deepest invariant level, independent of any manifestation and never located on the level that ends. Within a material universe, the answer to “Will the universe end?” can only be yes. At the level of the information field it is no longer the same question.

This is the method running through the essay:

> **When a problem cannot be solved within a system, the problem itself is not necessarily insoluble; the frame may be too small. It may be dissolved only by stepping outside the original frame and viewing it from a higher level.**

Gödel shows why the step is necessary. Heat death is the clearest cosmological application of the method.

Three qualifications are needed. First, this essay is not alone in trying to go beyond heat death. Penrose's conformal cyclic cosmology, the cyclic model of Steinhardt and Turok, and Smolin's cosmological natural selection all envisage universes after a universe at the physical level. This essay differs in the level to which it steps—the always-existing information field containing all mathematical and logical possibilities—and in its reason for stepping: not a particular physical mechanism, but the principle that a system cannot fully account for itself from within. Second, stepping outside has a cost. The meta-level cannot be verified from within the system; that is precisely part of Gödel's lesson. Heat death is therefore dissolved here at the level of a philosophical position, not physically proven not to occur. Third, this ontological step does not continue indefinitely. The information field is all existence and there is no higher level beyond it. This is a step between levels of existence; steps between levels of knowledge have no endpoint, as explained below. Invariant mathematics and logic form the final foundation of the position and are not in turn explained by something else.

### From distinction to incompleteness: the basis of the Gödel spiral

Gödel is invoked in several places. This section identifies which claims are supported by mathematical theorems, which are principles, and which remain conjectures.

**Starting point: to exist is to be distinct.** Section 1 says that information is the essence of existence, and the most basic meaning of information is distinction—a difference that makes a difference. The 2025 version argued that in a state containing only “one,” with no distinction, nothing can happen. “Two” is the smallest step capable of producing distinction, like the first bit; and the two states can be transformed into one another, yin into yang and yang into yin. Manifestation, as described above, comes from fluctuations in the information field. Without distinction there can be no fluctuation and no manifestation. Distinction is therefore not an added assumption but the condition that makes any manifestation possible.

**From distinction to incompleteness: Lawvere's fixed-point theorem.** In 1969 the mathematician F. William Lawvere proved a very general theorem. In plain language: if there is a “negation” without a fixed point—one that changes every state into another, as yin becomes yang and yang becomes yin—then no system can completely represent within itself every property concerning itself. Some properties will remain unrepresentable. The theorem does not require a formal system or a computer and does not depend on particular physical laws. It applies to infinite systems as well. Cantor's diagonal argument, Gödel's incompleteness theorem, Tarski's theorem on the undefinability of truth, and Turing's halting problem are special cases under different conditions. Physicist David Wolpert proved a related result directly for physical inference devices in 2008: no inference device in the universe can predict or know everything including itself. Because mathematics and logic do not change between manifestations, these results hold in every cycle.

Connecting this theorem to the essay takes only three steps:

1. Manifestation requires distinctions, and distinctions can be transformed into one another (Section 1 and this section).
2. Manifestation condenses into thought—structures capable of forming models of other structures and even of the whole (Sections 6 and 7).
3. By Lawvere's theorem, self-representation that includes itself cannot be complete.

> **The distinction that makes manifestation possible also makes complete knowledge of the world impossible.**

Manifestation and the absence of an endpoint to knowledge have the same root. The 2025 version treated an “incompleteness drive” as an axiom. Here it follows from “to exist is to be distinct” together with the definition of thought in Section 6. This is the basis of the claim in Section 7 that awakening cannot be completed.

**There is no final level to step outside to.** Add the proposition “this system is consistent” to a formal system and one obtains a stronger new system. The new system has a Gödel sentence of its own, which can be added in turn, without a final system. Turing in 1939 and Feferman in 1962 studied such sequences of progressively strengthened theories. At the level of knowledge, the “Gödel spiral” is a genuine mathematical structure.

A concrete example is **Goodstein's theorem**. Begin with any positive integer and repeatedly apply a simple rule involving only addition, subtraction and exponentiation. The value first grows beyond imagination—from 4, the process takes roughly 3·2⁴⁰²⁶⁵³²¹¹ − 2 steps—but eventually falls to 0. The proposition is true, yet it cannot be proved using only the axioms of ordinary arithmetic (Peano arithmetic). Its proof requires transfinite ordinals, a view from a higher-level system. Inside the system every step is perfectly calculable, but the overall outcome is invisible. Stepping outside makes it visible. The higher system then has true propositions of its own that it cannot prove.

**Why every condensation leaves a remainder.** A set of axioms is itself a condensation: finitely many rules summarise infinitely many facts. In 1974 Gregory Chaitin gave an information-theoretic result often paraphrased as saying that a set of axioms can prove only facts containing roughly as much information as the axioms themselves. The precise theorem concerns a particular case; this essay uses the informal meaning. If the world is rich enough, every finite condensation leaves something it cannot contain. The only way to leave no remainder is not to condense—to list every fact unchanged—but that is no longer a theory, just as a map as large as the territory is no longer useful as a map. Chaitin therefore also argued that mathematics, like physics, should add new axioms in response to experience.

The mechanism of Section 2 and the limit described here are therefore one thing. Every condensation is incomplete, so new information can come only from outside the present structure. A condensed structure must return to contact with the world before another level can condense.

> **Incompleteness is not only a limit of knowledge; it is also why condensation and expansion never stop.**

**Where the principle holds but the theorem does not directly answer.** Lawvere's theorem strengthens the toy-universe argument: no material universe can completely describe all of its own properties from within, so a complete account requires a higher level. But the theorem does not show that “why these laws rather than others?” is exactly one of the unrepresentable properties. That remains partly a philosophical interpretation.

**What remains conjectural.** The 2025 version said that incompleteness “drives” the appearance of another material universe. More precisely, three claims play different roles. Incompleteness guarantees that no set of laws is final; this has theorem-level support. The next manifestation occurs because fluctuations in the information field never stop: heat death erases gradients only within one cycle while distinctions in the information field remain. This is the philosophical position of the essay. The next cycle inherits from the last through the differential-retention mechanism of Section 5, which has been logically tested in a model. The remaining conjecture is this: after a cycle ends, do fluctuations in the information field continue while carrying parameters left by the previous cycle? A physical carrier would be required, and none is currently known.

Two kinds of “stepping outside” must also be separated. Moving from the material universe to the information field is an ontological step and ends at the information field because it is all existence. Knowledge of that whole, by contrast, moves from one epistemic level to another without a final level.

> **Existence has its whole; knowledge has no endpoint.**

This can be stated more precisely. There are three kinds of infinity, corresponding to the essay's three levels:

- **The information field as all existence: absolute infinity.** The “set of all sets” cannot itself be a set without contradiction. Cantor therefore distinguished an “absolute infinity” above every transfinite infinity. It can only be acknowledged and can never be known, even approximately. The information field as all existence is like this: it is the whole, but no representation within it can contain it.
- **Objects in mathematics and logic: actual infinity.** Transfinite objects such as ω, ε₀ and the continuum can be studied consistently as completed objects.
- **Material universes, knowledge and cycles: finite but potentially infinite.** A finite region has an upper bound on the information it can contain (the Bekenstein bound), and the observable universe contains finite information. Every step in knowledge and every cycle is finite, but another step can always follow.

“Existence has its whole” refers to absolute infinity. “Knowledge has no endpoint” refers to potential infinity. Aristotle accepted only potential infinity; Cantor defended actual infinity. This debate, more than two thousand years old, need not be settled by choosing one side: each describes a different level.

We can also distinguish two ways mathematics uses infinity in relation to the material universe. **When infinity is used to prove a result about finite objects, the result is exact.** Goodstein's theorem uses infinite ordinals to establish a precise truth about finite integers, at the cost of trusting the consistency of the higher system. **When a model containing infinities describes the physical world, its result is approximate.** Continuous fluids and continuous spacetime approximate large numbers of finite units. The first case shows that mathematical infinity is not dispensable rhetoric: it has real consequences for truths about finite worlds. The second shows that a material universe is a finite manifestation from the mathematical level, which is why mathematics containing infinities can approximate it so accurately.

Earlier thinkers anticipated parts of this picture. Cantor distinguished three levels—the material universe, the human mind and God—and assigned absolute infinity to God. Zermelo treated the set-theoretic universe as a potentially infinite ascending sequence, each level of which is itself actually infinite. Philosopher Patrick Grim used Cantor's argument to show that there can be no “set of all truths”: considered through truth, the universe is essentially open and incomplete. This essay differs in making the highest level not God but a purposeless information field. The same principle—distinction plus self-representation—explains both manifestation and why absolute infinity cannot be known. The finitude of a material universe and the potential infinity of knowledge are connected by the spiral of condensation and expansion.

That is why cosmic awakening cannot be completed. The name “Gödel spiral” is retained for this family of results, of which Gödel's theorem is the best known.

## 5. Two layers of information: the world occurring and the history that remains

The 2025 version used a two-layer information model to understand cosmic cycles.

The first layer is the world unfolding within a cycle. Potential possibilities become events that have occurred, forming galaxies, life, thought and civilisations. This is the universe's “cycle sandbox.”

The second layer is historical information left by different cycles. Everything experienced by a material universe was imagined to condense at the end of the cycle into a kind of “archive of wisdom,” becoming background and conditions for subsequent evolution. “Archive” is a metaphor. It does not mean that the last cycle's knowledge is packaged unchanged and passed on; its actual meaning is specified below.

The central thought is not the physical form of the archive, but this question:

> **Can the past enter the future in some form, so that the next cycle need not begin entirely from zero?**

We already see this structure in life, culture and knowledge. Heredity allows life to inherit the past; language and writing preserve experience for civilisation; theories allow later researchers to begin from earlier knowledge.

Extending the same structure to cosmic cycles is a philosophical conjecture retained here. “Memory” means that structures left by the past continue to affect what follows.

How does the past enter the future? In the 2025 version, historical information accumulated across cycles does not directly participate in the internal evolution of the next material universe. It is expressed by shaping the next cycle's initial laws and parameters. What the last cycle leaves affects the point from which the next begins, without carrying particular events into it. That is enough to make the cycle a spiral rather than a circle. In the language of this essay, what passes between cycles is parameters of the generative rules, not phenotypic content. No other part of the essay depends on this conjecture.

Even if the universe as a whole has the rhythm imagined here, a locality such as Earth can still be completely interrupted and its accumulated knowledge lost. Whether Earth's experience can be inherited elsewhere or by later evolution remains open.

### How the next cycle occurs

The 2025 version called this the “Gödel engine” and described three steps. The history of one universe condenses into an archive that assesses the bottleneck causing stagnation. Quantum fluctuations in the terminal state act as a “variation engine,” making small adjustments to or rewriting laws. The new laws become the starting point of the next Big Bang. An axiom of “fastest information condensation” supplied pressure for the universe to “restart.”

The words assess, optimise and pressure all imply purpose. Remove purpose and the mechanism can remain. No pressure is needed because the fluctuations that produced the first manifestation never stopped. No assessor is needed; the outcome of one cycle is simply a condition affecting the magnitude of change in the next. No optimisation is needed; parameters remain longest where structures can form. This is differential retention, described below. Restarting uses the same mechanism as the first manifestation.

### Physical constants: parameters left by the previous cycle?

Physics has long faced a question. Fundamental constants such as the strength of gravity and the mass of the electron seem to fall within ranges that permit stars, chemistry and life. With different values, complex structures can become difficult to form. This is the fine-tuning problem. Major explanations include a designer; many universes with different parameters, with observers appearing only in a suitable one; a deeper theory showing that the constants could only have these values; or constants gradually formed through successive cosmic cycles. The conjecture here belongs to the last group. How “fine” the tuning really is remains disputed: some work finds that stars can still form across large variations in several constants. [Adams (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2019.02.001)

Constants are extremely stable within a material universe. If they change, it can only be between cycles. My conjecture is that **the constants of the next cycle arise from parameters left by the previous cycle, and the magnitude of change depends on the previous outcome.** If a material universe forms rich and stable structures—where “rich” means the depth of its whole history, as explained in Section 7—the starting point it leaves is relatively stable and the next cycle's constants change only slightly. If its structures are poor and its terminal state chaotic, the next constants may change substantially. No one evaluates or selects. There is only one relation: the previous outcome affects the magnitude of subsequent variation.

That relation is enough for constants gradually to concentrate in regions compatible with structure formation. In a suitable region, change is small and parameters remain nearby for a long time. In an unsuitable region, change is large and they leave quickly. Over time, parameters spend longest in regions that produce rich structures. This requires neither many simultaneous competing universes nor a selector. It is **differential retention**: the same logic as “resilient structures are more likely to persist,” but operating through time rather than space. In the language of this essay, rich outcomes condense and deepen where they are; poor outcomes expand to explore new possibilities.

How a cycle ends must also be clarified. The 2025 version imagined history being condensed into an archive at the end of a cycle. If this required a separate “compilation” step, a problem would arise: what if the universe is interrupted too soon—through premature collapse, vacuum decay, or an end before stars form—so that history has no time to condense? But Section 1 said that recording is not separate from occurrence: occurrence itself is the record. A cycle's history is recorded as it occurs and need not be compiled at the end. There is therefore no state in which condensation is “not yet complete.” However and whenever a cycle ends, it leaves the historical depth reached by that moment. Premature interruption is simply a shallower history. Under differential retention, the next cycle varies more widely; no additional mechanism is needed. How a cycle ends is itself determined by its constants: the sign and magnitude of the cosmological constant help determine collapse or indefinite expansion. The form of the ending belongs to the same mechanism.

Earlier proposals are nearby. Wheeler envisaged physical constants being reset at each cosmic bounce, but as random restarts without accumulation. Smolin's cosmological natural selection lets constants be inherited with small variations in daughter universes born through black holes. Selection depends on the number of daughter universes and therefore requires many universes. In the cyclic model proposed by Steinhardt and Turok in 2006, the cosmological constant declines from cycle to cycle; the smaller it becomes, the longer each cycle lasts, so it spends most time at a very small value. This is the closest physical model to differential retention, though it concerns only one constant. In biology, bacteria can raise mutation rates under stress; evolutionary computation uses step-size adaptation that reduces variation after success and enlarges it after failure. The conjecture here is similar but distinct: the magnitude of variation depends on outcomes rather than competition among a population.

This is the most conjectural link in the framework. How could the richness of structures in a material universe affect the starting point left when it ends? No known physical process currently explains it. The essay presents it as a philosophical conjecture and lists it among the open questions.
