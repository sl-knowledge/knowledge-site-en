## 1. Information is the essence of the universe

What is the universe fundamentally made of? **My philosophical position is that information is the essence of the universe, and matter is one form that information takes under particular regularities.**

In this framework, information refers broadly to existence, relationships, structures and possibilities for change. This sense is broader than the knowledge people record and communicate or the quantity measured in Shannon’s theory of communication.

This essay is a concise revision of my 2025 work *Cosmic Awakening Information Field Theory*.

From this starting point, the next questions are how information evolves and how it takes the form of matter, life, cognition and such a diverse world. This leads to information expansion and condensation.

## 2. Information expansion and condensation

The alternation of information expansion and condensation is like the breathing of the universe.

- **Information expansion is like breathing out.** A structure formed through condensation comes back into contact with the world and encounters a wider range of situations. A scientific theory, for example, encounters new phenomena and opens new possibilities when applied to new questions. This unfolding of possibilities and diversity is what the original essay calls “information growth.”
- **Information condensation is like breathing in.** Dispersed information forms relatively stable structures. Science condenses many observations into theories, and civilizations condense complex experiences into knowledge that can be passed on.

The universe has no purpose. Relatively stable structures are more likely than scattered information to persist and be passed on. What survives can sometimes look as though it formed in pursuit of a purpose.

Evolution can then be understood as a recurring cycle.

> **Information expansion → new possibilities unfold → information condensation → another round of expansion.**

The results of past evolution become the starting point for what follows. Information condensation preserves the past and can help create the future.

Within its own timescale, a single system can alternate between condensation and expansion. Across the vast universe, condensation is happening in some places while expansion is happening in others. The essay envisages a similar rhythm for the universe as a whole over longer timescales.

## 3. Entropy, creation and disintegration

In this framework, entropy increase is a cost of maintaining complex structures. Living things use energy to sustain their internal activity while releasing heat and waste into their surroundings. The order within a living system and the dissipation around it are two aspects of the same process.

A structure has a life cycle from emergence and flourishing to eventual disintegration. Its manifest complexity can rise and then fall, and some of what it produced may be carried forward by new structures. The coffee automaton study gives an example of complexity rising and falling in a particular model under a specified measure. See [Aaronson, Carroll and Ouellette (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.6903).

A changeless universe whose structures lasted forever might be a magnificent but cold “crystal prison.” If everything were permanently fixed, there would be no new events, relationships or creations.

The breakdown of old structures sometimes reopens possibilities that had been closed off.

## 4. Cycles do more than repeat

A cosmic cycle begins again under conditions left by what came before, so the starting point changes.

Every set of structures and rules opens some possibilities and closes others. When old structures grow rigid and can no longer generate changes, a system may need to move from adjusting within the existing rules to changing the rules themselves.

The original essay calls this process the “Gödelian spiral.” Gödel’s incompleteness theorem serves here as a philosophical inspiration and a structural analogy. A set of rules can leave problems it cannot resolve, and the next round can encounter limits of its own.

This leads me to believe that the universe must keep evolving, one cycle after another. Each cycle inherits conditions from its predecessors and leaves new conditions for what follows. In this philosophical picture, there is no first cycle and no last. **It has neither a beginning nor an end.**

The same analogy applies to human understanding. Sometimes a difficulty persists because our concepts, goals or ways of observing set a boundary on what we can see. Solving it may require stepping back from the system to reconsider how it is framed.

## 5. Two layers of information: what happens and what remains

The original essay uses two layers of information to think about cosmic cycles.

The first is the world as it unfolds within a cycle. Possibilities become events, giving rise to galaxies, life, thought and civilization. It is the universe’s “sandbox” for that cycle.

The second is the historical information left by successive cycles. In this picture, what a universe has gone through condenses at the end of a cycle into a kind of “archive of wisdom,” which shapes the conditions for the next round of evolution.

The question concerns what the archive could carry forward, whatever physical form it might take.

> **Can the past enter the future in some form so that the next round need not start from scratch?**

We already see patterns like this in life, culture and knowledge. Heredity allows living things to inherit the past. Speech and writing allow civilizations to preserve experience. Theories give later generations a starting point for further inquiry.

Extending the pattern to cosmic cycles is a philosophical conjecture. “Memory” here means that structures left by the past continue to affect what follows.

## 6. Life, death and the meaning of existence

This view of the universe leads to a reconsideration of what it means for an individual to exist.

Life is a structure formed in the course of evolution that can perceive, remember, learn and create. With human beings, a part of the universe began to ask about the origins, structure and meaning of the universe itself.

We observe from within the universe. Our thoughts, feelings, science, art and creations belong to its unfolding history.

The original essay expresses the cycle of life and death in a deliberately strong sentence.

> **Life is for the sake of death, and death is for the sake of life.**

Here “for the sake of” expresses how life and death shape one another within the cycle. To live is to write a unique chapter, and death brings that particular life to a close. A life can end and still leave its mark on other lives, relationships, knowledge, works of art and civilization. Some of what it leaves can become part of later structures.

The meaning of life extends beyond resisting its end. It can lie in living out its finite course as fully, deeply and creatively as we can. The small cycles of our lives and deaths take part in the world’s longer processes of creation and change.

“Cosmic Awakening” can be understood in this sense, even if the universe as a whole has no humanlike consciousness.

> **The universe has given rise to beings capable of knowing, understanding and reflecting on it. A part of the universe has begun to know the universe itself.**

## 7. Connections with earlier ideas

John Archibald Wheeler’s *It from Bit* explores the relationship between information and physical reality. Seth Lloyd approaches the universe through computation and information processing, and Stephen Wolfram studies how simple programs produce complex behavior. These are lines of thought I cited in the 2025 essay. Starting from the view that information is fundamental, this essay asks how information expands, condenses into structures and, through those structures, changes the conditions for the next round of evolution.

Empedocles uses “Love” and “Strife” to describe how things come together and separate. In *First Principles*, Herbert Spencer contrasts the integration of matter and the accompanying dissipation of motion with the disintegration of matter. The *Book of Changes* depicts the alternation of yin and yang through the succession of sun and moon, cold and heat. These passages offer parallels to the alternation described here. This essay goes on to ask how structures formed through condensation preserve the past and open up the next round of possibilities. See [Empedocles’ fragments](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Empedocles), [Spencer’s First Principles](https://constitution.org/2-Authors/hs/first_prin.htm) and [the Book of Changes, Appended Statements II](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/易傳/繫辭下).

In *The Phenomenon of Man*, Teilhard de Chardin discusses the development of complexity and consciousness and describes evolution as moving toward the Omega Point. In *The Singularity Is Near*, Ray Kurzweil calls the stage in which intelligence spreads into the universe “The Universe Wakes Up.” This essay shares their interest in connecting cosmic evolution, complexity and consciousness. It leaves evolution open-ended, without a personal God or a final stage defined by technological expansion or an uninterrupted rise in complexity.

Lee Smolin’s cosmological natural selection and Roger Penrose’s conformal cyclic cosmology explore inheritance and cycles at the cosmic scale from different directions. Ilya Prigogine’s research on dissipative structures and Jeremy England’s work on self-replication and dissipation provide scientific background for the formation of ordered structures in systems with flows of energy.

## 8. Questions still open

Cosmic awakening remains an evolving set of ideas. Some questions are still open.

- Under what conditions does expansion produce enough new material for another round of condensation?
- What kinds of condensation can preserve the past and generate new capabilities?
- Why does a system grow rigid, and how can it reopen possibilities?
- How does history enter the future? Could the universe have a kind of memory?
- Are life, cognition and civilization contingent outcomes of cosmic evolution, or do they express a deeper process?

The framework aims to preserve this picture of ongoing change.

> **The world continually generates new possibilities. Some condense into structures that preserve the past, generate new capabilities and become the starting point for another round of possibilities.**

This idea is a seed. Every act of reflection, discussion, questioning and creation is part of cosmic awakening.

A separate study takes human knowledge as its subject and seeks to describe information condensation and expansion as a testable mechanism. The hierarchical causal–control hypothesis (HCC) starts with local systems and proposes testable hypotheses about how structures form, persist and generate new capabilities. See [The Hierarchical Causal–Control Hypothesis](../framework/foundations/hierarchical-causal-control.md).
