This process has no purpose and will never be complete.**

>

## 1. Information is the essence of the universe

What is the universe fundamentally? **My philosophical position is that information is the essence of the universe, and matter is one form taken by information under particular regularities.**

In this framework, information refers broadly to existence, relations, structures and possibilities for change. This is wider than the knowledge people record and communicate, and wider than the quantity measured in Shannon's theory of communication. I use this philosophical position to understand existence and its evolution. It is not a conclusion of physics: it offers a way of seeing the world and does not replace physical descriptions of matter. Philosophically, it is close to structural realism. Poincaré held that science can know only relations between things; Ladyman and Ross go further and argue that the world itself consists of relations and structures, with no “things” outside those relations. This essay adopts the latter position.

This essay is a concise revision of my 2025 work *Cosmic Awakening Information Field Theory*, referred to below as “the 2025 version.”

### The information field, manifestation and the material universe

More specifically, I hold that **the information field has always existed and is more fundamental than spacetime and matter**. The information field in this essay means the whole of existence. I understand it at three levels:

1. **The deepest level: mathematics and logic.** They determine what is coherent and what is possible. They do not depend on any particular manifestation and do not change with one: pi, prime numbers and logical consistency remain what they are. The 2025 version called this the “invariant foundation.”
2. **The middle level: physical laws and constants.** They are one set among the many possible rules permitted by mathematics and logic, and they determine how a particular manifestation occurs—for example, the strength of gravity or the mass of the electron. They remain constant within one manifestation; different manifestations may use different sets. The 2025 version called this the “variable algorithm.”
3. **The manifest level: the material universe.** This consists of events that actually occur under a particular set of laws, together with stable patterns that can maintain themselves and interact—what we call matter.

**Manifestation** has two meanings. First, it is a movement from possible to actual: under a particular set of laws, some possibilities in the information field actually occur as events that interact and can be observed. What occurs is only a minute part of what could occur. Second, matter consists of stable patterns in information. A vortex is not something apart from water but a stable form of its motion; similarly, matter is not something apart from information. The totality of these events and patterns is **one material universe**.

Chess offers an analogy. The logic of what counts as a coherent game does not change. Each game may use a different set of rules, like different variants of chess. The game actually played is the material universe. Once completed, its record affects how the rules of the next game change.

**Cosmic cycles** are the recurring relations among these three levels. A material universe manifests, evolves and disintegrates under one set of laws. Its spacetime carries its causal history. When its material structures disintegrate, the information they leave does not simply vanish; it may condense again and affect the laws and constants of the next cycle, which then manifests in turn. Mathematics and logic are the invariant stage, the rules change from cycle to cycle, and each cycle has its own history. The Big Bang was not the birth of the information field but the beginning of a manifestation. A material universe can be born and die, while the information field always exists. **The material world we can observe is only a subset of all existence.** Manifestation and return are condensation and expansion at the deepest level: the information field manifests as a material universe, and the history left by that cycle returns to the level of the information field and affects the next manifestation. Section 7 explains how this recursion reaches itself in one such manifestation.

Terminology matters here. When discussing the Big Bang, spacetime, energy, entropy, life and humanity, this essay says “material universe.” When discussing the whole of existence, it says “information field.” “Universe” in the title refers to the information field as a whole. “Information” also has two uses: information as existence itself—the information field—and structures, records and knowledge within a material universe. The latter can be observed and studied; the former is the philosophical position of this essay.

**Why is there something rather than nothing?** This is one of philosophy's oldest questions. Within this framework, it is not answered but dissolved. If existence means distinction, then “absolute nothing”—a condition without any distinction—is not a state that could have existed but happened not to. It is not a state at all. “Something” was not selected from several possibilities; “nothing” was never another possibility alongside it. This conclusion follows from the essay's definition of existence and is not a proof about reality. It uses the same move later applied to heat death in Section 4: rather than answer the question inside its original framework, change levels so that the question no longer arises in the same form.

### Time and space

Time and space also occupy different places within the three levels above.

**The form of spacetime belongs to the laws; a particular spacetime belongs to a material universe.** The number of spatial dimensions, the speed of light, and the relation between time and space belong to a set of laws and may differ between manifestations. The spacetime that actually unfolds in a material universe, together with every event within it, belongs to the manifest level.

**Two kinds of “before and after” must be distinguished.** The first is **physical time**. It exists only within a material universe, can be measured by clocks, and has a direction. That direction comes from increasing entropy and from the accumulation of records: the past can leave traces; the future cannot. The second is an **order of dependence**. The ordering between cosmic cycles, and the appearance and disappearance of possibilities in the meta-universal substrate, are not physical time and cannot be measured by a clock. They have an order because the latter depends on what the former leaves behind. When this essay speaks of something occurring “before the first material universe” or at “a level deeper than physical time,” it means this order of dependence, not an earlier moment in physical time.

**Space is the structure of relations among events.** It is not a container placed there first and then filled with things. It is the relational structure among events in one material universe. At the deepest level of the information field, concepts such as distance and position may not apply.

**Spacetime is the record of this cycle.** The 2025 version wrote that recording is not an act separate from occurrence: occurrence itself is the record. The spacetime of a material universe carries its complete causal history. Once an event occurs, it becomes an ineradicable part of that history.

These views are consistent with several earlier positions and current research directions. Leibniz held that time and space are relations among things rather than containers. Since Boltzmann, the direction of time has usually been understood through increasing entropy. Physicists including Rovelli and Barbour have argued that time may not be fundamental. Some contemporary work in quantum gravity explores whether spacetime may emerge from deeper structures related to quantum information. None of these programmes is settled. This essay claims consistency with them, not support from them.

The account above explains what it means for the information field to manifest as a material universe. The next question is how structures within one material universe grow from simple to complex, how life and cognition appear, and why the world becomes so diverse. This leads to information expansion and condensation.

In Sections 2 and 3, “information” means information within the material universe: structures, records and knowledge. Condensation and expansion at this level can be observed and studied. Sections 4 and 5 extend the same pattern between cosmic cycles—to the level of the information field. That extension is the philosophical conjecture of this essay.

## 2. Information expansion and condensation

Information condensation and expansion are the central mechanism proposed here. They are not deduced from a single principle but abstracted from superficially different phenomena. Stars condense from gas and disperse newly synthesised elements when they die. Life preserves traits adapted to an environment through heredity and spreads them through reproduction. People condense experience into rules and theories, then carry them into new problems.

Why does this pattern recur? Two reasons are enough, neither requiring the universe to have a purpose. First, relatively stable structures are more likely than scattered states to persist and continue, so what remains is often a condensed structure. The result can look as though it formed towards a purpose, when it was simply more likely to persist. Second, a condensed structure does not remain still. It interacts with its surroundings and thereby opens possibilities that did not exist before. This is expansion. There is a deeper reason as well: every finite condensation leaves a remainder that it cannot contain, and new information can come only from outside the present structure (see the end of Section 4). Preservation and interaction alternate, so condensation and expansion recur. This pattern can be observed and tested at many levels within a material universe. Extending it between cosmic cycles is a philosophical conjecture.

The alternation of information expansion and condensation is like the breathing of the universe.

- **Information expansion is like breathing out.** A condensed structure comes back into contact with the world and its range of contact grows. When a scientific theory is applied to a new problem, for example, it encounters new phenomena and opens new possibilities. This unfolding of possibilities and diversity is what the 2025 version called “information growth.”
- **Information condensation is like breathing in.** Dispersed information forms a relatively stable structure. Science condenses many observations into a theory; a civilisation condenses complex experience into knowledge that can be passed on.

Evolution within a material universe can therefore be understood as a recurring cycle:

> **information expansion → new possibilities unfold → information condensation → another round of information expansion.**

The results of past evolution become the starting point of what follows. Information condensation not only preserves the past; it can also create the future.

On its own timescale, a single system can alternate between condensation and expansion. Across the vast material universe, some places are condensing while others are expanding, so the two occur simultaneously. At a longer scale, this essay conjectures that the information field as a whole—the sequence of material universes—exhibits the same rhythm (Sections 4 and 5).

### Stability, modules and destruction

What remains, I have said, is often a condensed structure. Remaining depends on stability. A structure can be preserved only if it maintains or restores itself under disturbance. An unstable combination falls apart as soon as it forms and cannot accumulate. Each condensation therefore leaves a relatively stable unit—a **module**: an atom, a molecule, a cell, a gene segment, a concept or a skill.

Modules are also what expansion carries. Expansion does not scatter isolated details again; it brings stable modules into contact with the world and with other modules. A module can be reused and assembled into new combinations. The combinatorial space is far larger than it would be if construction always began from zero, and that is why expansion opens new possibilities. Stable new combinations remain and become modules at a higher level. Accumulation is therefore hierarchical: the modules of one level are the building blocks of the next. In “The Architecture of Complexity,” Herbert Simon illustrated this with his parable of the watchmakers. A system assembled from stable subcomponents loses only one component when interrupted and need not start again from the beginning, so hierarchical structures form much faster. The layered accumulation of resilience discussed in Section 8 is an outcome of this process.

> **Condensation leaves stable modules; expansion brings modules together and recombines them; further condensation fixes a new combination as a higher-level module.**

The universe has no purpose, and this mechanism does not guarantee that accumulation only increases. It says only that what can remain will remain. Destruction arises from the same mechanism and is not an exception to it. The two are related in at least three ways.

**First, destruction is often a form of expansion.** A massive star ends its condensation in a supernova, dispersing elements forged inside it and supplying material for later stars and planets (Section 6). Mass extinctions cleared ecological niches in Earth's history; after the end-Cretaceous extinction, mammals diversified on a large scale. When a structure is broken apart, its modules are released into new combinations.

**Second, condensation itself creates conditions for destruction.** The deeper the condensation, the greater the concentration of energy and organisation and the greater the release when it collapses. More massive stars end more violently; the more powerful a human technology, the more destruction it can cause (Section 7). Destruction is not merely an external accident. Part of it is produced by condensation itself.

**Third, the consequence of destruction depends on the ratio between the scale of destruction and the scale of the modules.**

- A disturbance smaller than a module is absorbed. This is resilience.
- If a higher-level structure is broken but lower-level modules remain, the higher level may be recombined from those modules, often in a new form.
- If every level is destroyed in one locality, accumulation stops there, although it may continue elsewhere.
- The end of a material universe destroys every structure within that cycle. Only laws and parameters can affect the next cycle (Section 5).

Accumulation can continue through destruction under two conditions: **modularity localises loss, and expansion distributes copies across different places.** DNA exists in many individuals; knowledge exists in many people and books. A structure that condenses without expanding can disappear in a single local catastrophe. We can look back along a line of accumulation today because we happen to stand on a line that was not interrupted (see the discussion of survivorship bias in Section 8). The “living seed” of Section 8 consists of lower-level modules and distributed copies that remain after destruction.

## 3. Entropy, creation and disintegration

The following discussion of energy, dissipation and entropy applies only to the manifest material universe, not directly to the information field itself.

Information condensation is not costless. In an open system, forming and maintaining a relatively stable structure requires exchanges of energy or matter with the environment and entails dissipation. A local region can become relatively ordered; when discussing increasing entropy, the system and its environment must be considered together. [Prigogine's Nobel Lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/prigogine-lecture.pdf)

Gravity adds a special feature. In a gravity-dominated system, matter clustering together can itself be a direction of increasing entropy. A nearly uniform gas forming stars and galaxies under gravity does not violate the second law of thermodynamics. Penrose particularly emphasises this in *Cycles of Time*. Thus the most important condensation on cosmic scales occurs precisely in the direction of increasing entropy.

Structures do not remain unchanged forever. They can form, develop and disintegrate; manifest complexity may rise and then fall. The coffee automaton study provides an example of rising and falling complexity in a particular model under a particular measure. See [Aaronson, Carroll and Ouellette (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.6903). It is used here as a concrete example, not as proof of a universal law of material universes.

If every structure were fixed forever, new events and relations would also be constrained. The disintegration of an old structure does not guarantee a better one, but it can reopen possibilities that had been closed. Whether it does depends on the scale of disintegration relative to the scale of its modules (Section 2, “Stability, modules and destruction”).

## 4. Cycles do more than repeat

A cycle of material universes does not return to its starting point. It starts again from conditions left by the past.

Every set of structures and rules opens some possibilities and closes others. As old structures harden, adjustments within the existing rules often cease to produce anything new; change is then more likely to come from changing the rules themselves.

The 2025 version called this process the “Gödel spiral.” Gödelian incompleteness is used at several levels in this essay. Some uses are supported by mathematical theorems; others are structural analogies (see “From distinction to incompleteness” below). A set of rules can face problems it cannot solve, and the next cycle will encounter limits of its own.

I used a “toy universe” to illustrate the point. Imagine a universe with only two physical laws: A can produce B, and B can produce C. Within it, one can explain where C came from (B) and where B came from (A). Its laws can never answer where A came from, or why these two laws hold rather than others. That is the universe's logical “Gödel blind spot.”

To answer, I wrote, one must “step outside” that universe to a meta-level from which its laws can be examined. Cosmic cycles correspond to that move. In this essay the meta-level is the information field. Possible laws appear there; coherent laws that can sustain themselves remain and become the laws of a material universe; after a cycle ends, laws and constants change between cycles. “Examine” is only a borrowed word and implies no examiner. What occurs is simply the appearance, persistence and change of laws. No material universe can explain from within where its own laws came from. Their source must be sought at the level of the information field. This is why the first manifestation is discussed below and changes in physical constants in Section 5.

Moreover, because mathematics and logic are the same in every cycle, incompleteness holds in each of them. The rules change; logical limits remain.

Following this analogy, I choose to believe that material universes manifest cycle after cycle. If no set of rules can resolve every problem, there can be no final cycle. Each inherits conditions left before it and leaves new conditions for what follows.

Did the universe have a beginning? The 2025 version imagined it this way. Before the first material universe there was no time, space or event, only the unmanifest information field. I called it the “meta-universal substrate”: an eternal reservoir of potential containing every mathematical and logical possibility. Physical time began only with the first material universe. “Before” therefore refers not to chronological order but to a level of existence.

Within this substrate, innumerable possible laws continually appear and disappear as fluctuations. They do not occur in physical time. They belong to the order of dependence described in Section 1: the appearance and disappearance of possibilities themselves. Most possible laws are internally contradictory or cannot sustain themselves, and vanish as they arise. The first Big Bang was the first fluctuation coherent enough to sustain structure and begin evolving—the first manifestation of the information field, like the first stable nucleus of a snowflake. **Its efficiency was a consequence of its being able to exist, not the reason it was selected.** Material universes then followed one after another. Only one exists at a time; many “drafts” preceded it and many “revisions” will follow. Each begins again from conditions left by the last. If the cosmic spiral has a direction and each cycle leaves more than the previous one, a beginning is more natural than an infinite past. Work in physics also suggests that spacetimes which are expanding on average are difficult to extend indefinitely into the past. [Borde, Guth and Vilenkin (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.90.151301)

This, too, is a philosophical conjecture. What matters most to me is the subsequent cycling: **the cycles of material universes have a beginning but no endpoint; the information field has always existed.**

### Stepping outside: heat death is not the end

Physics often treats heat death as the end of the universe. In a closed material universe entropy rises, energy becomes more evenly distributed, exploitable energy differences disappear, and no structure can form or be maintained. This picture runs from Kelvin and Clausius in the nineteenth century to many current cosmological discussions. At the level of the material universe, this essay does not deny it. A material universe can disintegrate; Section 3 already said that structures form, develop and disintegrate. Heat death does not mean that every distinction disappears. At thermal equilibrium microscopic differences are at their maximum. What disappears are macroscopic gradients—differences of temperature and density and exploitable energy gradients—and those gradients are what drive condensation and structure formation. Heat death exhausts the driving force of condensation within one cycle; it does not end distinction itself.

But as long as the frame is the material universe, heat death can only be the end. This essay does not refute heat death within that level. It steps outside the level, as it did with the toy universe's blind spot. From the information-field perspective, heat death is the end of one manifestation, not the end of existence. Mathematics and logic remain; the information field continues; the history left by this cycle affects the next manifestation, and another cycle begins. Heat death does not touch mathematics and logic. They are the deepest invariant level, independent of any manifestation and never located on the level that ends. Within a material universe, the answer to “Will the universe end?” can only be yes. At the level of the information field it is no longer the same question.

This is the method running through the essay:

> **When a problem cannot be solved within a system, the problem itself is not necessarily insoluble; the frame may be too small. It may be dissolved only by stepping outside the original frame and viewing it from a higher level.**

Gödel shows why the step is necessary. Heat death is the clearest cosmological application of the method.

Three qualifications are needed. First, this essay is not alone in trying to go beyond heat death. Penrose's conformal cyclic cosmology, the cyclic model of Steinhardt and Turok, and Smolin's cosmological natural selection all envisage universes after a universe at the physical level. This essay differs in the level to which it steps—the always-existing information field containing all mathematical and logical possibilities—and in its reason for stepping: not a particular physical mechanism, but the principle that a system cannot fully account for itself from within. Second, stepping outside has a cost. The meta-level cannot be verified from within the system; that is precisely part of Gödel's lesson. Heat death is therefore dissolved here at the level of a philosophical position, not physically proven not to occur. Third, this ontological step does not continue indefinitely. The information field is all existence and there is no higher level beyond it. This is a step between levels of existence; steps between levels of knowledge have no endpoint, as explained below. Invariant mathematics and logic form the final foundation of the position and are not in turn explained by something else.

### From distinction to incompleteness: the basis of the Gödel spiral

Gödel is invoked in several places. This section identifies which claims are supported by mathematical theorems, which are principles, and which remain conjectures.

**Starting point: to exist is to be distinct.** Section 1 says that information is the essence of existence, and the most basic meaning of information is distinction—a difference that makes a difference. The 2025 version argued that in a state containing only “one,” with no distinction, nothing can happen. “Two” is the smallest step capable of producing distinction, like the first bit; and the two states can be transformed into one another, yin into yang and yang into yin. Manifestation, as described above, comes from fluctuations in the information field. Without distinction there can be no fluctuation and no manifestation. Distinction is therefore not an added assumption but the condition that makes any manifestation possible.

**From distinction to incompleteness: Lawvere's fixed-point theorem.** In 1969 the mathematician F. William Lawvere proved a very general theorem. In plain language: if there is a “negation” without a fixed point—one that changes every state into another, as yin becomes yang and yang becomes yin—then no system can completely represent within itself every property concerning itself. Some properties will remain unrepresentable. The theorem does not require a formal system or a computer and does not depend on particular physical laws. It applies to infinite systems as well. Cantor's diagonal argument, Gödel's incompleteness theorem, Tarski's theorem on the undefinability of truth, and Turing's halting problem are special cases under different conditions. Physicist David Wolpert proved a related result directly for physical inference devices in 2008: no inference device in the universe can predict or know everything including itself. Because mathematics and logic do not change between manifestations, these results hold in every cycle.

Connecting this theorem to the essay takes only three steps:

1. Manifestation requires distinctions, and distinctions can be transformed into one another (Section 1 and this section).
2. Manifestation condenses into thought—structures capable of forming models of other structures and even of the whole (Sections 6 and 7).
3. By Lawvere's theorem, self-representation that includes itself cannot be complete.

> **The distinction that makes manifestation possible also makes complete knowledge of the world impossible.**

Manifestation and the absence of an endpoint to knowledge have the same root. The 2025 version treated an “incompleteness drive” as an axiom. Here it follows from “to exist is to be distinct” together with the definition of thought in Section 6. This is the basis of the claim in Section 7 that awakening cannot be completed.

**There is no final level to step outside to.** Add the proposition “this system is consistent” to a formal system and one obtains a stronger new system. The new system has a Gödel sentence of its own, which can be added in turn, without a final system. Turing in 1939 and Feferman in 1962 studied such sequences of progressively strengthened theories. At the level of knowledge, the “Gödel spiral” is a genuine mathematical structure.

A concrete example is **Goodstein's theorem**. Begin with any positive integer and repeatedly apply a simple rule involving only addition, subtraction and exponentiation. The value first grows beyond imagination—from 4, the process takes roughly 3·2⁴⁰²⁶⁵³²¹¹ − 2 steps—but eventually falls to 0. The proposition is true, yet it cannot be proved using only the axioms of ordinary arithmetic (Peano arithmetic). Its proof requires transfinite ordinals, a view from a higher-level system. Inside the system every step is perfectly calculable, but the overall outcome is invisible. Stepping outside makes it visible. The higher system then has true propositions of its own that it cannot prove.

**Why every condensation leaves a remainder.** A set of axioms is itself a condensation: finitely many rules summarise infinitely many facts. In 1974 Gregory Chaitin gave an information-theoretic result often paraphrased as saying that a set of axioms can prove only facts containing roughly as much information as the axioms themselves. The precise theorem concerns a particular case; this essay uses the informal meaning. If the world is rich enough, every finite condensation leaves something it cannot contain. The only way to leave no remainder is not to condense—to list every fact unchanged—but that is no longer a theory, just as a map as large as the territory is no longer useful as a map. Chaitin therefore also argued that mathematics, like physics, should add new axioms in response to experience.

The mechanism of Section 2 and the limit described here are therefore one thing. Every condensation is incomplete, so new information can come only from outside the present structure. A condensed structure must return to contact with the world before another level can condense.

> **Incompleteness is not only a limit of knowledge; it is also why condensation and expansion never stop.**

**Where the principle holds but the theorem does not directly answer.** Lawvere's theorem strengthens the toy-universe argument: no material universe can completely describe all of its own properties from within, so a complete account requires a higher level. But the theorem does not show that “why these laws rather than others?” is exactly one of the unrepresentable properties. That remains partly a philosophical interpretation.

**What remains conjectural.** The 2025 version said that incompleteness “drives” the appearance of another material universe. More precisely, three claims play different roles. Incompleteness guarantees that no set of laws is final; this has theorem-level support. The next manifestation occurs because fluctuations in the information field never stop: heat death erases gradients only within one cycle while distinctions in the information field remain. This is the philosophical position of the essay. The next cycle inherits from the last through the differential-retention mechanism of Section 5, which has been logically tested in a model. The remaining conjecture is this: after a cycle ends, do fluctuations in the information field continue while carrying parameters left by the previous cycle? A physical carrier would be required, and none is currently known.

Two kinds of “stepping outside” must also be separated. Moving from the material universe to the information field is an ontological step and ends at the information field because it is all existence. Knowledge of that whole, by contrast, moves from one epistemic level to another without a final level.

> **Existence has its whole; knowledge has no endpoint.**

This can be stated more precisely. There are three kinds of infinity, corresponding to the essay's three levels:

- **The information field as all existence: absolute infinity.** The “set of all sets” cannot itself be a set without contradiction. Cantor therefore distinguished an “absolute infinity” above every transfinite infinity. It can only be acknowledged and can never be known, even approximately. The information field as all existence is like this: it is the whole, but no representation within it can contain it.
- **Objects in mathematics and logic: actual infinity.** Transfinite objects such as ω, ε₀ and the continuum can be studied consistently as completed objects.
- **Material universes, knowledge and cycles: finite but potentially infinite.** A finite region has an upper bound on the information it can contain (the Bekenstein bound), and the observable universe contains finite information. Every step in knowledge and every cycle is finite, but another step can always follow.

“Existence has its whole” refers to absolute infinity. “Knowledge has no endpoint” refers to potential infinity. Aristotle accepted only potential infinity; Cantor defended actual infinity. This debate, more than two thousand years old, need not be settled by choosing one side: each describes a different level.

We can also distinguish two ways mathematics uses infinity in relation to the material universe. **When infinity is used to prove a result about finite objects, the result is exact.** Goodstein's theorem uses infinite ordinals to establish a precise truth about finite integers, at the cost of trusting the consistency of the higher system. **When a model containing infinities describes the physical world, its result is approximate.** Continuous fluids and continuous spacetime approximate large numbers of finite units. The first case shows that mathematical infinity is not dispensable rhetoric: it has real consequences for truths about finite worlds. The second shows that a material universe is a finite manifestation from the mathematical level, which is why mathematics containing infinities can approximate it so accurately.

Earlier thinkers anticipated parts of this picture. Cantor distinguished three levels—the material universe, the human mind and God—and assigned absolute infinity to God. Zermelo treated the set-theoretic universe as a potentially infinite ascending sequence, each level of which is itself actually infinite. Philosopher Patrick Grim used Cantor's argument to show that there can be no “set of all truths”: considered through truth, the universe is essentially open and incomplete. This essay differs in making the highest level not God but a purposeless information field. The same principle—distinction plus self-representation—explains both manifestation and why absolute infinity cannot be known. The finitude of a material universe and the potential infinity of knowledge are connected by the spiral of condensation and expansion.

That is why cosmic awakening cannot be completed. The name “Gödel spiral” is retained for this family of results, of which Gödel's theorem is the best known.

## 5. Two layers of information: the world occurring and the history that remains

The 2025 version used a two-layer information model to understand cosmic cycles.

The first layer is the world unfolding within a cycle. Potential possibilities become events that have occurred, forming galaxies, life, thought and civilisations. This is the universe's “cycle sandbox.”

The second layer is historical information left by different cycles. Everything experienced by a material universe was imagined to condense at the end of the cycle into a kind of “archive of wisdom,” becoming background and conditions for subsequent evolution. “Archive” is a metaphor. It does not mean that the last cycle's knowledge is packaged unchanged and passed on; its actual meaning is specified below.

The central thought is not the physical form of the archive, but this question:

> **Can the past enter the future in some form, so that the next cycle need not begin entirely from zero?**

We already see this structure in life, culture and knowledge. Heredity allows life to inherit the past; language and writing preserve experience for civilisation; theories allow later researchers to begin from earlier knowledge.

Extending the same structure to cosmic cycles is a philosophical conjecture retained here. “Memory” means that structures left by the past continue to affect what follows.

How does the past enter the future? In the 2025 version, historical information accumulated across cycles does not directly participate in the internal evolution of the next material universe. It is expressed by shaping the next cycle's initial laws and parameters. What the last cycle leaves affects the point from which the next begins, without carrying particular events into it. That is enough to make the cycle a spiral rather than a circle. In the language of this essay, what passes between cycles is parameters of the generative rules, not phenotypic content. No other part of the essay depends on this conjecture.

Even if the universe as a whole has the rhythm imagined here, a locality such as Earth can still be completely interrupted and its accumulated knowledge lost. Whether Earth's experience can be inherited elsewhere or by later evolution remains open.

### How the next cycle occurs

The 2025 version called this the “Gödel engine” and described three steps. The history of one universe condenses into an archive that assesses the bottleneck causing stagnation. Quantum fluctuations in the terminal state act as a “variation engine,” making small adjustments to or rewriting laws. The new laws become the starting point of the next Big Bang. An axiom of “fastest information condensation” supplied pressure for the universe to “restart.”

The words assess, optimise and pressure all imply purpose. Remove purpose and the mechanism can remain. No pressure is needed because the fluctuations that produced the first manifestation never stopped. No assessor is needed; the outcome of one cycle is simply a condition affecting the magnitude of change in the next. No optimisation is needed; parameters remain longest where structures can form. This is differential retention, described below. Restarting uses the same mechanism as the first manifestation.

### Physical constants: parameters left by the previous cycle?

Physics has long faced a question. Fundamental constants such as the strength of gravity and the mass of the electron seem to fall within ranges that permit stars, chemistry and life. With different values, complex structures can become difficult to form. This is the fine-tuning problem. Major explanations include a designer; many universes with different parameters, with observers appearing only in a suitable one; a deeper theory showing that the constants could only have these values; or constants gradually formed through successive cosmic cycles. The conjecture here belongs to the last group. How “fine” the tuning really is remains disputed: some work finds that stars can still form across large variations in several constants. [Adams (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2019.02.001)

Constants are extremely stable within a material universe. If they change, it can only be between cycles. My conjecture is that **the constants of the next cycle arise from parameters left by the previous cycle, and the magnitude of change depends on the previous outcome.** If a material universe forms rich and stable structures—where “rich” means the depth of its whole history, as explained in Section 7—the starting point it leaves is relatively stable and the next cycle's constants change only slightly. If its structures are poor and its terminal state chaotic, the next constants may change substantially. No one evaluates or selects. There is only one relation: the previous outcome affects the magnitude of subsequent variation.

That relation is enough for constants gradually to concentrate in regions compatible with structure formation. In a suitable region, change is small and parameters remain nearby for a long time. In an unsuitable region, change is large and they leave quickly. Over time, parameters spend longest in regions that produce rich structures. This requires neither many simultaneous competing universes nor a selector. It is **differential retention**: the same logic as “resilient structures are more likely to persist,” but operating through time rather than space. In the language of this essay, rich outcomes condense and deepen where they are; poor outcomes expand to explore new possibilities.

How a cycle ends must also be clarified. The 2025 version imagined history being condensed into an archive at the end of a cycle. If this required a separate “compilation” step, a problem would arise: what if the universe is interrupted too soon—through premature collapse, vacuum decay, or an end before stars form—so that history has no time to condense? But Section 1 said that recording is not separate from occurrence: occurrence itself is the record. A cycle's history is recorded as it occurs and need not be compiled at the end. There is therefore no state in which condensation is “not yet complete.” However and whenever a cycle ends, it leaves the historical depth reached by that moment. Premature interruption is simply a shallower history. Under differential retention, the next cycle varies more widely; no additional mechanism is needed. How a cycle ends is itself determined by its constants: the sign and magnitude of the cosmological constant help determine collapse or indefinite expansion. The form of the ending belongs to the same mechanism.

Earlier proposals are nearby. Wheeler envisaged physical constants being reset at each cosmic bounce, but as random restarts without accumulation. Smolin's cosmological natural selection lets constants be inherited with small variations in daughter universes born through black holes. Selection depends on the number of daughter universes and therefore requires many universes. In the cyclic model proposed by Steinhardt and Turok in 2006, the cosmological constant declines from cycle to cycle; the smaller it becomes, the longer each cycle lasts, so it spends most time at a very small value. This is the closest physical model to differential retention, though it concerns only one constant. In biology, bacteria can raise mutation rates under stress; evolutionary computation uses step-size adaptation that reduces variation after success and enlarges it after failure. The conjecture here is similar but distinct: the magnitude of variation depends on outcomes rather than competition among a population.

This is the most conjectural link in the framework. How could the richness of structures in a material universe affect the starting point left when it ends? No known physical process currently explains it. The essay presents it as a philosophical conjecture and lists it among the open questions.

## 6. Within one material universe: from stars to life

Sections 4 and 5 concerned relations between cycles. We now return to the material universe in which we live. How did structures grow level by level from the simplest states?

The same principle is used throughout: **within one material universe, generative rules apply universally and create statistical tendencies towards some outcomes; what actually appears depends on local parameters and the history that has already occurred.** “Universal” means that the same regularities operate throughout this material universe. “Tendency” means that under given parameters some structures are more likely than others to appear or persist, not that the rules “want” them to appear.

The principle is applied below to three cases: generations of stars, the origin of life, and the path from learning to thought. The appearance of humans is left to Section 7.

### Generations of stars: a spiral within the material universe

The clearest condensation and expansion in our material universe occurs between generations of stars.

Soon after the Big Bang, matter was distributed almost uniformly and consisted mainly of hydrogen and helium, with a very small amount of lithium. Under gravity, slightly denser regions attracted more matter. Gas gathered and formed the earliest stars and galaxies. This was **condensation**.

Nuclear fusion inside stars combined light elements step by step into heavier ones such as carbon and oxygen, up to iron. This, too, was condensation: dispersed matter compressed into more complex nuclei.

At the end of their lives, massive stars exploded and scattered newly synthesised elements into space. Elements heavier than iron, such as gold and uranium, are produced mainly in processes including neutron-star mergers. This was **expansion**: condensed results spread across a wider region and mixed with interstellar gas.

Later generations of stars and planets formed from matter that already contained heavy elements. This was **new condensation**: new structures began from conditions left by earlier generations. Astronomers classify stars into “populations” by their heavy-element content. Stars formed later usually contain more heavy elements. The theoretically earliest stars contained almost only hydrogen and helium and have not yet been directly confirmed by observation.

Our Sun and Earth formed after many such generations. Most of the carbon, oxygen and iron in rocks, oceans and our bodies came from earlier stars. [NASA on the stellar life cycle](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/star-lifecycle/) · [Burbidge, Burbidge, Fowler and Hoyle (1957)](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.29.547)

Two points matter. First, this is an observable spiral within a material universe: each generation starts from conditions left by the previous one. Second, gravity and nuclear reactions drive condensation here, not purpose. Condensed structures remain only because they are sufficiently stable under the conditions of the time. In the terms used above, gravitational and nuclear laws apply universally and tend to produce stars and heavy elements; exactly where and in what order they form depends on local density, composition and history.

### The origin of life

Before life appeared on such planets, rocks and minerals and changing chemical environments already existed. They formed conditions under which life could later emerge.

How did the earliest life arise? Energy sources, chemical composition, temperature, liquid water and available time are all important local parameters. Under suitable parameters, chemical systems capable of self-replication and heritable variation may be more likely to form, but that does not make life's origin inevitable. The sequence experienced by the early Earth remains under investigation. From Earth as our single known example, it is difficult to estimate how common such parameters are in the universe and therefore whether life is common or rare. [Research on inference from life's early emergence on Earth](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1111694108)

### From learning to thought

Once life appears, experience can condense at several levels, forming a ladder:

1. **Heredity.** Some traits pass to descendants through reproduction. Experience condenses in genes and changes across generations.
2. **Learning.** Along some of Earth's evolutionary lines, organisms developed perception, memory and learning. Past experience changes what they do the next time they meet a similar situation. Condensation can now occur within one lifetime rather than waiting for genetic change across generations.
3. **Thought.** Some organisms form internal models of the external world, simulate without acting, and use the result to guide action.
4. **Reflection.** At another level, they form models of their own models: they think about their thinking.
5. **Knowledge.** Thought is fixed in carriers outside the body—language, writing and tools—and can persist beyond the individual.

**Thought**, as used here, begins at the third level: **a kind of structure in the material universe that forms models of other structures, uses them to simulate and predict without first acting, and lets the result guide action.** Two features matter. The first is representation: thought carries information about something else and can be wrong. A thermometer merely covaries with temperature; a judgement about tomorrow's weather can be false. Its ability to be false shows that it represents rather than merely reacts. The second is offline simulation: the system can try something internally before trying it in reality.

Two easily confused words must be separated. **Knowledge** is the external form of thought and may outlast the individual. **Consciousness** means subjective experience—what it is like to be the system. The definition above concerns only the function of thought, not consciousness. This essay does not explain why subjective experience exists. By the functional definition, some current artificial-intelligence systems contain part of the structure of thought. Whether they have experience is a different question.

This ladder resembles earlier accounts. Kenneth Craik proposed in 1943 that the mind builds “small-scale models” of reality and uses them to predict. Daniel Dennett described a “tower of generate-and-test”: from genetic trial and error, to learning during life, to trying possibilities first in an internal model, and then using language and tools to draw on other people's experience. From the perspective of condensation, each level makes condensation faster and less dependent on paying the cost of real-world trials.

## 7. The appearance of humans and the meaning of existence

Among the many forms of life on Earth capable of learning, one evolutionary line reached thought, reflection and knowledge: humans.

### Was the appearance of humans inevitable or contingent?

Stephen Jay Gould imagined rewinding the tape of life and playing it again: humans would probably not reappear. Simon Conway Morris places greater weight on convergent evolution, noting that different lineages sometimes independently develop similar structures. This disagreement separates two questions. Under similar conditions, will a class of structures recur? And specifically for humans, how much of Earth's path could be repeated? [Conway Morris, *Life's Solution*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/lifes-solution/82BFF629ABD29A44F1B428D03C094102)

Both positions fit the earlier principle. Tendencies in the rules explain why similar conditions can produce convergence; parameters and prior history explain why a particular path remains contingent. Under some conditions, disturbance-resistant structures are more likely to remain. When the environment changes, a formerly stable structure can lose the conditions that sustained it. **Humans are one particular path—a phenotype—produced by Earth's parameters and history.**

With humans, the carriers of condensation expanded from genes to language, writing and tools. Experience could pass between individuals and generations and the pace of condensation accelerated. Genetic change takes millions of years, cultural change generations, and some knowledge now changes within one lifetime. At the current stage, the most complex and resilient structures in the known universe continue to extend. Humans are the present leading edge of that extension on Earth, **but neither its endpoint nor its purpose.** Along the path of human knowledge we can observe how what remained from the past changes the range that later people can encounter and explore.

### Several senses of meaning

Once humans appeared, asking about meaning became possible. People ask about the origin and structure of the world and turn the question back towards themselves, considering how to understand a finite life. From rocks to sentient life to people who can ask these questions, “meaning” has several senses.

1. **An assigned purpose.** The universe has no predetermined purpose. People can set purposes in their own lives.
2. **A place in evolution.** Rocks and minerals form part of the environment of living things; plants turn sunlight into energy usable by organisms. In this sense everything has a place. Many structures formed before us became conditions for later life.
3. **Good and bad as experienced.** What contact with the world means for a sentient being must be understood from its experience. Even if animals cannot say the word meaning, pain and pleasure are real for them.
4. **Asking about and creating meaning.** People organise experience through language, look back over their lives, imagine the future and know that they will die. They therefore ask what they value and how they want to live. We ordinarily ask what things are for, extend that question to natural objects, and finally ask it of ourselves. [Kelemen on children's teleological explanations](https://doi.org/10.1037/0012-1649.35.6.1440)

In summary:

> **Everything has a place in evolution; sentient life also experiences good and bad; and humanity is the part of the universe that has begun to ask about and create meaning.**

We are not observers outside the universe. Our thoughts, feelings, science, art and creations also belong to its unfolding history.

### Life and death, and cosmic awakening

The cycle of life and death can be expressed in one sentence:

> **Life moves towards death; death makes life whole.**

Life is the process of writing a unique chapter that will eventually be complete. Death completes that particular process and opens space and conditions for later life. Although a life ends, its effects may remain in other lives, relationships, knowledge, works and civilisations and become parts of later structures.

The meaning of life is therefore not only to resist its end, but to make its finite process as rich, deep and creative as possible. Our small cycles of life and death participate in the world's longer generation and transformation.

“Cosmic awakening” need not mean that the universe as a whole has a consciousness resembling ours. It can mean this:

> **The universe evolved life capable of knowing, understanding and reflecting on the universe. A part of the universe began to know the universe itself.**

Carl Sagan expressed a related thought in *Cosmos*. [The Carl Sagan archive at the Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/static/collections/finding-our-place-in-the-cosmos-with-carl-sagan/about-this-collection/overview.html)

From the position of Section 1, this sentence has another depth. “Universe” in the title means the information field as a whole. Matter is a stable pattern in information, and thought is a pattern capable of representing other patterns (Section 6). When humans discover mathematics, logic and physical laws, a structure at the shallowest level—the material universe—re-condenses the two deepest levels within itself. Information recognises information; manifestation recognises the source of manifestation. What awakens is therefore not merely one species in a material universe. Through one of its manifestations, the information field begins to know itself:

> **The information field manifests as the world, and the world in turn recognises the information field.**

No purpose is required. Manifestation did not occur in order to be known; one manifestation simply left structures capable of knowing it. Because mathematics and logic remain the same in every manifestation, knowledge in every cycle encounters the same Gödel boundary (Section 4). The information field continues, manifestation follows manifestation, and knowing likewise continues without an endpoint.

### Knowing and changing: the coupling of material and thought structures

“A part of the universe began to know the universe itself” can be made more concrete. A structure appeared within the material universe that contains models of the material universe, including its generative rules. Human brains and human knowledge contain physical laws, cosmic history and accounts of star formation. Part of the universe has condensed the universe's generative rules. This is the deepest level of condensation: what is condensed is no longer only phenomena, but rules that generate phenomena.

At the level of reflection described in Section 6, a system represents itself and self-reference appears—the site of Gödelian incompleteness. In Section 4 the Gödel spiral was still an analogy about cosmic laws. In beings able to know themselves, it appears for the first time inside a concrete system. A knower can never know itself completely, so knowing can never be complete. This claim has theorem-level support; see “From distinction to incompleteness” at the end of Section 4. Awakening is therefore not a completed state but a process without an end.

Knowledge is not detached from matter. Within a material universe every thought is a state of a brain, and every item of knowledge is stored in a material carrier—a neuron, a page or a hard drive. Under the definition in Section 6, thought is not another substance outside matter but a special material structure: deeply organised and capable of representing other structures. This is consistent with Section 1: matter consists of stable patterns in information, and thought is a pattern that can represent other patterns.

Matter and thought are coupled in both directions. Matter constrains thought. The human brain is about two per cent of body mass but consumes about twenty per cent of the body's energy. Landauer's principle in physics says that erasing one bit of information must release at least a determinate amount of heat: information processing is never free. Destroy the carrier and the thought disappears unless it has already been transferred elsewhere. This is one reason the “living seed” matters.

Thought in turn changes matter, with enormous leverage. It can act only through bodies and tools, but a small pattern can direct a great flow of matter: a blueprint determines how much steel a bridge uses; a genome determines how a tree organises tens of tonnes of matter. Information acts as a constraint that guides flows of matter and energy. One estimate found that around 2020 the mass of human-made materials—concrete, metals, plastics and buildings—surpassed the mass of all living biomass on Earth. This is consistent with the Hierarchical Causal–Control hypothesis proposed by the author: higher-level information structures control lower-level physical processes through constraints.

At the scale of the material universe, this influence remains tiny in absolute terms, but it has leverage and is growing. At the same time a transition is visible. The cosmic star-formation rate peaked roughly ten billion years ago and has since declined to about one tenth of its maximum. Cosmic expansion also changed: it began accelerating about six billion years ago, and from roughly four billion years ago dark energy exceeded matter in the cosmic energy budget; it now accounts for about seventy per cent. At the largest scale, gravitational aggregation is yielding to accelerated expansion: the material universe is moving from condensation towards expansion at this scale. Physical condensation is slowing while the condensation of knowledge accelerates. The 2025 version distinguished two kinds of complexity: manifest physical complexity can rise and fall, while the depth left by history can continue to grow. The leading edge of evolution is moving from physical structures to structures of knowledge. No one arranged this; the new carrier simply condenses faster.

Accelerated expansion does not prove that the universe will expand forever. That depends on whether dark energy is truly constant. In 2025, baryon acoustic oscillation measurements in DESI's second data release remained compatible on their own with the standard cosmological model; when combined with cosmic-microwave-background and supernova data, several dataset combinations preferred time-varying dark energy. This is a hint, not direct proof that dark energy is weakening. In one axion dark-energy model, using best-fit parameters, the authors estimate a total cosmic lifetime of about thirty-three billion years before expansion stops and collapse begins. This is model-dependent, not a direct conclusion of DESI. The framework here does not depend on how this cycle ends (Section 5), but it does imply one point: contraction would not be condensation. Stars can drive planets, life and thought because energy flows from hot stars into a colder environment. Expansion cools the background radiation and sustains a large temperature gradient. During prolonged contraction the background would warm, and the usable gradient would eventually shrink and disappear. Collapse would erase distinctions rather than condense them. In the language of this essay, expansion is a condition of condensation. Condensation without expansion does not produce deeper condensation; it stops condensation. The universe would not contract “in order to condense.” If contraction occurs, condensation would first slow and then stop.

Why do new carriers appear? Condensation reaches the capacity limits of its carrier. Individual memory cannot hold enough, so writing appears. Hand copying is slow and error-prone, so printing appears. No person can read everything, so disciplines and scientific communities appear. Calculation exceeds human capacity, so computers appear. No person can read and connect knowledge across all fields, so artificial intelligence appears. A bottleneck is often broken not by exerting more force inside the old carrier, but by changing carriers. This is the epistemic form of Section 4's claim that when adjustment within existing rules stops producing novelty, change is more likely to come from changing the rules. Each new carrier does not eliminate the bottleneck; it moves it to the next tightest constraint.

Even before artificial intelligence, condensation using only humans as carriers showed signs of a bottleneck. Economist Benjamin Jones found that as knowledge accumulates, researchers take longer to reach the frontier, the age of first major invention rises, and teams grow larger. Bloom and colleagues found that maintaining the same growth in research output requires increasing numbers of researchers. Under the mechanism proposed here, once AI relaxes the “too much to read and connect” bottleneck, the tightest constraints will move to contact with reality (experiments and field tests), verification of truth and falsity, asking good questions and judging what matters, and eventually to the physical limits of energy and computation.

This also answers a question left by Section 5. The “richness” of a material universe is neither physical structure alone nor thought alone but **the depth of its whole history**. Bennett's “logical depth” is a useful concept: the complexity of a structure depends on the length of the incompressible process required to generate it from the simplest starting point. A hot gas contains vast numbers of molecular collisions but has little logical depth. A living being, a book or a theory is logically deep. Thought structures are among the deepest material structures we know, and they continually generate other deep material structures. If the conjecture in Section 5 is correct and the starting point left at the end of a cosmic cycle depends on the depth of its history, then the appearance of knowers affects the starting point of the next cycle. This does not mean that the universe produced us for a purpose. Knowers appeared contingently; once present, they became the deepest part of this cycle's history.

The leverage itself is neither good nor evil. It can build and destroy. The discussion of protection in Section 8 therefore acquires a deeper reason.

The material universe is the form of manifestation we know. We cannot know whether it is the only form available to the information field.

“A part of the universe begins to know the universe itself” thus has two meanings. It knows the universe, and on the basis of that knowledge it can reorganise part of the universe. **Knowing and changing are two sides of the same coupling.**

## 8. What, then, should people do?

An account of how the world evolves answers a factual question. To say what people should do requires stating what we value. Hume reminds us that the move from facts to “ought” requires reasons. My starting commitments are these: **the suffering borne by people and other sentient life deserves concern; the choices of others deserve respect; and later generations' opportunities to live, learn and explore deserve protection.** [Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature*, Book III](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/3/1/1)

From these commitments, I choose several practices:

1. **Reduce avoidable suffering.** When deciding, ask who bears the harm and what it means for people or other sentient life.
2. **Do work that later people can continue.** Leave knowledge and methods they can understand, use and improve.
3. **Protect what is difficult to rebuild.** Protect human lives and the conditions that sustain living, transmission and cooperation.
4. **Keep knowledge open.** Re-examine judgement when new experience arrives and leave room for disagreement and questions.
5. **Use education to help others form capabilities of their own.** Help them judge independently and carry what they learn into new problems.

These practices sometimes conflict. If long-term construction imposes present suffering, we must ask who pays the cost and whether a less harmful path exists. I discuss how such disagreements form in [*Where Does Ethical Disagreement Come From?*](../framework/methods/ethical-disagreement-map.md).

### Why goodness is worth carrying forward

Looking back, evolution can seem always to lead towards good. That need not mean that the universe has a purpose directed towards goodness. This essay itself rests on earlier discovery, recording, education and cooperation. **Beings able to ask about good and evil and to look back over history can appear only where constructive processes continued. Where everything was destroyed, no observer remains.** We live in the first kind of place, so looking back we naturally see traces of good everywhere. This is survivorship bias.

Survivorship bias also warns us that our ability to review this path presupposes that it continued until now. Estimating risk from a history experienced by survivors may underestimate catastrophes that would eliminate the observer if they occurred. [Research on observer-selection effects](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6924.2010.01460.x)

The fact that knowledge remains available for inheritance shows that some protective actions worked. What survived a particular disaster, what was lost and what people did must still be examined separately. In those concrete choices we can see how goodwill produced results that could be passed on.

### The layered accumulation of resilience

Cells repair some damage, bodies maintain temperature as environments change, and immune systems resist many threats. Each is an example of a structure maintaining or recovering function under disturbance. In human societies, writing, institutions and science add another form of protection. People can study risks that have not yet occurred, discuss responses and act together in advance.

More resilient structures are more likely to continue; what continues becomes the foundation of the next level, so resilience accumulates layer by layer (see Section 2, “Stability, modules and destruction”). **When a method of protection can be preserved, learned and improved, protective capability itself can accumulate.** Resilience at different levels has different conditions. A structure that withstands one disturbance may fail under another.

Before humans, these were properties of structures, not intentions. DNA repair does not “want” to protect a cell; it merely makes cells possessing it more likely to persist. Only with humans do words such as protection and choice literally apply, because people have purposes.

Nor does the universe favour us. The same universe produces asteroids, viruses and supernovae. Section 2 showed that destruction and accumulation arise from the same mechanism. The tendency can be interrupted at any time. Even if the information field continues and material universes follow one another, continuation of the whole does not guarantee continuation of a locality. Earth can still be completely interrupted. That is precisely why protection matters. We can therefore say:

> **The universe has no purpose, but processes of selection repeatedly leave more resilient structures; with humans, resilience can for the first time be realised through conscious anticipation and choice.**

Human goodwill can also be understood this way. Compassion and connection can calm a person, and the brain records that state as rewarding, making the person more willing to repeat it. For a cooperative species, brains with such rewards are more likely to continue. But the reward is likewise a result of selection, not a cosmic arrangement; human brains can also reward revenge and conquest. Goodwill can be cultivated in practice. One study found that after particular compassion training, participants reported more positive feelings when confronted with others' suffering. How goodwill becomes a habit and then an act of protection must still be understood within human life and relationships. [Klimecki and colleagues on compassion training](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nst060)

### Knowledge changes outcomes

Knowledge of a situation can change the situation itself. If a well-founded warning changes human action, the expected harm may diminish. In the 1970s scientists warned that certain chemicals damaged the ozone layer. Countries adopted the Montreal Protocol in 1987. International scientific assessments find that actions under the Protocol reduced controlled ozone-depleting substances and promoted recovery of the ozone layer. [Montreal Protocol](https://ozone.unep.org/treaties/montreal-protocol) · [WMO and UNEP assessment](https://csl.noaa.gov/assessments/ozone/2022/executivesummary/)

This can be understood as a shift from inside the system to a meta-level. A condensing system turns towards itself; knowledge condensed about itself changes its own trajectory. People recognise that their activity changes the environment and use that knowledge to change their activity. Knowing that something difficult to rebuild may be lost can become a reason to protect it in advance.

### What I choose to believe

I choose to believe that good will ultimately exceed evil. I think of particular people in successive generations: caring for those before them while protecting things for strangers who will come later. Some protect life, some preserve records, and some teach their craft and judgement. Such actions allow some achievements to survive destruction and give later people the chance to carry the goodwill forward.

But this belief is not a guarantee. It is a choice to become one of the people who make it true.

I call the conditions that allow life, knowledge and hope to begin again after catastrophe a “living seed.”

> **The universe has no purpose and does not guarantee the victory of good. But it evolved beings capable of seeing risk and choosing to protect the “living seed.” This may be the most literal meaning of cosmic awakening: not that the universe helps us, but that we become the part of the universe capable of helping it continue to generate.**

## 9. Relations to earlier thought

This essay continues a tradition that understands existence and meaning through cosmic evolution. It asks a further question: in a world without predetermined purpose, where construction and destruction occur together, how do existing structures preserve the past and open new possibilities? When people recognise that their choices affect this process, what responsibility can they assume?

Compared with most works in this tradition, the essay emphasises eight points:

1. **It consistently denies cosmic purpose while explaining why outcomes often look purposeful.** Resilient structures are more likely to persist, and observers face survivorship bias. Efficiency is a consequence of existence, not the reason something was selected.
2. **One mechanism explains accumulation and destruction.** Condensation leaves stable modules; expansion brings modules together and recombines them. Destruction is often a form of expansion and a product of deep condensation; its consequences depend on the ratio between the scale of destruction and the scale of modules (Section 2).
3. **Stars, life and humans are understood through universal generative rules with tendencies, while phenotypes depend on parameters and history** (Sections 6 and 7).
4. **Thought receives a functional definition, and the two-way coupling between material and thought structures is made explicit.** Thought is a material structure that models other structures, simulates offline and guides action. It is constrained by matter and reorganises matter with enormous leverage (Sections 6 and 7).
5. **It steps outside an existing frame and derives the absence of an endpoint to knowledge from “to exist is to be distinct.”** A problem insoluble within a system must be viewed from a higher level. No material universe can explain its own laws from within; there is no final cycle, and heat death ends one manifestation rather than existence. The same distinction that permits manifestation prevents complete self-inclusive knowledge, as follows from Lawvere's fixed-point theorem. Every finite condensation leaves a remainder, which is also why condensation and expansion never stop (Sections 2, 4 and 7).
6. **It proposes a conjecture about physical constants.** Constants are parameters left by a previous cycle, while the magnitude of variation depends on the previous outcome. Without a designer or competition among many universes, parameters can concentrate in regions compatible with structure formation (Section 5).
7. **It posits no endpoint and grounds meaning and ethics in choices people can make.** There is only a leading edge that continues and may be interrupted. The essay marks which claims are positions, which are observable, which are conjectures and which are beliefs.
8. **It makes the connection between ontology and practice calculable.** From “to exist is to be distinct,” through “condensation combines distinctions,” the argument reaches a concrete design judgement: when simplifying a system, which distinctions must remain so that paths of recovery are not cut off? This can be calculated and falsified. The complete derivation appears on this site in *From Cosmic Awakening to Constraint Theory: Different Levels of the Same Structure* and *Simplification and Paths of Recovery*.

**Spinoza is especially close to this essay on whether nature has a predetermined purpose.** In the *Ethics* he discusses how people project their purposes onto nature and how understanding nature as a whole changes human life. Everything in his system follows necessarily. This essay instead emphasises historical contingency in particular evolutionary paths and a cyclic picture without a predetermined endpoint. In Part V, Spinoza also writes that the mind's intellectual love of God—that is, nature—is part of the love by which God loves himself. Its shape is close to the information field knowing itself through manifestation. Schelling and Hegel also imagined the whole coming to know itself through human consciousness. Their pictures, however, contain a necessary process or endpoint—Hegel's Absolute Spirit—whereas this essay has neither purpose nor a moment of completion.

**Cantor is closest to this essay on whether the whole can be known.** He distinguished three levels of existence—the material universe, the human mind and God—and held that the “absolute infinity” beyond every transfinite infinity can only be acknowledged and never known. This is almost the structure of “existence has its whole; knowledge has no endpoint.” Cantor assigned the highest level to God. Here it is a purposeless information field, and its unknowability follows from distinction and self-representation rather than divine transcendence (end of Section 4). Grim's Cantorian argument that truth makes the universe open and incomplete is likewise related.

One historical point matters. Gödel's incompleteness theorem appeared in 1931, after Spinoza, Schelling and Hegel. They could not use it to explain why the whole's knowledge of itself cannot be completed. The thought of every period is limited by what that period has already condensed. This essay's synthesis likewise depends on its time. Earlier thinkers left enough modules—Spinoza's nature, Gödel's incompleteness, Simon's hierarchy, Bennett's logical depth and Holling's adaptive cycle—scattered across philosophy, mathematics, physics, biology and cognitive science. Artificial intelligence lets modules from different fields meet more quickly. Many comparisons with earlier thought were found and checked in discussions with AI. In the essay's own terms, that is an act of condensation: existing modules meet through a new carrier and are fixed into a new structure. Under the definition in Section 6, these AI systems themselves contain part of the structure of thought. Their participation in condensation is also part of the information field knowing itself through manifestation. No one arranged this; the conditions happened to be present in this period. [Spinoza, *Ethics*, Part I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm)

Sagan places human knowledge back within the universe; Simon explains how stable intermediate structures let work survive interruption; Merton studies how people's understanding of a situation changes the situation. These three lines connect respectively to the essay's discussions of knowledge, protection and action. Further comparisons appear in the guide at the end.

## 10. Open questions

Cosmic awakening is not a completed system. It retains fundamental questions:

- Under what conditions does expansion produce enough new material for another condensation?
- What kinds of condensation preserve the past and also generate new capabilities?
- Why does a system harden, and how can it reopen possibilities?
- How does history enter the future? Could the universe possess some form of memory?
- Are life, cognition and civilisation contingencies of cosmic evolution or expressions of a deeper process?
- What structures can continue after destruction? Section 2 gives a first answer: it depends on the ratio between the scale of destruction and the scale of modules, and on whether copies are distributed. Can this be quantified and tested separately for stars, life and knowledge?
- Did the cycles have a beginning? If the spiral has a direction, is an infinite past coherent?
- How could the richness of structures in one material universe affect the next cycle's initial state and parameters? Do physical constants change between cycles?
- Where does consciousness—subjective experience—come from? The functional definition of thought does not answer this.
- Is the material universe the information field's only form of manifestation?
- How should the “depth” of the history of one material universe be measured? Is logical depth suitable?
- Is historical depth measured across the whole or by the deepest locality? Accelerated expansion divides the universe into regions that will never reach one another. If one region develops deep knowledge while most of the universe is sparse gas, is the cycle deep or shallow? Under a maximum measure, knowers strongly affect the next cycle; under a global measure, local knowledge has almost no effect. The Section 7 claim that knowers affect the next starting point depends on this question. There is currently no basis for choosing between the measures.
- Will self-reflective beings appear elsewhere? No extraterrestrial-intelligence signal has been confirmed within the region searched. What do existing searches tell us? This is one question raised by the Fermi paradox. [Research on SETI search coverage](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/aae099)

### Interfaces for physics

This is a philosophical framework and does not answer questions for physics. It should nevertheless identify points where future physics could connect to or overturn it. The following are properties derived from the framework or inherent in its position.

1. **Regions compatible with structure formation should be broad “foothills.”** Differential retention in Section 5 conditionally predicts that the suitable region of parameter space must have a broad transition zone. Small variations can then lead parameters towards it and keep them there. If the region is an isolated needle point, the mechanism is weak. Adams (2019) finds that stars can form across wide ranges of many constants, which is consistent with this condition. If suitable regions prove extremely narrow and isolated, the mechanism becomes implausible.
2. **Inheritance requires a carrier.** Differential retention requires the outcome of one cycle to affect parameters of the next. Possible candidates in physics include scalar fields that determine physical constants; the cosmological constant, whose theoretical estimate and observed value differ by roughly 120 orders of magnitude; and black holes, whose interiors in some theories bounce into new universes. Steinhardt and Turok have shown a process in which the cosmological constant declines across cycles and remains longest at small values. If physics ultimately shows that physical constants cannot change in any sense, the Section 5 conjecture fails.
3. **Information should be more fundamental than spacetime.** This is the position of Section 1. Black-hole entropy scales with surface area, inspiring the holographic principle that information in a region is determined by its boundary. Some approaches to quantum gravity treat spacetime as emerging from quantum entanglement. These directions are consistent with the essay but are unsettled. If physics shows that spacetime is fundamental and cannot emerge from anything else, this position is challenged.

### Predictions derived from the framework

A framework that explains only the past can explain everything and therefore says nothing. The following table gathers predictions distributed across the essay, states their sources and identifies what would overturn them. Their status differs: some have evidence, some are conditional, some require long tests, and one has theorem-level support.

| Source | Prediction | What would overturn it |
|---|---|---|
| Section 2: the result of destruction depends on the ratio between the scale of destruction and the scale of modules | Recovery after major destruction comes from lower-level modules that remain and often produces new forms; for example, surviving lineages diversify after mass extinction | Recovery after major destruction is unrelated to the number and kinds of modules that remain |
| Sections 2 and 4: condensed structures must return to contact with the world | A system that only condenses and encounters nothing new will degrade; for example, successive AI generations trained only on their predecessors' outputs will drift away from reality | A system relying indefinitely on its own output, without external checks, continues improving |
| Section 6: generative rules are universal and have tendencies | Under similar conditions, similar structures independently recur through convergence; life discovered elsewhere will have modular, hierarchical organisation and a carrier for transmitting information | Life is found with none of these organisational features |
| Section 7: physical condensation slows while epistemic condensation accelerates | Condensation at the level of knowledge continues accelerating, and new carriers such as AI accelerate it further | Knowledge growth stagnates or reverses for a long period for reasons other than external destruction |
| End of Section 4: knowledge has no endpoint (theorem-level support) | Any supposedly complete “theory of everything” leaves questions about itself that it cannot answer | A theory appears that completely explains everything including itself |
| Section 8: knowledge changes outcomes | A warning that is taken seriously and acted upon reduces the harm it predicts, as in ozone recovery | A well-founded warning is adopted and implemented but makes no difference to the harm |
| Section 5: differential retention (conditional on cosmic cycles) | The structure-compatible region of constant space is a broad foothill rather than an isolated needle point | The suitable region proves extremely narrow and isolated |
| Section 7: carrier bottlenecks and changing carriers | After AI relaxes the “too much to read and connect” bottleneck, contact with the real world—experiments and field tests—and verification become relatively scarcer and more expensive | After widespread AI adoption, experiments and verification do not become relatively scarcer and condensation itself remains the main bottleneck |
| Section 1: information precedes spacetime | Physics increasingly supports emergent spacetime | Spacetime is shown to be fundamental and irreducible |

These predictions are general. Operational predictions for particular problems require the tools of this site's constraint theory (see *From Cosmic Awakening to Constraint Theory: Different Levels of the Same Structure*).

The framework's central legacy is not a closed conclusion but a dynamic picture:

> **The world continually produces new possibilities. Some condense into structures; those structures preserve the past, generate new capabilities, and become starting points for another round of possibility.**

This idea is a “seed.” Every act of thought, discussion, criticism and creation is part of cosmic awakening.

Another study uses human knowledge to turn information condensation and expansion into a testable mechanism. The Hierarchical Causal–Control hypothesis (HCC) begins from local systems and proposes testable claims about how structures form, persist and generate new capabilities. See [*The Hierarchical Causal–Control Hypothesis*](../framework/foundations/hierarchical-causal-control.md).

## Afterword: a guide to earlier thought

This essay grew from my long-standing reflections on the universe, evolution and the meaning of life. While writing, I repeatedly discussed the ideas with AI and gradually found that some resembled earlier thought, so I brought the relevant works into comparison. My ideas are also shaped by education, reading and personal experience, influences that cannot always be traced individually. The guide records the connections I can presently identify and the questions I still want to pursue from them.

### 1. Alternation between aggregation and separation

- **Laozi's “being and non-being give rise to each other”** (*Daodejing*, Chapter 2): being and non-being arise through mutual dependence. Section 1 dissolves “why something rather than nothing?” under the definition that existence is distinction; the two can be read together.
- **Empedocles** used the alternation of Love and Strife to explain aggregation and separation. This essay uses a related alternation and asks how one aggregation leaves conditions for the next. [Fragments of Empedocles](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Empedocles)
- **The *Xici* commentary to the *Yijing*** presents waxing and waning yin and yang through the alternation of sun and moon, heat and cold. This essay uses condensation and expansion to ask how structures formed through alternation accumulate. [*Xici*, Part II](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/易傳/繫辭下)
- **Herbert Spencer's *First Principles*** contrasts the integration of matter and concomitant dissipation of motion with dissolution. This distinction can be read alongside the essay's account of formation, maintenance and breakdown. [*First Principles*](https://constitution.org/2-Authors/hs/first_prin.htm)

### 2. Cosmic evolution, complexity and consciousness

- **Teilhard de Chardin's *The Phenomenon of Man*** connects complexity, consciousness and the Omega Point. This essay retains a link between complexity and consciousness while keeping evolution open.
- **Whitehead's *Process and Reality*** understands reality through process and becoming, but gives God and “initial aims” explicit roles. This essay places formation, persistence and change in a philosophical picture without predetermined purpose.
- **Carl Sagan's *Cosmos*** presents humanity as one way the cosmos knows itself. This essay asks how knowledge of our situation changes action and what may happen later. [Library of Congress Sagan archive](https://www.loc.gov/static/collections/finding-our-place-in-the-cosmos-with-carl-sagan/about-this-collection/overview.html)
- **David Christian's *Maps of Time*** places cosmic, biological and human history on one timescale and studies conditions for new levels. This essay asks how formed structures are preserved and reused. [University of California Press](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/maps-of-time/epub-pdf)
- **Eric Chaisson's *Cosmic Evolution*** compares complexity through energy-flow density per unit mass. It offers a quantitative route; this essay poses philosophical questions through information condensation and expansion.
- **Schelling** held that nature reaches self-consciousness in humanity: “Nature is visible Spirit; Spirit is invisible Nature.” **Hegel's *Phenomenology of Spirit*** describes Spirit reaching absolute self-knowledge through history. Both resemble the information field knowing itself through manifestation, but this essay has no endpoint or purpose.
- **Ray Kurzweil's *The Singularity Is Near*** calls the expansion of intelligence into the universe “the universe wakes up.” This essay adopts the phrase to ask how self-knowledge relates to action.

### 3. Information and the universe

- **Poincaré** and **Worrall**: science knows only relations (epistemic structural realism). **Ladyman and Ross, *Every Thing Must Go***: the world itself consists of relations and structures (ontic structural realism). Section 1 adopts the latter position.
- **John Wheeler** explored information and physical reality through *It from Bit*.
- **Seth Lloyd** understands the universe through computation and information processing.
- **Stephen Wolfram** studies how simple programs generate complex behaviour.
- **Leibniz**, in his correspondence with Clarke, argues that time and space are relations among things rather than independent containers. This agrees with Section 1's “space is the structure of relations among events.”
- **Carlo Rovelli, *The Order of Time***, and **Julian Barbour, *The End of Time*** argue that time may not be fundamental. **Sean Carroll, *From Eternity to Here*** discusses the arrow of time and entropy. This essay distinguishes physical time from an order of dependence and relates time's direction to entropy and accumulated records.
- **Mark Van Raamsdonk** and related work on building spacetime from quantum entanglement represent an approach in which spacetime may be emergent. This agrees directionally with the priority of the information field, but remains unsettled.
- **Max Tegmark's mathematical-universe hypothesis** says that all mathematical structures exist and ours is one of them. It is close to the substrate containing all mathematical and logical possibilities. Tegmark treats all structures as equally existing. Here there is a process of manifestation: only self-sustaining structures stand, while invariant mathematics and logic and cycle-dependent physical laws produce successive evolution.

Wheeler, Lloyd and Wolfram were the three information-related sources cited in the 2025 version. This essay asks how information expands, condenses into structures, and changes conditions for later evolution.

### 4. Purposeless nature and meaning

- **Spinoza's *Ethics***, Part I, discusses projection of purpose onto nature, while Part V discusses the intellectual love of God. The first helps identify teleological misreading; together they show why the essay values both natural regularity and historical conditions.
- **Ursula Goodenough's *The Sacred Depths of Nature*** seeks awe, belonging and ethical concern within scientific nature. This essay asks how those concerns become things people preserve. [Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/book/52725)
- **Sartre** emphasises how people shape themselves through choice. This essay also locates meaning in choice and places that capacity within the evolution of life and cognition.
- **Kelemen's** work finds that children more readily than adults explain natural features by their uses. It informs the discussion of human preference for purpose explanations.

### 5. Structure, selection and contingency

- **Lawvere's fixed-point theorem (1969)**: where a fixed-point-free negation exists, no system can completely represent all its own properties. **Yanofsky (2003)** explains how the results of Cantor, Russell, **Gödel (1931)**, Tarski and **Turing (1936)** are special cases. **Turing (1939)** and **Feferman (1962)** studied progressions strengthened by successive consistency statements. **Wolpert (2008)** shows that no physical inference device can predict or know everything including itself. Section 4 uses these results to argue that the distinction enabling manifestation also prevents complete knowledge.
- **Goodstein (1944)** and **Kirby and Paris (1982)**: a true arithmetic statement using only addition, subtraction and exponentiation is unprovable in Peano arithmetic and requires transfinite ordinals. It illustrates why stepping outside reveals the whole.
- **Chaitin (1974, 2005)**: information-theoretic incompleteness—finite axioms cover finite information—and the proposal that mathematics add new axioms from experience. Section 4 connects this to the unending alternation of condensation and expansion.
- **Aristotle** accepted potential but not actual infinity. **Cantor** developed transfinite numbers and defended actual infinity while distinguishing unknowable absolute infinity and three levels: material universe, human mind and God. **Zermelo (1930)** treated the set-theoretic universe as a potentially infinite ascent of actually infinite levels. **Grim, *The Incomplete Universe* (1991)** argues that there is no set of all truths and that truth leaves the universe essentially open. **Hilbert, “On the Infinite” (1926)** and **Ellis, Meissner and Nicolai (2018)** address actual infinity in physical reality. Section 4 maps three infinities onto the information field, mathematics and material universes.
- **Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form* (1969)** constructs a logical algebra from the primitive act of drawing a distinction. This is close to “to exist is to be distinct” and the 2025 claim that two is the first bit.
- **Wheeler's resetting of constants** and **Smolin's cosmological natural selection**: Wheeler randomises constants at each bounce; Smolin transmits varying constants to daughter universes and selects through their number. Differential retention lies between them: heredity and variation, but outcome-dependent variation without population competition.
- **Stress-induced mutagenesis in bacteria** and **Rechenberg's evolution strategy** offer biological and computational analogies for raising variation under stress and reducing step size after success.
- **Borde, Guth and Vilenkin** relate past incompleteness to averaged expansion. **Fred Adams** warns against overstating how narrowly fine-tuned constants must be.
- **Burbidge, Burbidge, Fowler and Hoyle (1957)** systematically explained stellar nucleosynthesis. Section 6 treats stellar generations—gravitational condensation, nucleosynthesis, explosive expansion and new condensation—as a spiral within the material universe.
- **Prigogine** showed how energy flows far from equilibrium can produce ordered dissipative structures. **Jeremy England** discusses self-replication and dissipation statistically. Both provide scientific background for order arising in energy flows.
- **Simon, “The Architecture of Complexity”**: stable intermediate components permit complex systems to be built in layers and resumed after interruption. Section 2 uses this to explain modules, hierarchical accumulation and the living seed.
- **Holling's adaptive cycle and panarchy**: ecosystems move through exploitation, conservation, release and reorganisation. Accumulation and tight coupling create rigidity; release is collapse; reorganisation builds new structures from released resources; nested slow levels provide memory for faster levels. This is close to condensation, over-condensation, destruction as expansion, re-condensation, and the ratio between destructive and module scales.
- **Schumpeter's creative destruction** describes new economic combinations replacing old structures.
- **Gould's *Wonderful Life*** and **Conway Morris's *Life's Solution*** emphasise contingency and convergence respectively. This essay separates recurrent structures under similar conditions from paths shaped by particular histories.
- **Smolin's cosmological natural selection** and **Penrose's conformal cyclic cosmology** offer different accounts of inheritance and cycles. In **Steinhardt and Turok (2006)**, the cosmological constant decreases across cycles and remains longest at small values, making it the closest physical model to differential retention.
- **Kelvin and Clausius** developed the nineteenth-century picture of cosmic heat death. The cyclic model of **Steinhardt and Turok**, like Penrose and Smolin, places universes after a universe at the physical level. This essay instead steps to the information-field level because a system cannot account for itself from within.
- **Aaronson, Carroll and Ouellette** provide a concrete coffee-automaton example of complexity rising and falling.

### 6. Meaning and ethics

- **Cummins** understands the function of a part as its role in a system. This can be read with “place in evolution.”
- **Nagel** discusses the irreducibility of animal subjective experience. **Bentham and Peter Singer** bring animal suffering into ethics. These correspond to experienced good and bad and reducing avoidable suffering.
- **Hume** argues that ought does not follow from is. This essay therefore states its ethical commitments and offers only conditional directions.
- **Hans Jonas's ethics of responsibility** and **Aldo Leopold's land ethic** stress long-term consequences for future generations and living communities, corresponding to continuable work and protection of what is hard to rebuild.
- **Popper's** fallibilism and open society correspond to keeping knowledge open.
- **Kenneth Craik** proposed that minds build small-scale models of reality to predict. This is close to Section 6's functional definition of thought.
- **Daniel Dennett's tower of generate-and-test** moves from genetic and learned trial and error to internal simulation and language-and-tool-assisted learning, closely matching heredity, learning, thought, reflection and knowledge.
- **Charles Bennett's logical depth** measures complexity by the length of the incompressible process required to generate a structure. Section 7 uses it for the depth of cosmic history.
- **Rolf Landauer's principle** gives an unavoidable thermodynamic cost to erasing information, a precise example of matter constraining thought.
- **Howard Pattee** and **Terrence Deacon** study how symbols control matter and how information and meaning constrain physical processes, close to the claim that thought structures reorganise matter through constraints.
- **Merton's self-fulfilling prophecy** shows how beliefs about a situation change it. This essay emphasises the inverse: a warning taken seriously may prevent its prediction.
- **Ćirković, Sandberg and Bostrom's anthropic shadow** concerns how observer survival limits estimates of catastrophic risk.
