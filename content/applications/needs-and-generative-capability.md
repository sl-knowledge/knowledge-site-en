## One-page guide

**Research question:** when important needs cannot be sustainably covered by current capabilities and support conditions, why do some situations produce learning and exploration, others reliance and commitment, and others contraction or avoidance?

This article proposes a candidate explanatory framework: identify needs, check which paths can cover them within a specified horizon, and compare actually feasible paths with perceived ones. Behaviour may be jointly influenced by uncovered needs, available support, buffers, transition time and perceptual error. This is not a unified law of all behaviour.

Four distinctions are central:

1. **Capability, support channels and actual outcomes differ.** Skills do not produce income independently of their environment; support can also help develop lasting capability.
2. **Actual and perceived gaps differ.** Perception affects action, but correcting it cannot replace missing resources.
3. **One infeasible path does not mean all paths are closed.** When capability cannot be built in time, bridging support, combined paths and revised targets must still be checked.
4. **Describing a mechanism differs from evaluating a choice.** Reliance is not automatically a lack of autonomy, contraction is not automatically failure, and exploration is not automatically better.

**Knowledge condensation:** needs, capability beliefs, external support and recovery time are brought into one path-analysis table. They remain different objects, rather than being reduced to a score for “independence” or “courage”.

| Previously separate question | Shared structure | Distinction retained |
|---|---|---|
| What is needed, and why maintain an arrangement? | Needs and alternative paths | Need importance is not belief truth |
| Why not try despite capability, or overreach without it? | Differences between actual and perceived paths | Changed perception is not changed resources |
| Rely on support or develop capability? | Combinations of capability and support | Autonomy is not self-sufficiency |
| Can the present arrangement last until transition? | Buffers and path-formation time | Everyday maintenance is not recovery after disturbance |

Section 2 supplies a record card; section 7 states conditional propositions. Related work and references are at the end.

## 1. Distinguishing the objects

### 1.1 Needs: analytical dimensions, not a fixed ladder

Depending on the task, needs may be distinguished as survival and safety, order and resources, belonging and relationships, recognition and identity, meaning and self-transcendence. This is a working classification, not an exhaustive list or a necessary sequence of satisfaction. Needs can conflict and priorities can change.[1]

For each need, specify what must be maintained, its minimum conditions and the horizon. “High meaning” cannot simply be subtracted from “low income”.

### 1.2 Buffers, generative capability and rebuilding capability

| Object | Question | Checks required |
|---|---|---|
| Buffer | How long can the current arrangement be supported after damage? | Accessibility, consumption and simultaneous demands |
| Generative capability | Can required outcomes be produced repeatedly under given conditions? | Skills, tools, organisation, available opportunities and continuing costs |
| Capability to rebuild generative capability | Can an alternative be developed after the original method fails? | Learning, feedback, resource reorganisation, transition time and environmental support |

The latter two are not inherently more reliable than buffers. New capability may arrive too late, and a skill may lose its applicable environment. A relationship network can be a support channel or supply immediately usable assistance; it cannot be counted twice without an explicit accounting distinction.

### 1.3 Own capability, external support and actual coverage

Let $K$ denote capability, $E$ available external support and channels, and $B$ buffers. These symbols are local to this article. Skills, jobs, institutions, groups and cooperation usually produce outcomes jointly: a skill does not guarantee a job, and holding a job does not guarantee independent task capability.

The default object of analysis is therefore **whether a particular path can cover a particular need**, rather than a direct sum of “own capability” and “external provision”.

A local gap can be expressed numerically only when the need dimension, measurement units and horizon are aligned:

$$
G_i(\pi;H)=\max\{0,N_i(H)-Q_i(\pi;H)\}.
$$

Here $N_i(H)$ is the target amount for need $i$ over horizon $H$, and $Q_i(\pi;H)$ is the amount supplied by path $\pi$ on the same basis. The latter depends jointly on capability, support, buffers and environment. Sufficient totals do not guarantee constraints are met at every time; stages must be checked separately where necessary.

Without common units, record “covered”, “uncovered” or “insufficient evidence” rather than forcing a calculation. Without additional assumptions, this article does not adopt a universal formula of needs minus capability minus support.

## 2. A path-analysis card

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Needs and horizon | What must be maintained? What are the minimum conditions, deadline and non-negotiable boundaries? |
| Current coverage | Which capabilities, supports and buffers jointly produce outcomes? Which remain unverified? |
| Actual candidate paths | What conditions are required for self-development, cooperation, bridging support or target revision? |
| Perceived paths | Which paths does the agent recognise and trust? Which are merely imagined or possibly overlooked? |
| Time structure | How long do recognition, initiation, capability formation, transition, stabilisation and verification take? Which can run in parallel? |
| Critical dependencies | Which support interruption would change coverage? Are alternatives genuinely available? |
| Action and feedback | What is the smallest reversible trial? What observation would change the assessment? |
| Rival explanations | Could values, habits, responsibilities or other conditions also explain the behaviour? |

The card supports research and review. It does not automatically score personal choices or treat unknowns as absent.

## 3. Candidate mechanism: how paths influence behaviour

The core hypothesis is: **in a class of situations where need coverage is an important constraint, behaviour changes with perceived uncovered needs, feasible paths and their time costs.** At least three responses are possible, including combinations:

| Path | What it may change | What does not follow directly |
|---|---|---|
| Develop or rebuild capability | New ways of producing outcomes over time | Investment guarantees success or independence from external conditions |
| Obtain, maintain or reorganise support | Bridge transitions and expand feasible paths | Receiving support removes autonomy, or support is necessarily reliable |
| Revise targets and action scope | Reduce resource pressure and reorder priorities | Revision necessarily reflects fear or unreasonable contraction |

One action may belong to several categories. Cooperation supplies support and may build capability. Commitment closes some immediate options while potentially developing long-term knowledge and trust.

Autonomy requires separate examination: can the choice be understood, reviewed and revised; is it coerced; and are its main consequences endorsed by the agent? Autonomy and independence from others are not synonymous, a distinction also compatible with self-determination theory.[3]

### 3.1 How support may become load-bearing

When an arrangement covers an important need and alternatives are hard to obtain, losing it may become more costly. If a premise is considered necessary for maintaining the arrangement, defending it may also serve to preserve support, identity or commitment.

This is a conditional mechanism requiring testing, not an equation in which “strength of commitment equals need value”. Persistence may also reflect evidence assessment, tradition, relationships, moral commitments or other causes. An important need does not guarantee a particular belief.

### 3.2 Actual and misperceived constraints

A path may be conceivable yet unreachable, reachable yet unsustainable, or available but unacceptable because of identity or values. These situations differ.

Low buffers may make caution reasonable; underestimated capability may also exclude feasible trials. Failure to try does not alone distinguish these explanations, and rejecting a path is not necessarily a cognitive error.

## 4. How perception enters the loop

Actual paths are rarely fully known; they are estimated from current evidence. Perception can affect whether a trial occurs. Its feedback may then change capability, support conditions and perception itself.

| Situation | Possible error | Review direction |
|---|---|---|
| Capability or support underestimated | Feasible paths remain untried | Seek evidence through low-cost, reversible tests |
| Capability or support overestimated | Transition costs or failure consequences are neglected | Check boundaries, exit conditions and whether support can be delivered |
| Insufficient information | Error direction is unknown | Preserve uncertainty; do not treat confidence as verification |

Self-efficacy research supplies a comparison for the relationship between capability beliefs and action.[2] The “perceived gap” is not identical to self-efficacy and cannot replace checking actual conditions.

Fear and confidence may provide clues but are not direct readings of gap size. Emotions also reflect bodily states, experience and context. Understanding constraints may help action without making emotions disappear or establishing that someone's capability is inadequate.

## 5. Turning points: which paths are still timely?

External change may affect income, relationships, identity, targets and support simultaneously. It may alter current states or longer-lasting preferences, institutions and capability-formation conditions. One cannot stipulate that a shock changes states while parameters remain unchanged.

Compare paths using explicit time definitions for recognising the problem, initiation, capability formation, transition, stabilisation and verification. Specify which stages overlap and which must be supported by buffers. Related timing distinctions appear in [Stress Testing](https://sl-knowledge.github.io/knowledge-site-en/framework/methods/stress-testing).

**Abstract example:** suppose a path relying entirely on a new skill requires twelve weeks to deliver stable coverage, while existing buffers last eight weeks and no other support is available. This path is not timely under the current plan. It does not prove all alternatives infeasible. Parallel learning, bridging support, revised targets or shorter critical stages may change the conclusion.

External arrangements also involve initiation, eligibility, waiting and failure risks; their response time cannot be assumed zero. Greater urgency may make timely, deliverable support more attractive. Whether a belief premise is accepted along with it still depends on entry requirements, alternatives and existing judgements.

## 6. States, parameters and fast and slow variables

Distinguishing states from parameters depends on the horizon. A short study may temporarily treat skills and need priorities as parameters; long-term learning or major-change research may instead explain their evolution.

Likewise, speed cannot be fixed by an object's name. Support may disappear instantly or remain stable for years. An institutional change can occur quickly yet affect paths for years. Learning may update a current judgement or gradually form enduring capability.

Specify the speed, duration, reversibility and transmission path of each change, and whether it alters feasible options within the target horizon. Education does not always “change parameters”, nor does assistance only “change states”. Timely support can make long-term learning possible.

## 7. Six conditional research propositions

These are proposed mechanism hypotheses, not validated universal laws. Tests must address alternatives and record cases without the expected effect.

| Proposition | Expected relation and conditions | Rival explanations to distinguish |
|---|---|---|
| NG1 Perception correction | If feasible paths exist and underestimation is the main obstacle, credible feedback may increase trials | New resources, rewards or external requirements rather than corrected perception |
| NG2 Recoverable trials | With limited buffers, reducing unrecoverable loss after failure may support exploration better than encouragement alone | Merely increasing short-term returns or transferring losses to others |
| NG3 Load-bearing support | Dependence on one arrangement for important needs, with few alternatives, may raise exit costs and motives to maintain it | Continued participation may reflect endorsement, affection or evidence assessment |
| NG4 Transition deadlines | When critical path-formation time exceeds support endurance, the path becomes less usable; timely support may change this | Undelivered support, parallel stages or already revised targets |
| NG5 Rebuilding capability | After an existing production method fails, transfer and reorganisation capabilities may improve recovery given resources, opportunities and feedback | Prior advantage, unequal opportunities or sampling only successes |
| NG6 Multiple-entry loops | Prior support or practice may foster later identification, without strong initial belief | Recording only already-identified participants and omitting non-entrants and leavers |

Renaming behaviour cannot validate these propositions. NG1 requires independent observations of conditions, prior assessments and subsequent actions, rather than defining the start of exploration itself as “increased perceived capability”.

## 8. Connections with practice loops, freedom and learning

### 8.1 Practice loops: needs are not the only entry

[A Belief-Gated Practice Loop](./belief-gated-practice-loop.md) distinguishes entry through belief, relationships, inheritance and prior practice. The present addition is that needs and support conditions may change entry, persistence and exit costs. It does not reduce all religious participation to an “ultimate gap”. Conversion research also points to relationships and interaction, but particular samples cannot stand for every tradition.[4]

Practical improvement, its attribution and premise truth must still be assessed separately. Mechanism similarities do not make religion, education and other organisations equivalent in value, evidential status or consequences.

### 8.2 Freedom: paths rather than self-sufficiency

[Freedom and Unconstrained Wandering](https://sl-knowledge.github.io/knowledge-site-en/framework/foundations/freedom-and-future-options) distinguishes imagining, reaching and sustaining. [Constraints, Feasibility and Future Options](https://sl-knowledge.github.io/knowledge-site-en/framework/book/constraints-and-future-options) further examines adaptation and recovery. This article applies those distinctions to paths for covering needs.

The question to preserve is whether adjustment, recovery or rebuilding remains possible after conditions change. Answers may come from own capability, reliable cooperation, public conditions and diverse supports. [Stability Does Not Mean a Way Back](https://sl-knowledge.github.io/knowledge-site-en/framework/foundations/lock-in-vs-probability) cautions that good everyday performance does not establish exit or recovery after disturbance.

### 8.3 Learning: developing and testing capability

Learning can organise factual, procedural, structural and metacognitive knowledge. They are interdependent: factual knowledge cannot simply be dismissed as answering only old questions. Transfer still depends on practice, condition recognition and feedback.

For tasks requiring investment before benefits, study whether early verifiable outputs, staged support and recoverable trials sustain learning. Small successes should supply genuine feedback, rather than maintain confidence by hiding failures.

## 9. Related work: what is connected in this condensation?

| Research line | Connection here | Equivalence not asserted |
|---|---|---|
| Needs and well-being [1] | Examine needs by dimension, without assuming a fixed order of satisfaction | This article's classification is not a complete needs theory validated by that study |
| Self-efficacy [2] | Capability beliefs participate in action choice | Belief is not actual capability or the whole perceived path |
| Self-determination theory [3] | Distinguish autonomy, competence and relatedness | Reliance on support is not absence of autonomy |
| Conversion processes [4] | Relationships, turning points and interaction may change entry conditions | Particular processes do not establish a universal origin of faith |
| This site's feasibility and recovery research | Place support, capability and buffers within time-bound paths | Recoverability does not replace all value judgements |

The value of this organisation is that these questions can check one another on a shared card. Whether it yields new explanations or predictions depends on independently observing the distinctions and excluding rival explanations, rather than citation counts or breadth across domains.

## Scope and version notes

This is a mechanism framework concerning need coverage, support and path choice, not a personality classification or clinical assessment. It does not require anyone to abandon supporting relationships. The three kinds of paths have no presumed ranking; effects and justification require separate assessment.
