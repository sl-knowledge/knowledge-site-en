> **Research programme · Discussion organised on 6 November 2026.** This document outlines the shared starting points and purposes of the existing work on representation, constraints and feasibility, evidence updating, joint human–AI cognition and applications across fields.

<a id="research-objects"></a>

## 1. What are we studying?

**We study the states of things in the world: how those states form, persist and change under environmental conditions, relationships and constraints, and which paths of evolution are possible from the current state.**

A second question is equally fundamental: **how do people build representations of the world from limited observations, use them to explain and predict change, and revise their understanding when reality differs from expectation—including redefining the question when necessary?**

The research therefore has two connected objects:

1. **Dynamic processes in the world:** how things exist, interact and move between states; which futures are reachable or sustainable, and which lose feasibility under constraints.
2. **The dynamic process of understanding the world:** how researchers observe, frame questions, choose representations, form hypotheses, make predictions, test them and revise models.

The aim is to find **ways of asking questions and a modelling language** that can be used across fields, reconstructing the mechanisms at work beneath different appearances. This does not imply that a single set of equations explains every field.

<a id="minimal-representation"></a>

## 2. A minimal representation for studying the world

**At the beginning of a study, clarify the question and how it is represented, while allowing subsequent observations to test and revise that representation.**

Six questions offer a starting point that can be simple enough to use while supporting further investigation:

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| **State** | What state is it in now? What is observable, and which important internal states remain unknown? |
| **Environment** | What external conditions surround it? How are they changing? Can it affect its environment in return? |
| **Relations** | What other things is it connected to? How do resources, information, interests and effects pass between them? |
| **Constraints** | For a specified objective and horizon, what actually limits its persistence, action or change? |
| **Capabilities and mechanisms** | What tasks can it perform? Through which internal structures, rules and mechanisms does it produce outcomes? |
| **Dynamics and paths** | How might it change from its current state? Which paths are reachable, sustainable, recoverable or open to reconfiguration? |

Capabilities concern which tasks can be accomplished; mechanisms explain how outcomes arise. The two should be distinguished. These questions are not a form to complete mechanically. Retain variables and relationships that materially affect the question, and check whether new limits emerge when a constraint is relieved.

Three distinctions are particularly important:

- **An observation is not a mechanism.** Revenue growth, a withered flower or a person's choice does not by itself identify the cause.
- **A current state is not future capability.** Two systems with similar results today may differ greatly in internal capabilities, buffers and future feasible paths.
- **Feasibility does not imply inevitability or desirability.** Distinguish what can happen, what is more likely, what can be sustained, and what is worth pursuing under specified values.

<a id="changing-possibilities"></a>

## 3. How do states change, and how do future possibilities change?

A static description is a cross-section in time. The larger question is:

**Current state → interactions within an environment and relationships → relevant constraints and mechanisms → future states and feasible paths.**

This is not a fixed one-way chain. A system can change its environment, competitors may respond, and actions can alter the system's own capabilities and constraints through feedback.

Rather than asking only whether something will improve or deteriorate, examine:

- Why might it follow a particular path?
- Which conditions for continued functioning and resources must hold along that path?
- What becomes the next bottleneck after a constraint is relieved?
- Can the system persist, recover or reorganise when a disturbance arrives?
- Can an action open future options that were previously unreachable?

The focus can therefore be stated as follows: **how are a system's future possibilities jointly shaped—and continually changed—by its current state, history, environment, relationships, capabilities and constraints?**

Possibility should be tied to observable conditions, explicit mechanisms and testable expectations, rather than left as an expression of optimism.

<a id="representation-and-revision"></a>

## 4. Why does research begin with representation?

It is tempting to begin explaining, gathering data or selecting a response immediately. But if the object, objective or variables have been poorly chosen, more detailed analysis may move further from the actual question.

For example, studying a company by asking only whether revenue will grow may overlook a question relevant to investors: **how do sales volumes, prices, costs, capacity utilisation, capital investment and financing obligations jointly change the future value accruing to ordinary shareholders?**

Likewise, a question such as whether a business transformation has succeeded may combine distinct paths. Repairing the credit cycle of an existing business and establishing a sustainable growth mechanism in a new business need separate representations and evidence.

Two forms of revision should be distinguished:

1. **Judgement updating:** the representation remains provisionally applicable, while new evidence changes parameter estimates, probabilities or conclusions.
2. **Representation updating:** the original question has missed relevant objects, variables or mechanisms, requiring the question to be reframed, systems to be separated or the model to be changed.

These can occur together: rebuilding a representation also changes judgements. The roles of corrected records, actual changes and revised mechanisms should be recorded separately.

**When predictions differ from reality, examine whether the question was framed adequately, as well as whether the explanation needs adjustment.** Replacing the story whenever a counterexample appears is not sufficient. A claim that a new representation improves explanation or prediction needs testable differences or explicit criteria for improvement.

<a id="research-purposes"></a>

## 5. Why study the world this way?

The purpose extends beyond labelling things or producing more elaborate explanations. Four aims are connected.

### Understanding: why things are as they are

The aim is to move from surface observations towards the processes that generate them, examining how history, structure, environment and constraints interact. This may reduce errors based on intuition, labels or a single outcome.

It also reminds us that **a model is not the world itself**. Knowing what remains unproved matters alongside knowing what has happened.

### Prediction: identifying possible future paths

Understanding mechanisms supports conditional judgements about change. The task includes paths, timing, risks and sustainability, rather than only forecasting an outcome.

For complex systems, a reasonable result is often a set of path judgements with explicit conditions and differing evidential support, open to continued revision, rather than one certain answer.

### Choice and action: finding conditions that can change the future

Understanding constraints and mechanisms may help identify potentially effective changes: reallocating resources, improving conditions, altering rules, building capabilities or retaining essential buffers.

**Research may help create feasible options that did not previously exist, as well as compare existing ones.**

A model cannot decide which ends are most worth pursuing. Shareholder value, system safety, human welfare and personal goals require their own explicit evaluation. The ability to change something does not establish that it should be changed; costs and the interests of others also matter.

### Understanding limits: when to act and when to accept

Every system is constrained by conditions. Human choices and behaviour are also affected by experience, capabilities, cognition, desires, resources and environment.

Understanding these conditions does not entail approving every action or removing responsibility. It may, however, support understanding and compassion rather than judgement based on a simple label.

Research can therefore have two practical directions: **act on changeable conditions with better grounds, and understand the limits of what cannot presently be changed, reducing unproductive attachment.**

<a id="research-cycle"></a>

## 6. A cycle of understanding and action

```text
Clarify the object and objective
        ↓
Construct a representation
(states, environment, relations, constraints, mechanisms)
        ↓
Reconstruct processes and dynamic feedback
        ↓
Explore feasible paths and make testable predictions
        ↓
Evaluate ends; choose action or defer it
        ↓
Observe reality and update judgements from evidence
        ↓
Revise hypotheses or rebuild the representation when needed
        ↺
```

The cycle operates at two levels: **the external world changes, and our understanding of it changes.** Researchers have no all-knowing viewpoint outside the system. Their observations, models, actions and feedback are themselves subject to conditions.

<a id="cross-domain-transfer"></a>

## 7. What can transfer between fields?

The specific regularities differ across fields, but the questions may share a structure:

| Field | Questions about states and change |
|---|---|
| **Companies and investment** | How do business capabilities and cash cycles change? How might future value per share evolve? What do market prices and expectations already reflect? |
| **Macroeconomics** | How do production, payments, demand, technology and institutions interact to sustain, stall or reorganise an economy? |
| **Control and safety** | Under resource, performance, environmental and safety constraints, how can states follow feasible paths while retaining recovery options? |
| **Life** | How does a living system maintain itself, respond to environmental change, grow or decline? |
| **Learning and AI** | How does a cognitive system generate predictions from a representation, detect mismatch through feedback, and develop better understanding and questioning? |
| **People and society** | How do behaviour and social relationships form under history, cognition, interests and constraints? Which conditions make change possible? |

What transfers is **the ability to frame questions, investigate mechanisms, identify constraints, test predictions and revise representations**, rather than ready-made answers from another field.

<a id="testable-method"></a>

## 8. Why make the method an explicit process?

Years of practice may be needed to develop the ability to identify relevant variables, notice inferential jumps, seek disconfirmation and recognise when a conclusion is not justified.

The aim here is to **make some of these disciplines explicit and procedural**, and use AI's retrieval, organisation, calculation and comparison capabilities to help people without extensive research experience make better judgements. It is not a claim to outperform the best experts.

The question to test is whether this process, compared with asking AI directly for an answer, helps identify inadequate representations earlier, reduce unsupported transfers of evidence, find more discriminating counterevidence and revise judgements more accurately as information arrives.

This is a research objective requiring controlled comparisons and continued follow-up, not an established effect.

<a id="summary"></a>

## 9. Overall statement

**What do we study?**

> Things in the world are not isolated, static objects. They exist in environments and relationships as systems whose states change. We seek to understand their current states, mechanisms, constraints and future feasible paths, while studying how people construct, test and revise their understanding of those processes.

**Why do we study them?**

> To understand more accurately why the world is as it is, identify what may happen, discover which conditions can be changed and which new options can be created, and recognise the limitations of things and people: acting with better grounds where action is possible, and finding understanding and acceptance where change is beyond reach.

In a short sequence:

**Understand the world → recognise possibilities → choose and act → revise through feedback → understand limits.**

This is a cycle that continually revises its own representation, rather than a line that ends after one pass.
