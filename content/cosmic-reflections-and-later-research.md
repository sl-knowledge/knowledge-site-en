Why does the world form structures? Why do life, cognition and civilisation appear? What do past events leave for what comes next?

A philosophical essay about the universe in 2025 brought these questions together. Later research gradually turned to more specific objects: how life continually generates itself, how history changes future possibilities, which differences a model should retain, and how knowledge from different fields can be connected.

One thread connecting the two is the early dynamic idea of **repeated information diffusion and condensation**. Possibilities unfold into diverse experiences and knowledge. Scattered information is distilled into structures that can continue to do useful work. Those structures enter new environments, producing further exploration and knowledge, followed by another round of condensation.

From this perspective, the present work of organising scattered knowledge into theory and taking it into different fields can itself be understood as a round of this cycle. The early philosophical reflections both raised research questions and offered a way of understanding what this research is doing.

This essay traces several threads through the surviving texts. Correspondences between texts can show how questions continue, but cannot fully reconstruct how each idea arose or establish that every later result was derived from the early essay.

## 1. The initial conjecture: how quickly complex structures form

The early essay's central conjecture was:

> **The universe moves towards the highest complexity at the fastest possible rate.**

The original text formulated its central principle as selecting, among all possible paths, the path that maximises the growth rate of internal structural complexity, \(dC/dt\).

The conjecture concerned both the formation of complex structures and its rate. Why do some processes merely repeat, while others keep producing new organisation, relationships and capabilities? Why do life, cognition and technology deserve particular attention? These questions were already present in the initial reflections.

The original text did not complete a unified measure of complexity or specify a definite upper bound on its final level. “Moving towards the highest complexity” expresses the philosophical conjecture; maximising the growth rate is the principle the text sought to make more explicit. A rigorous connection between the two still needs an argument.

Later research gradually focused on specific processes: how a structure forms, persists and leaves an influence, and under what conditions it fails. Parts of the initial questions, difficult to answer directly, became separately investigable.

## 2. From describing an object to understanding how it is generated

An important question in the original essay was whether DNA stores a complete blueprint of an organism or a “seed of a generative algorithm” that unfolds in an environment.

This changes the way an object is studied. An adult organism already contains many complex structures. Knowing their inventory does not explain how they form. Fertilised eggs, seeds and development direct attention towards how limited initial conditions, together with ongoing physical, chemical and environmental interactions, generate complex organisation.

Later research on life continued this thread, examining generative rules, the renewal of relationships and whether organisation can recover after disturbance. Attention gradually moved towards processes that can repeatedly realise certain organisational relationships.

A continuing question can be drawn from this:

> **What process can generate this structure? What conditions allow that process to continue?**

It can also be taken into other fields. Studying an organisation, a capability or a body of knowledge invites questions about how it is established, maintained and realised again. Each mechanism needs its own investigation, while generation and maintenance provide a shared direction for inquiry.

## 3. From preserving history to studying how history changes the future

The early essay distinguished structures formed through current activity from the history those activities leave behind, and conjectured that history could influence the conditions of a subsequent round of evolution.

Its account of cosmic cycles remains philosophical speculation. The question it raises can nevertheless be studied in particular systems:

> **What does past activity leave behind? How do those persistent structures change what can happen later?**

History can persist in many forms: organisational procedures, substances accumulated in an environment, parameters changed through training, or relationships established by repeated activity. Studying their effects requires specifying how they enter subsequent processes.

The later core account of life attempts to turn this into a comparative design: hold the current system state constant, vary only the environmental structures left by past activity, and compare recovery after disturbance.

“History affects the future” thereby becomes a more specific research question: Which variable persists? Through what mechanism does it act? How long does its effect last, and how can it be tested?

This thread also changed. The early essay imagined accumulated history driving more efficient evolution; later models must accommodate beneficial and harmful historical effects. Persistent structures may assist recovery, or create burdens and lock-in. **The direction of a historical effect depends on mechanisms and conditions.**

## 4. Information diffusion and condensation: the research itself participates in the cycle

The original essay used “information growth” and “information condensation” for two alternating processes: unfolding many possibilities, and distilling more stable, effective structures from them. Viewed as “diffusion–condensation–renewed diffusion,” the interest is in a recurrent generative dynamic: unfolding provides new material, condensation forms new structures, and those structures enable another round of unfolding.

This is a central thread for understanding the present research.

Theories, discoveries, methods and experience accumulated across fields constitute knowledge that has already unfolded. Many researchers produced it through different questions, and it remains distributed across their literatures, concepts and practices. Bringing that knowledge together, identifying shared relationships, organising concepts and writing theory constitute a round of condensation.

The resulting theory can then enter different fields. New objects and conditions bring applications and questions, and may expose differences not previously recognised. This is another round of diffusion: it includes both the spread of knowledge and the generation of new knowledge using existing structures.

The resulting knowledge and feedback provide material for comparison, testing and refinement, prompting further condensation. The process can be written as:

> **Scattered knowledge and experience → extracting relationships and forming theory → cross-domain applications and exploration → new questions, knowledge and feedback → renewed condensation and revision → another round of unfolding**

Writing theory is therefore not the endpoint. Theory is a reusable structure left by a round of condensation. Its value also depends on whether it connects previously scattered knowledge, supports new exploration and can change in response to feedback from applications.

From the perspective of the early philosophical reflections, drawing out patterns, writing theory, making it available to people and AI, and generating new knowledge through applications are a concrete instance of repeated diffusion and condensation. The research process itself becomes an object that this way of thinking seeks to understand.

Later work on models and abstraction asks more specifically what condensation should retain:

> **Which relationships support new understanding and applications? Which differences, if omitted, make a judgement fail?**

Condensation involves selection, and selection can go wrong. What needs to be retained may vary with the goal and time horizon. New applications may support existing theory, or require splitting concepts previously combined and restoring neglected conditions. The effectiveness of a round of condensation must be tested through subsequent unfolding.

The complexity of real structures also needs to be distinguished from the length of a representation. Condensation can make an account more concise while retaining relationships that support more applications; diffusion can use that account to generate diverse new knowledge. The capacity to generate knowledge and the amount of text are different matters.

## 5. From operating within rules to examining rules and frameworks

The early essay distinguished a system's internal operation from changes to its operating rules. It also asked whether recurring difficulties might arise from the limits of the current conceptual framework.

The original text drew inspiration from mathematics and included conjectures about cosmic evolution. A question about research practice can be carried forward:

> **Does the current difficulty call for better action, or for another look at the goal, representation or framework?**

When a model repeatedly fails to explain observations, for example, its parameters can be adjusted. Alternatively, one can examine missing variables, the chosen boundary, or changes in conditions previously assumed constant. These revisions occur at different levels and need to be distinguished.

Later book drafts gradually incorporated this examination into research: construct a representation, propose explanations, seek evidence that distinguishes them, and revise according to where failure occurs.

This helps explain the continuing attention to how a question should be posed, alongside how to answer it. The early interest in rules and a meta-level found more concrete uses in later work.

## 6. Seeking connections across fields, and the requirements for establishing them

Life, learning, science and AI were compared from the beginning. Seeking shared structures was a deliberate research direction.

Later work retained that direction while raising the requirements for establishing a connection. Similar words or diagrams do not establish a shared mechanism. Further checks are needed: Do the relationships correspond? Can the causal mechanism operate? Does transfer produce new predictions? Does evidence in the target field support it?

A connection across fields can begin with a question:

> **Does this situation contain a relationship already studied in another field?**

Domain knowledge must then supply the variables, mechanisms, conditions and evidence. Later drafts explicitly distinguish analogy from evidence: analogy helps generate candidate explanations; their validity must be tested in the target field.

This also gradually shaped the site's organisation. Concepts, relationships and conditions are gathered in the theory section; application essays explain how they map onto specific problems. Raising the level of abstraction makes scattered knowledge easier to compare and easier for people and AI to retrieve, connect and use. The theory section carries the results of condensation, while the applications provide room for new unfolding. Questions and feedback from applications can return to the theory section and prompt another round of revision.

## 7. From wanting tests to arranging concrete tests

The early essay already called for defining complexity, building computable models and finding distinctive predictions. Testability was part of the research ambition from the outset.

Later changes gradually clarified how to meet these requirements: what object to study, which representation to adopt, which conditions to compare, what outcomes to observe, and which results would require revision.

The core account of life attempts to connect relational structure, a bridge model and experiment. Work on models asks about omissions and task mismatch. Work on simplification and recoverability defines cost and recovery scope separately. These projects address different objects, while turning broad questions into claims that can be assessed individually.

Human–AI collaboration also runs through this history. The early essay records ChatGPT's participation in initial discussions and collaboration mainly with Gemini in developing the framework and refining the text. Later collaboration developed more explicit divisions of work and methods of revision. Research began examining the reliability of its own process alongside external objects.

## 8. The early essay's place in the research history

Looking back, the early essay proposed a strong conjecture about the universe as a whole and left a set of continuing questions:

- How are complex structures generated and repeatedly regenerated?
- What does past activity leave behind, and how does it change future possibilities?
- How can experience be distilled into usable, transferable knowledge?
- How can condensed theory unfold into new knowledge, and how can that knowledge be distilled again?
- When should action be adjusted, and when should the framework be examined?
- Which relationships can be mapped across fields, and how should the mapping be tested?

Later research made these questions more specific and gradually found ways of discussing them across fields. The initial questions were already profound; the difficulty lay in answering them directly. Subsequent work gradually identified objects, conditions and ways of testing some of their parts.

Each claim still needs its own assessment. Evidence for generation, memory or recovery mechanisms in particular systems does not automatically establish that the universe as a whole maximises complexity growth. The early conjectures about cosmic cycles, conservation of information and the inevitability of life also require independent arguments.

The relationship between the early essay and later research also concerns how the research understands itself. The present work condenses knowledge that has already diffused into theory, then unfolds it through applications, producing knowledge and questions that provide material for another round of condensation.

**Understanding the current research through the earlier philosophy means placing the work within a continuing process of diffusion, condensation and renewed diffusion.** This offers a perspective on research practice. Whether the universe as a whole follows the same dynamic remains a separate research claim.
