> A research note retaining exploratory and conversational elements; views and references remain revisable.

> **Build foundations, then deepen as needed.** Reconstruct mechanisms from minimal real situations, identify new structures at larger scales, compare established knowledge and test with discriminating evidence.
>
> **The goal is not one story explaining every economic phenomenon, but a way of understanding economics that keeps learning, diagnosing and correcting.**

**Version note (15 October 2026):** retains the economic atom → Viability/Generation → differences/frictions → eight steps → real-world testing sequence. Corrects three issues: economic blockage is not only structure/confidence—real resources and ability to fulfil obligations need separate treatment; more IOUs do not unconditionally cause inflation; known economic relationships do not imply accurate market pricing of firm-level consequences. Adds competing explanations, scale transitions and causal-evidence discipline.

---

## 1. Why learn this way?

Conventional courses organise concepts, models and specialisms rigorously and cumulatively, but learners may remember conclusions without their common mechanisms.

A **complementary route** is:

1. Start with actual questions, not terminology.
2. Reconstruct actors, targets, capabilities, resources, information and rules in a minimal situation.
3. Include real behaviour: trustworthiness, suspicion, delay, deception, altruism, learning and change.
4. Check new structures/variables when moving from micro to macro.
5. Learn what established theories tested before declaring what remains unknown.
6. Seek **competing explanations and discriminating evidence** for major conclusions.

This organises knowledge rather than replaces economics. **Understanding includes knowing when one's mechanism explanation fails.**

## 2. Foundations: two real people

### 2.1 The economic atom: cooking and laundry

A is hungry; B can cook. B needs laundry; A can wash. They may cooperate, exchanging these tasks.

Need alone does not create cooperation:

| Question | Meaning in the atom |
|---|---|
| Target | Food for A, clean clothes for B |
| Information | Knowledge of goals, ability and intentions |
| Incentive | Why each contributes time/labour |
| Capability/resources | Ingredients, water, tools, time, skill |
| Execution/feedback | Fulfilment, quality and adjustment after breach |

With real behaviour, results differ: one acts first and is cheated; both demand the other go first; past deception breeds suspicion; illness prevents fulfilment; help is voluntary; standards of cleanliness differ.

**The experiment's insight:** economic relationships are not collisions of static demands, but ongoing interactions among agents with different goals, dispositions, memories and constraints.

### 2.2 Rules emerge and then change behaviour

Suppose B cooks while A repeatedly delays laundry:

- B requires laundry first: **sequencing rules**.
- A fears no meal afterwards: simultaneous work or staged acceptance.
- Quality disputes: standards.
- Illness: delay, substitute performance or exemptions.
- Repeated cheating: reputation, guarantees or third-party adjudication.

**Behaviour → coordination problem → rules → changed behaviour → results/feedback → evolving rules.**

Rules may make self-interested agents comply or reward opportunism and punish honesty. More punishment is not automatically better. **Institutions need correction, dispute resolution and exit, rather than assuming permanent rationality, kindness or honesty.**

### 2.3 From acquaintances to strangers: mechanisms change with scale

Repeated meetings support reciprocity/reputation; never meeting again weakens future refusal as a sanction.

Large-scale stranger cooperation cannot merely copy familiar-person transactions. New structures may include:

- Widely accepted money/payment systems overcoming double coincidence of wants;
- Portable reputation, contracts, law and independent enforcement;
- Firms, platforms and supply chains organising dispersed tasks continuously;
- Banking, insurance and public finance connecting times, risks and obligations.

**Scale is not just quantity: networks, power, rules and actors may change.** Explain/test these mechanisms separately; macroeconomics cannot all be deduced directly from a two-person story.

## 3. Two axes: viability and generation

| Axis | Fundamental question | Observe |
|---|---|---|
| **Viability** | Why does cooperation break, and can operations continue? | Cash/physical buffers, connections, credit fulfilment, alternatives, adjustment speed |
| **Generation** | How do new capabilities, needs and cooperation form? | Knowledge, technology, organisational innovation, new demand, productivity, rules |

**Sustainability is not growth.** Stable systems can lack progress; rapid expansion can be unsustainable through depleted resources/unfulfilled promises.

New capabilities/rules may relieve old constraints, while growth creates new bottlenecks and risks.

## 4. Differences, frictions and constraints

> **Differences create potential gains; information reveals opportunities; incentives motivate action; resources make it feasible; credible fulfilment sustains cooperation.**

Behavioural complexity includes goals, preferences, honesty, emotion, time preference, risk and learning. Yet **not every economic problem is disagreement**: even altruistic, unanimous agents face scarce land, energy, time, skills and information.

The table provides diagnostic entries, not one-to-one institutional laws:

| Difference/friction | Blocked link | Coordination and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Different wants/capabilities | No direct match | Specialisation, exchange, money, markets; feasible reciprocity required |
| Different information | Unknown needs/providers/quality | Prices, ratings, advertising, intermediaries, platforms; prices omit some real needs |
| Uncertain intentions/behaviour | Deception, concealment, delay, free riding | Contracts, reputation, guarantees, arbitration/law; enforcement costs/capability |
| Different time/risk tolerance | Timing/risk-sharing mismatch | Credit, insurance, saving, collateral; real losses remain |
| Different emotion/expectation/learning | Herding, panic, coordination failure | Disclosure, risk management, regulation, lender of last resort; no assured judgement |
| Different resources/geography/technology | Willing but unable | Investment, transport, training, technology/reallocation; actual time/cost |

**Information shortage, unwillingness and lack of fulfilment resources are distinct.** Better information cannot create food; harsher penalties cannot instantly give an insolvent debtor cash.

## 5. Five conditions for continuing circulation

1. **Needs:** whose actual goals does activity serve? Begin with needs, then ask whether transactions can form.
2. **Purchasing power/income:** which resources, income or supported rights enable others to act?
3. **Allocation:** are labour, capital, natural resources and time worth the results?
4. **Payment/credit:** can obligations be fulfilled, without valuable production collapsing through funding?
5. **Buffers/adjustment:** can errors be corrected before liquidity/resources/other buffers run out?

Fast money circulation is not sufficient health. Real resources, fulfilled goals and sustainable value/cost alignment also matter.

### Three blockages, not two

| Type | Atomic problem | Manifestation | Candidate response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Connection mismatch** | Needs do not match capabilities, or cannot connect | Skills mismatch, contraction, search cost | Transition, skill transfer, new demand/technology, matching |
| **Coordination/trust failure** | Gains exist but each fears acting first | Hoarding, no trade credit, delayed investment, runs | Credible commitments, enforcement, risk sharing, stabilisation where needed |
| **Insufficient fulfilment/resources** | Willing but lacking cash/physical means | Broken cash flow, insolvency, food/energy shortage | Distinguish liquidity, solvency and supply shortages; finance, restructure or expand supply accordingly |

These interact: demand shift → lower revenue → weaker payment → default → defensive responses → otherwise feasible transactions stop.

**Diagnose before choosing policy.** Lending to permanently unproductive projects may prolong risk; demanding honesty does not supply missing funds.

### Disturbance, buffers and state transitions

Economies are not simply normal/collapsed. Corrections faster than buffer depletion may absorb local problems; slower correction may create reinforcing contraction.

> What binds, how long can it last, what alternatives exist, and how are actors/rules adjusting?

## 6. Eight steps from story to testable model

| Step | Operation | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Real question** | Specify phenomenon/bounds | Explain whom, when, which outcome? |
| **2. Minimal situation** | Minimum agents/resources covering behaviour | Can two people/tasks suffice? What else is essential? |
| **3. Real behaviour** | Strategy, emotion, memory, error, altruism, self-interest, adaptation | Does changing behaviour change outcome? |
| **4. Constraints/alternatives** | Failure links and at least one competing explanation | Matching, trust, payment/resources or something else? What else predicts the same pattern? |
| **5. Scale/time** | Familiar to stranger, short to long, transaction to network | Which mechanisms survive, and which actors/rules/feedback arise? |
| **6. Shared framework** | Viability/Generation and five conditions | Actual Binding Constraint and state-boundary transition? |
| **7. Established research** | Theory, evidence, counterexamples/bounds | Rediscovery or unverified hypothesis? |
| **8. Evidence/refutation** | Signals, dates, outcomes | What distinguishes explanations or changes judgement? |

**Principle:** these are checks, not compulsory ritual. Adopt mature answers directly; reconstruct more deeply for transfers, boundaries or major decisions.

## 7. Inflation: applying and testing the atom's limits

**1. Question:** why do many goods/services rise in price over a period?

**2. Minimal situation:** cooking/laundry with a shared payment token. Who issues it, why accepted, redeemable how, used in how many transactions? A private IOU is not automatically national currency.

**3. Behaviour:** precautionary money holding, borrowing for purchases, expectations changing balances, quotes and credit.

**4. Competing mechanisms:**

- **Nominal spending expands without matching supply:** overall prices may rise.
- **Materials, energy or capacity suffer:** costs/prices rise alongside lower output.
- **Money and demand to hold money rise together, without spending growth:** prices need not rise.

They predict different output, jobs, wages, credit and price trajectories.

**5. Scale transition:** multiple agents/goods, banks and currency institutions require credit creation, budgets, supply, wage/price setting and trade. More private IOUs cannot simply be scaled into macro conclusions.

**6. Framework:** purchasing power, supply, money/credit, expectations and buffers jointly determine dynamics.

**7. Prior work:** Fisher supplies an important quantity relationship; Keynesian, monetary, supply-shock and expectations research specify conditions. Velocity, output and institutions cannot be omitted when inferring inflation.

**8. Tests:** aggregate nominal spending, credit/money, output gap, energy/import prices, wages, price diffusion and household/business expectations; seek demand-versus-supply discrimination.

> **The lesson:** first principles do not infinitely enlarge an intuitive story. Establish its conditions step by step. Coherence shows possibility, not actual causation.

## 8. Where new knowledge connects

| Topic | First question | Additional structures/checks |
|---|---|---|
| Specialisation/exchange | Why work for one another? | Goals, capabilities, matching, reciprocity, comparative advantage |
| Money/inflation | Shared payment and changing purchasing power? | Issuance, banks, money demand, supply/demand, spending, expectations |
| Monetary policy | Financial conditions to behaviour? | Bank balance sheets, credit demand, interest, expectations |
| Fiscal policy | Whose income/resources/incentives change? | Tax, debt, budgets, multipliers, project value |
| Labour/unemployment | Unneeded capability or temporary mismatch? | Wages, search, skills, mobility, aggregate demand |
| Financial crisis | Which obligations fail and how spread? | Leverage, maturity mismatch, collateral, networks, lender of last resort |
| Firms | Why internal coordination over market transactions? | Authority, agency, monitoring, knowledge, transaction costs |
| Trade/exchange rates | Which constraints do borders change? | Comparative advantage, trade cost, capital flows, currency regimes |
| Technology/AI/growth | Which constraints ease, who gains income? | Productivity, displaced/new tasks, capital, distribution |
| Industrial transition | Can new links form before buffers end? | Specific assets, skill conversion, demand scale, competition |
| Institutions/development | Which rules sustain valuable cooperation? | Rights, enforcement, political/organisational capacity, adaptation |

**Macro is not micro copied many times.** Networks, states, banks, externalities, public goods and power require additional mechanisms.

## 10. Traps and AI collaboration

### Five traps

1. **General principle replaces mechanism:** behavioural complexity is broad, but each outcome needs specific constraints.
2. **Coherence becomes fact:** ask evidence for every arrow; plausible long chains are not enough.
3. **Private IOU becomes modern money:** add state, banking, acceptance and nominal spending.
4. **Scale becomes copying:** stranger cooperation, law, banks, organisations and public decisions create new mechanisms.
5. **Known becomes priced:** knowing collection matters does not mean markets know which company, when, how much or fair price.

### Conditions for AI collaboration

Use AI for collection/checking, reconstruction, transfer, alternatives and refutation, not obligatory full procedures.

Ask:

- What is my target and the framework's boundary?
- Can a minimal atom reconstruct this? Where must structure be added?
- Strongest alternative and discriminating evidence?
- What precisely was tested previously, and what was not?
- Which state variable first reveals mechanism error?

**AI must identify over-extrapolation, not continually fit reality to our narrative.**

## 11. Locate in existing research before claiming originality

These are entry points for local mechanisms, not automatic proof of the whole model.

| Topic | Research | Connection/limit |
|---|---|---|
| Specialisation/exchange | Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations* (1776) | Division/scope of exchange; later transaction/institution research needed |
| Dispersed knowledge/prices | Hayek, "The Use of Knowledge in Society" (1945) | Coordination; prices are not all needs/social value |
| Firms/coordination | Coase, "The Nature of the Firm" (1937) | Why internal coordination |
| Repeated cooperation | Axelrod, *The Evolution of Cooperation* (1984) | Conditional strategies, not universal selfish cooperation |
| Community governance | Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990) | Rules, monitoring, graduated sanctions, cheap dispute resolution |
| Institutions/uncertainty | North, *Institutions, Institutional Change and Economic Performance* (1990) | Formal/informal incentives and transaction costs |
| Incentives/preferences | Bowles, *The Moral Economy* (2016) | Incentives may reinforce or crowd out cooperation |
| Money/inflation | Fisher, *The Purchasing Power of Money* (1911) | Quantity relationship with conditions, not inevitable inflation from issuance |
| Demand/coordination | Keynes, *The General Theory* (1936); Krugman, "Baby-Sitting the Economy" (1998) | Insufficient spending/coordination mechanisms and thought experiments |
| Debt/fragility | Minsky, *Stabilizing an Unstable Economy* (1986) | Financing structure and feedback |
| Critical changes | Leijonhufvud, "Effective Demand Failures" (1973); Scheffer et al. on critical transitions | Coordination/boundaries; retain domain differences |
| Tasks/technology/jobs | Acemoglu and Autor's research | Task allocation, wages/jobs, beyond "AI replaces work" |

**Reading:** find the closest short study for the present question, then revisit what the atom explains/omits.

## 12. From understanding to investment: mechanisms meet pricing

Consider government arrears and builders:

1. **Diagnose blockage:** orders/matching, liquidity, insolvency or deliberate withholding by powerful parties?
2. **Rule change:** one-off old-debt repayment differs from permanent new-project terms/funding rules.
3. **States:** actual receipts, receivables/contract assets, subcontract payments, operating cash, funding cost and provisions.
4. **Alternatives:** collection, less construction, asset sales, downstream payment delay or new borrowing?
5. **New space:** after repair, how much profitable/reliably paid demand remains, and how fast does competition respond?
6. **Expectations:** opportunity requires differences in direction, magnitude, speed or persistence from price-implied expectations, plus risk/cost assessment.

> **Established relationships can support new investment judgements. Better facts, earlier state-change recognition and more accurate effects can matter without inventing new economics.**

## Appendix: one sentence

> **An economy is a dynamic system in which behaviourally complex actors continually form and adjust cooperation through information, incentives, commitments and rules under real resource constraints, serving goals, preserving capabilities and generating new ones. Start with minimal real cooperation, then check scale transitions, constraints and feedback, letting established theory and evidence jointly determine conclusions.**
